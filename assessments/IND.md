## Bottom line up front
India's political risk is Elevated and broadly stable over the next 12 months. Growth is fast (7.8% in April to June) and no general election is due before 2029, but Narendra Modi faces what analysts call his most serious political crisis since 2014: near-daily protests since late September against a revision of voter rolls that has removed more than 130 million names. Inflation reached 5.54% in September, and the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) raised rates on 7 October. A mass-casualty attack in Jammu and Kashmir followed by Indian strikes on Pakistan, or US use of a paused 100% tariff on buyers of Russian oil, would move my outlook to Deteriorating.

## Key judgments
1. Narendra Modi is **very likely** (~90%) to still be prime minister on 1 November 2027. No general election is due until 2029, the BJP won West Bengal in May, and the protests are led by the opposition and student groups. The residual risk is a health shock or a split in the governing alliance.
2. Gyanesh Kumar is **likely** (~75%) to still be Chief Election Commissioner on 1 November 2027. The BJP denies the Commission is compromised and the defence minister calls the protests a Congress bid for power. The threats to him are an opposition petition to the Supreme Court demanding his resignation and the doubts of his two fellow commissioners.
3. Monthly CPI inflation is **likely** (~70%) to exceed 6.0% at least once before November 2027. Food inflation was 7.74% in September, and the RBI projects 6.0% for October to December, citing a deficient south-west monsoon, strong El Niño conditions and volatile crude prices. Brent closed at $117.64 on 30 October.
4. India and the US are **unlikely** (~35%) to sign the first tranche of a bilateral trade agreement before November 2027. The February interim framework cut the US tariff on most Indian goods to 18%, but talks slowed after the US Supreme Court struck down Trump's sweeping tariffs that month, and in October Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman said they had reached a "plateau". The paused 100% tariff law gives Washington leverage after the midterms.
5. India and Pakistan are **very unlikely** (~15%) to strike each other's territory with missiles, drones or aircraft before November 2027. A warship collision in the Arabian Sea on 15 September and the killing of two Pakistanis by Indian border guards in Punjab on 2 October both ended with diplomats summoned. I hold this with low confidence: a mass-casualty attack in Jammu and Kashmir that India blames on Pakistan, as in April 2025, could change it within days.

## Drivers
### Political
In state elections in May the BJP won West Bengal, ending 15 years of Trinamool Congress rule, and the Congress-led front took Kerala from the Left, which now governs no state. Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Uttarakhand, Goa and Manipur are expected to vote in February and March 2027, and a presidential election is due in July 2027.

The main dispute is over the voter rolls. The Election Commission's revision, begun in Bihar in June 2025, has removed more than 130 million names, about 9.1 million of them in West Bengal before the April vote. The Commission says it struck off duplicate, dead and fraudulent entries, and that wrongly removed voters can register again; critics say Muslims and other minorities were hit disproportionately. Protests began after Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar's two fellow commissioners raised concerns, and on 30 September the opposition INDIA bloc demanded the old rolls back and Kumar's removal.

The Cockroach Janta Party, a youth movement founded in May over exam paper leaks and poor job prospects, forced out Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan in July and has joined the campaign against Kumar. Police detained Rahul Gandhi on 7 October, deployed more than 30,000 officers in New Delhi against a march to the Supreme Court on 12 October and detained the movement's founder, Abhijeet Dipke, on 16 October. Thousands joined a Congress-led rally in the capital on 1 November.

### Security
The May 2025 fighting followed an attack on tourists in Indian-administered Kashmir in April that killed 26 people; India blamed militants backed by Pakistan, which Pakistan denies. Four days of strikes and artillery fire killed more than 70 people on both sides before a ceasefire on 10 May. India has kept the Indus Waters Treaty suspended since April 2025 and rejected an August 2026 ruling by the Permanent Court of Arbitration that the treaty remains binding. After the September collision, a retired Pakistani vice admiral noted that the two navies have no incidents-at-sea agreement or hotline.

In Jammu and Kashmir, Omar Abdullah heads an elected government, but de facto authority in the federally governed territory rests with an administrator appointed by New Delhi.

Manipur has had an elected government under Yumnam Khemchand Singh since early February 2026, with a Kuki-Zo deputy chief minister, but tension between Kuki-Zo and Naga communities now adds to the violence. Houses were torched in Ukhrul in February, and in June the bodies of six Naga men allegedly abducted in Kangpokpi district in May were found a day before an attack on Kultuh village in Kamjong killed two men. On 19 October three Meitei villagers collecting firewood near the Imphal East and Kangpokpi boundary were shot dead in an attack blamed on suspected tribal militants, and the Congress says thousands protested in five districts on 23 October.

On the border with China, the Indo-Tibetan Border Police chief said in late October that face-offs with Chinese troops in Arunachal Pradesh, reportedly in Upper Subansiri, were continuing, a month after Xi Jinping visited New Delhi for the BRICS summit.

### Economic
Real GDP rose 7.8% in April to June, against 6.9% a year earlier, and the RBI expects 7.1% for 2026-27. Prices are the weak point: CPI inflation rose from 4.83% in August to 5.54% in September. On 7 October the RBI raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% and moved to a stance of calibrated tightening, which it says rules out cuts in the near term. India imports about 90% of its oil, so the re-escalation of the West Asia conflict feeds straight into prices, and a general government deficit of 7.5% of GDP (the model's IMF figure for 2026) leaves little room to cushion fuel costs.

US trade policy is the other external risk. Tariffs on Indian goods reached 50% in August 2025, half of it a penalty for buying Russian oil. The February 2026 framework cut the rate to 18% in return for India ending Russian oil purchases and buying $500 billion of US goods over five years, but nothing has been signed. A 10% US duty announced on 24 July over forced labour covers about 55% of India's exports to the US. Trump has since signed a law allowing tariffs of up to 100% on buyers of Russian oil, then paused it before the midterms.

### Social
Government data put youth unemployment at 15.2% in March 2026, and the State of Working India 2026 report finds nearly 40% of graduates aged 15 to 25 out of work. The voter-roll dispute has a communal edge: in October 2025 the West Bengal BJP reportedly planned about 700 camps in border districts to help Hindu refugees apply for citizenship under the Citizenship Amendment Act. In Manipur, the Congress says about 300 people have been killed since May 2023 and 24,000 remain displaced.

## Indicators to watch
- The October CPI print in mid-November and the MPC meeting on 2 to 4 December.
- Use of the 100% tariff after the midterms, or a separate US tariff on India over excess capacity in industries such as textiles.
- Supreme Court action on the opposition petition against Kumar, and any resignation by the other two election commissioners.
- A militant attack in Jammu and Kashmir that kills more than 10 people, and whether India blames Pakistan.
- A naval or border incident with Pakistan involving gunfire or deaths.
- The Manipur election schedule, and killings along the Imphal East and Kangpokpi boundary.

The economic pillar (51), the highest of the four, uses IMF figures for 2026 of 7.0% growth and 5.0% inflation; growth in April to June was 7.8%, but September inflation was 5.54% and the RBI expects 6.0% for October to December. The social pillar (34) is the lowest, and its displacement indicator (0.02% of the population in 2025) counts only people who have left the country, so it misses those displaced inside Manipur. The app's watchlist holds no India events. I expect the score to edge up when 2025 conflict data arrive, without leaving the Elevated band.

## Implications
Exporters to the US face the 10% duty on goods outside exempt sectors such as generic drugs and smartphones, and a sharp rise if the 100% law is used; Reuters reported in July that textile and apparel exporters were likely to lose ground to Asian rivals. Borrowers face higher rates, and another rise is possible at the December meeting. Firms in central Delhi should plan for barricades and closures on protest days, and operators in Manipur's hill districts for prohibitory orders and internet suspensions like those imposed in Ukhrul in February. Governments with ties to both India and Pakistan should press for a navy hotline, since a collision with casualties would be harder to contain than September's.

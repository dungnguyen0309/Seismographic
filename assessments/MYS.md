## Bottom line up front
Malaysia's political risk is Moderate and stable over the next 12 months. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's government is secure: on 29 October seven opposition MPs, among them former prime minister Muhyiddin Yassin, signed a confidence and supply agreement (CSA) to back it until the end of the term. The main driver is the run-up to the 16th general election (GE16), due by February 2028, as Umno, a partner in Anwar's government, moves towards PAS. I would move the outlook to Deteriorating if Umno left the cabinet early or the US raised its tariff on Malaysian goods.

## Key judgments
1. Anwar is **very likely** (~85%) to still be prime minister on 1 November 2027. His coalition holds 143 Dewan Rakyat seats before counting the CSA signatories, and analysts see no reason for Gabungan Parti Sarawak (GPS) to leave. The likeliest way he loses office is an early election that Pakatan Harapan (PH) loses.
2. GE16 polling is **unlikely** (~25%) to take place before 1 November 2027. Parliament dissolves automatically on 19 December 2027, and on 1 November DAP secretary-general Loke Siew Fook told a party convention that DAP faced a lot of work over the next 12 months. Umno president Ahmad Zahid Hamidi would prefer an earlier vote, and the date is Anwar's call, so I hold this with low confidence.
3. Umno is **very unlikely** (~15%) to pull its ministers out of the government before Parliament is dissolved. Zahid said on 27 September that Barisan Nasional (BN) would stay until dissolution, calling this "our promise". With the CSA in place, leaving early would cost Umno its ministries without bringing the government down.
4. GPS is **very likely** (~85%) to win at least 70 of the 82 seats in the coming Sarawak election. It won 76 in 2021 and is contesting all 82. PSB, the opposition party that won four, has since dissolved, and its leaders joined the Progressive Democratic Party, a GPS component.
5. Real GDP growth is **likely** (~70%) to average at least 4% year on year over the first three quarters of 2027. Third-quarter 2026 growth was 5.3% by advance estimate, and the budget forecasts 4.2% to 5.2% for 2027. The main risks are a higher US tariff and a longer Middle East conflict.

## Drivers
### Political
PH has lost ground in three state elections. In Sabah in November 2025, DAP lost all eight seats it contested. In Johor on 11 July 2026, BN won 48 of the 56 seats it contested and PH fell from 12 seats to 8. In Negeri Sembilan, after all 14 Umno-BN assemblymen withdrew support from PH's Menteri Besar in April, BN and Perikatan Nasional (PN) ran in a formal pact on 1 August and won a two-thirds majority. Loke lost his own state seat.

The opposition is regrouping around Umno. The Registrar of Societies deregistered PN on 20 October, an order the High Court has stayed pending judicial review, and PAS spiritual leader Hashim Jasin said this frees PAS to work with Umno. BN and PN have formed another alliance for Melaka, which votes on 14 November.

Inside the government, DAP's Jelutong MP RSN Rayer accused Zahid of conspiring with the opposition to topple it, after Zahid said rural projects in Sabah and Sarawak had been shelved; Zahid denied this on 3 November. The CSA signatories want the prime minister's tenure limited and, before the term ends, the attorney-general's role split from the public prosecutor's.

### Security
Malaysia has no armed conflict. It has overlapping claims with China at Luconia Shoals off Sarawak. On 1 January the defence minister, Khaled Nordin, said the armed forces were monitoring a Chinese Coast Guard vessel reported to be patrolling inside what Malaysia regards as its exclusive economic zone, and that Chinese vessels there had been the norm since 2013.

### Economic
The 2027 budget, tabled on 9 October and passed at the policy stage on 29 October, expects fuel subsidies to stay at RM40 billion, a level it blames on what it calls the West Asia crisis. The 2026 federal deficit is now projected at 3.6% of GDP against a 3.5% target, and the government aims for 3.3% in 2027.

US trade policy is the main external risk. The US Supreme Court struck down Washington's blanket tariffs, which had charged Malaysia 19%, in February 2026. Since 24 July the US has charged a 10% duty on Malaysian goods under Section 301 measures related to forced labour. A second Section 301 investigation, into excess industrial capacity in 16 economies including Malaysia, is still open: on 29 September US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said he did not want to get ahead of its report, and USTR had announced no outcome by early November. Electrical and electronics (E&E) exports reached RM711 billion in 2025, about 65% of them semiconductors.

### Social
The budget calls the cost of living the main issue weighing on Malaysians and raises STR SARA cash aid to RM16 billion, for up to 9 million recipients. PH blamed its Negeri Sembilan defeat on its rivals' identity politics. In Johor, Ong Kian Ming estimates that BN's support among eligible Malay voters rose from 36.5% to 64.1% while PN's fell from 34.5% to 6.8%. Sabah's claim to 40% of the growth in net federal revenue from the state is before the Court of Appeal.

## Indicators to watch
- The Melaka result on 14 November, especially whether PH keeps the five seats it won in 2021.
- GPS's seat count in Sarawak, once the Election Commission sets the dates on 6 November.
- Any Umno move to leave the cabinet before dissolution, or a signed BN-PAS pact for GE16.
- The USTR excess-capacity finding, and any US duty on Malaysian goods above 10%.
- Fuel subsidy spending against the RM40 billion budgeted.
- The High Court ruling on PN's deregistration.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 29 (Moderate), 162nd of 203 countries, with no red flags and full data coverage. The score fell 0.8 points on last year and has stayed between about 29 and 33 every year since 2015.

The stability pillar (20, the lowest-risk of the four) rests on a political stability score that rose from about 69 in 2022 to about 72 in 2025, and on no battle deaths in 2024. Malaysian instability takes the form of defections and snap state polls, which these indicators barely register, and PN's deregistration and the CSA came after the 2025 data year. The economic pillar (40, the highest-risk) uses IMF figures for 2026, including government debt of 69.2% of GDP and a deficit of 3.5%. The model does not see how much of the export base is semiconductors, and the app's watchlist holds no Malaysia events. The political churn would justify a higher rating only if it reached the federal government, so I keep Moderate with a Stable outlook.

## Implications
Investors should expect policy continuity until GE16, with more cash aid than reform before the vote. Contractors to the rural and regional development ministry face delays: Finance Minister II Amir Hamzah Azizan says the ministry was told to hold off awarding new road contracts until unpaid bills are cleared. E&E exporters should keep contingency plans for a US duty above 10% until the excess-capacity report is out. Foreign governments should plan on Anwar as their counterpart for the next 12 months.

## Bottom line up front
I rate Myanmar's political risk Severe, one tier above the model's High, with a stable outlook to November 2027. The elections of December 2025 and January 2026 made coup leader Min Aung Hlaing president of a nominally civilian government, and China-brokered ceasefires in the north have let the army halt its losses. The war continues: the army opened a new offensive against the Arakan Army in September, an airstrike on a market in Kyauktaw killed at least 50 people, and the kyat has weakened to about 4,600 to 4,700 per dollar on the informal market. A ceasefire with the Arakan Army or the Kachin Independence Organisation (KIO) would move me towards High; the fall of Sittwe or Kyaukphyu, or price protests in Yangon and Mandalay, would point to the downside case.

## Key judgments
1. Min Aung Hlaing is **very likely** (~90%) to still be president in November 2027. Loyalists command the army and chair the upper house, he met no major internal pushback on his way to the presidency, and Beijing has backed him since August 2024. The main risk is a rift with the Union Solidarity and Development Party (USDP), whose veterans resent losing cabinet posts to retired officers.
2. The Arakan Army is **very unlikely** (~15%) to take full control of Sittwe or Kyaukphyu town before November 2027. Military sources quoted by DMG say it sank two navy ships near Sittwe on 26 October, but Crisis Group found in June that its push on Kyaukphyu had largely stalled and that Sittwe is heavily defended. I hold this with low confidence, because most front-line reports come from outlets close to one side.
3. The military is **very unlikely** (~15%) to agree a publicly announced ceasefire with either the Arakan Army or the KIO before November 2027. The army reportedly sought Chinese help for talks with the Arakan Army in February, but in September it launched Operation 4926 to retake ground in Rakhine, and in October the Kachin Independence Army (KIA) renewed its attacks in Bhamo.
4. The UN General Assembly is **unlikely** (~30%) to seat Naypyitaw's nominee in place of Ambassador Kyaw Moe Tun before November 2027. The Credentials Committee has deferred the question every year since 2021; this year's committee includes China and Russia, and Washington's new talks with Naypyitaw make a change more plausible, but deferral remains the easiest consensus.
5. Min Aung Hlaing is **unlikely** (~30%) to attend an ASEAN summit as Myanmar's leader before November 2027. ASEAN foreign ministers met their Myanmar counterpart in Bangkok on 12 July, the first in-person meeting since the coup, but ASEAN still bars Myanmar's leaders from summits, and that day Singapore, the incoming chair for 2027, said it was disappointed with progress on the Five-Point Consensus.
6. The kyat is **likely** (~70%) to trade weaker than 5,000 to the dollar on the informal market at some point before November 2027, from about 4,600 to 4,700 in mid-October. The government reportedly plans to borrow more than 2 trillion kyats from the central bank, and the ADB forecasts inflation of 24%.

## Drivers
### Political
The elections ran in three phases, with Aung San Suu Kyi's National League for Democracy dissolved and polling cancelled in large parts of the country. The military-backed USDP won 339 of 420 contested seats, which with the quarter of seats reserved for the army gives military-friendly members more than 86% of parliament; the authorities put turnout at 54%. Parliament elected Min Aung Hlaing president on 3 April and he was inaugurated on 10 April, having handed command of the army to General Ye Win Oo, a close ally and former head of military intelligence. On 30 April state television said Suu Kyi had been moved from prison to house arrest.

Min Aung Hlaing says his 100-day programme, which ended on 31 July, drew 13 ethnic armed organisations into talks; ACLED notes that none of them is fighting the army. DMG reported that the Arakan Army, the KIA, the Karen National Union (KNU) and the National Unity Government (NUG) were left out. On 30 March the NUG, the KIO, the KNU and two other ethnic organisations formed a joint steering council, but the NUG's claim to govern rests on the 2020 election, whose term has expired.

Naypyitaw's isolation is easing. Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi visited in April and India hosted Min Aung Hlaing from 30 May. On 15-16 September Michael Vance, the US assistant secretary of state for intelligence and research, held talks with him on scam centres and an American detainee, and Reuters reported that US officials also discussed access to Myanmar's heavy rare earths. In October the Treasury removed the daughter of a military-linked businessman from its sanctions list.

### Security
The army has regained momentum, but resistance forces still control many rural areas. Conscription has added about 100,000 men since early 2024, and Chinese pressure pushed the Myanmar National Democratic Alliance Army (MNDAA) and the Ta'ang National Liberation Army (TNLA) into ceasefires: the MNDAA returned Lashio in April 2025 and the TNLA left Mogoke that November. ACLED counts about 27 towns and trade corridors retaken this year up to July, and Tagaung, the last resistance-held town in Mandalay Region, fell on 10 March.

ACLED expects the fighting to intensify in Sagaing, Magway and Mandalay as the army moves troops from areas where ethnic armed groups have stopped fighting. In Rakhine the army launched Operation 4926 on 4 September and had not regained significant territory by the end of the month, according to ACLED; on 7 October it turned to amphibious landings on Ngapyi Island near Sittwe. Airstrikes have spread to towns the Arakan Army runs: on 28 September strikes on a market and a bridge in Kyauktaw killed at least 50 people and injured 57. In Kachin State the KIO holds the country around Hpakant and the Pangwa rare-earth mines, and the battle for Bhamo has run since December 2024.

### Economic
The ADB estimates that the economy shrank 2.2% in the year to March 2026. The earthquake of 28 March 2025 killed about 3,800 people and caused about $11 billion of damage by the World Bank's estimate, and the gradual recovery expected from rebuilding faltered when the US-Iran war began on 28 February 2026. Myanmar imports about 90% of its fuel oil, and in April fuel prices were 123% higher than a year earlier. Inflation reached 24.6% in April; the ADB forecasts 24% for the year to March 2027, with growth of 2.2%.

The kyat has three prices. On 2 November the central bank's reference rate was 2,100 to the dollar and its market trading rate 3,660; the informal rate was about 4,600 to 4,700 in mid-October, after two to three years at about 4,300 to 4,400.

### Social
In July OCHA counted 16.2 million people in need and nearly 3.8 million displaced; the $890 million appeal was 43% funded. OCHA also reports that displaced families in Kayah and southern Shan are under pressure to leave camps, some before it is safe to return. The World Bank put poverty at 29.9% in 2025. ACLED says emigration is shrinking the army's pool of recruits.

## Indicators to watch
- Who represents Myanmar at the ASEAN summit in Manila on 16-18 November, and Singapore's line as the incoming chair.
- The Credentials Committee's report on Myanmar's UN seat and the General Assembly vote on it.
- Fighting inside Sittwe or Kyaukphyu town, or Arakan Army or KIO meetings with army officers in China.
- The informal kyat passing 5,000 to the dollar, or a change to the 2,100 reference rate.
- The ICJ's merits judgment in The Gambia v. Myanmar, under deliberation since hearings closed on 29 January 2026.
- Further US talks with Naypyitaw, or open dissent from the lower house speaker, Khin Yi.

## Why my rating differs from the model
The model gives Myanmar 74 (High), seventh of 203 countries, with four red flags: war-level violence, inflation of at least 20%, mass displacement and a fiscal deficit of at least 5% of GDP. I rate it one tier higher. The score fell 0.8 points on last year, and political stability edged up from 26.5 in 2022 to 28.5 in 2025 on the app's scale. Both fit the lower risk of a disorderly regime collapse since 2024, while for anyone operating in the country the war and the currency have got worse.

The social pillar (54) is held down by unemployment of 3.05% and youth unemployment of 9.81%, modelled ILO figures that say little about a country where 16.2 million people need aid. The battle-death rate of 4.28 per 100,000 is an annual figure; Al Jazeera puts the war's toll at about 100,000 dead since 2021, an average well above it. The economic pillar (66) uses inflation of 20.0% and a deficit of 5.0% of GDP, while the ADB expects 24% inflation and the government reportedly plans to borrow more than 2 trillion kyats from the central bank. Reserves data are missing and the watchlist holds no Myanmar events. The stability pillar (73) rests on annual data, so the September offensive in Rakhine and the Kyauktaw strike are not yet in the score.

## Implications
A dollar buys 2,100 kyats at the reference rate and about 4,600 on the informal market, so a firm converting at the reference rate gets less than half the street value, and kyat earnings keep losing value. Western governments have kept targeted sanctions on the military leadership and its businesses, and US delistings have been individual cases, so counterparties still need screening. Firms on the China corridor or at Kyaukphyu face Arakan Army operations, and the KNU called the road from Myawaddy to Hpa-an an active conflict zone when the army reopened it on 2 April. NGOs face access restrictions and airstrikes on markets; Fortify Rights reported that troops detained three aid workers near Dawei in July, and their bodies were found 11 days later. Governments must decide on Myanmar's UN seat and ASEAN place in the coming weeks.

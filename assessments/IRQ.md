## Bottom line up front
Iraq's political risk is High and broadly stable over the next 12 months. The US-Israeli war on Iran, which began on 28 February 2026, cut crude exports from 3.36 million barrels a day (b/d) in February to 131,000 b/d in April and left Baghdad unable to pay salaries on time. Exports passed 3.4 million b/d in October, but the war is unresolved and almost all Iraqi oil still leaves through the Strait of Hormuz. The main driver now is whether Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi can bring Kataib Hezbollah and Harakat al-Nujaba under state control by 30 June 2027 without a fight. If Hormuz closed to Iraqi tankers again, or state forces fought those factions, I would move my outlook to Deteriorating.

## Key judgments
1. Ali al-Zaidi is **likely** (~75%) to still be prime minister on 1 November 2027. Parliament filled defence, interior and most other vacant posts on 15 October, the Coordination Framework backed his disarmament plan on 19 October, and Washington endorsed him after blocking Nouri al-Maliki. The main threats are a faction backlash or a new salary crisis.
2. Iraq's crude exports are **unlikely** (~30%) to average below 2 million b/d in any month before November 2027, since that would take Hormuz closing to Iraqi tankers again. The risk is live: UKMTO reported explosives attached to a tanker off Khasab on 29 October, US forces struck Iran's Qeshm Island on 2 November, and the Ceyhan pipeline carries only about 200,000 to 230,000 b/d.
3. The government is **very unlikely** (~15%) to announce by 30 June 2027 that Kataib Hezbollah and al-Nujaba have handed their weapons to the state. Both have refused, and Kataib Hezbollah said as the coalition left that it is bound by no political timetable.
4. A Gulf government is **unlikely** (~35%) to publicly blame launches from Iraqi territory for a drone or missile attack before November 2027, as Saudi Arabia did in July. With the factions' current pause on attacks, this turns mostly on whether the US-Iran war resumes at full scale. I hold this with low confidence.
5. Iraq is **unlikely** (~25%) to reach a staff-level agreement with the IMF before November 2027. Baghdad has asked for a programme, but on 29 October the Fund said it would first engage with the authorities to understand their priorities. Oil above $110 reduces the need, and Zaidi's plan to make 500,000 contract and daily-wage workers permanent would sit badly with fiscal consolidation.
6. Parliament is **likely** (~60%) to pass a 2027 budget law before 1 July 2027. The draft is due in parliament by 15 November, but the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK) will not vote for the current text, and as of May parliament had approved neither the 2025 budget tables nor a 2026 budget.

## Drivers
### Political
Mohammed Shia al-Sudani's list came first in the 11 November 2025 election with 46 of 329 seats. The ruling Shia Coordination Framework first put forward Nouri al-Maliki, then dropped him after President Trump warned that all US support to Iraq would stop if he took office. President Nizar Amedi named Ali al-Zaidi, a former bank chairman who had never held senior political office, on 27 April. Parliament approved 14 ministers on 14 May, with defence and interior among the posts left vacant. On 15 October it approved 11 more portfolios, including both, and three deputy prime ministers, among them Laith al-Khazali of Asaib Ahl al-Haq, whom the US designated a global terrorist in 2020. The vote on a migration minister was postponed.

Zaidi has to balance Washington, which wants Iran-backed militias dismantled, against Tehran-aligned partners in his own coalition. The next test is the Popular Mobilisation Forces (PMF) law, approved by his cabinet on 30 September and sent to parliament, which would make the PMF a permanent institution on a par with the other armed forces. Washington says it entrenches Iranian influence.

### Security
The Pentagon counted more than 600 attacks by Iran-affiliated militias on US citizens and facilities between February and April. On 29 July US and Saudi forces struck PMF bases in seven provinces, after Saudi Arabia said drones launched from Iraq had targeted oil facilities in Riyadh and the Eastern Province; Iranian and Iraqi media reported at least 20 PMF members killed. Baghdad called the strikes a violation of its sovereignty, and Zaidi cancelled his first visit to Saudi Arabia. Oil fields in the Kurdistan Region have faced repeated drone and missile attacks since the war began.

The US-led coalition ended its mission on 30 September, when the last forces left Erbil Air Base, and the US air defences at Erbil went with them. Kataib Hezbollah declared that "the resistance has won this round" and halted operations except against hostile aircraft. Asaib Ahl al-Haq, Kataib Imam Ali and Moqtada al-Sadr's Saraya al-Salam say their weapons are under state authority. The government's roadmap is a 90-day pause on attacks from 30 September, then a six-month handover ending on 30 June 2027. On 2 November the head of the Huqooq bloc, which is affiliated with Kataib Hezbollah, said a Coordination Framework committee had reached an agreement with the factions that Zaidi had not signed; the government has published no text. ISIS is weak, though at least one federal officer was killed in an attack near Kirkuk in October.

The US-Iran war is unresolved. A June interim agreement brought neither a lasting ceasefire nor normal shipping through Hormuz, and Qatari mediators reportedly travelled to Tehran on 2 November to discuss a draft deal.

### Economic
Oil provides more than 90% of state revenue. Exports recovered to 2.65 million b/d in September and passed 3.4 million b/d in October. Brent closed at $117.64 on 30 October. The Ceyhan pipeline through Turkey has been covered since 1 August by a one-year deal for 750,000 b/d, but carries a fraction of that volume.

A government source put revenue in May and June at no more than 3 trillion dinars a month, against a monthly salary and welfare bill of 7.8 trillion. The state has delayed salaries in several provinces, deferred payments to wheat farmers and construction firms, and borrowed short term from state banks. Foreign reserves fell from about $109 billion to $80.6 billion. In early October the cabinet devalued the dinar: the central bank now sells dollars to the public at 1,520 dinars, against 1,320 before, and dozens of MPs protested that parliament had not been told why. Iraq has asked the IMF for a new programme, its first since the last one ended in 2019, and the Fund forecasts a 16% contraction this year. The draft 2027 budget plans record spending of 217 trillion dinars and a deficit of about 43 trillion, assuming oil at $58.

### Social
Iraq pays roughly 8 million public employees, contractors, farmers and pensioners, and the model puts youth unemployment at 32%. Electricity Ministry staff protested in August over unpaid July wages. In Kurdistan, the regional government (KRG) said in October that federal transfers covered only 46 of 144 monthly payrolls from 2014 to 2025. The 2027 draft assumes the region markets 400,000 b/d through the state marketer SOMO, a volume it is not now exporting.

## Indicators to watch
- Monthly crude exports fall below 2.5 million b/d, or loadings at Basra stop.
- The 90-day pause on attacks, which runs to about 29 December 2026, lapses without renewal, or Kataib Hezbollah claims an attack.
- Zaidi signs and publishes the factions agreement, and whether it names Kataib Hezbollah and al-Nujaba.
- Parliament votes on the PMF law.
- The 2027 budget vote, and whether the Kurdistan Democratic Party and the PUK support it.
- Salary payment dates for federal and Kurdistan Region employees, and protests over unpaid wages.
- The parallel dollar rate moves more than 15% above the official 1,520, or the central bank changes the official rate again.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 73 (High), eighth of 203 countries, with five red flags. The score rose 4 points on last year, to its highest since 2020, though it remains below the 78 to 80 of 2015 to 2017. The economic pillar (68) carries IMF figures for 2026: real growth of -16.0%, a fiscal deficit of 15.1% of GDP and a current-account deficit of 10.8%. Those include the April trough, and with exports back at pre-war levels I expect the 2027 figures to look much better.

The stability pillar (60, the lowest of the four) errs the other way. The political stability indicator improved from about 26 in 2022 to about 33 in 2025, and the battle-deaths figure (0.86 per 100,000) is annual, so neither shows the militia attacks of early 2026, the July strikes on PMF bases or the Iranian missiles and drones fired at the Kurdistan Region. Homicide data are missing, the app's watchlist holds no Iraq events, and the reserves figure (11.6 months of imports) misses the fall to $80.6 billion. I read the two errors as roughly cancelling, so I keep High with a Stable outlook.

## Implications
Buyers lifting crude at Basra on free-on-board terms carry the Hormuz transit risk themselves. Contractors to the state should expect late payment until a 2027 budget passes and should price at the 1,520 official rate plus a parallel premium. Dealings with any office under Laith al-Khazali, a US-designated deputy prime minister, need sanctions advice. Operators in Kurdistan face drone risk to fields and a Ceyhan route running far below its contracted volume. Gulf governments should plan for launches from Iraq if the US-Iran war resumes; the end of the 90-day pause around 29 December is the first date to watch.

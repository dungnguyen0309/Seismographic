## Bottom line up front
Bhutan's political risk is Moderate and stable over the next 12 months. The main risks are economic: public debt of about 116% of GDP, fuel subsidies driven up by the US-Iran war, bad loans at a record Nu 11.7 billion and young people leaving for Australia. Revenue from the Punatsangchhu-II hydropower plant, exporting to India since September 2025, eases most of them. I would move to Elevated if fuel costs forced cuts to the 13th Five-Year Plan, or if the ruling party's August rift led to an early election.

## Key judgments
1. Tshering Tobgay is **very likely** (~90%) to still be prime minister on 1 November 2027. Eight of the People's Democratic Party's (PDP) 31 MPs backed the opposition's candidate for Speaker in August, but a no-confidence vote needs 32 of the 47 members, the opposition Bhutan Tendrel Party (BTP) has no plans for one, and the PDP MPs The Bhutanese spoke to said Tobgay would probably not dissolve the Assembly early.
2. A civil service pay rise is **likely** (~65%) to take effect before 1 November 2027. Tobgay said in October that the King had commanded a Pay Commission, and The Bhutanese expects Punatsangchhu-II to net Nu 9 to 10 billion in 2026-27. I hold this with low confidence, because the commission has only just been formed and the 13th Plan has no provision for a rise.
3. Australia is **likely** (~70%) to grant Bhutanese students and dependants fewer visas in July 2026 to June 2027 than the 10,167 of the previous year. The grant rate for Bhutanese fell from 89.9% in the second half of 2025 to 69.3% in the first half of 2026, and on 17 September Canberra said most international students will no longer be able to bring family. The Bhutanese consultancies' association expects that rule to apply from June 2027.
4. Right-bank stabilisation at the 1,200 MW Punatsangchhu-I project is **likely** (~65%) to have started by 30 June 2027. The work was due to start in April 2026, but approval of the final design was delayed and it is now expected in the first quarter of 2027. The draft supplementary agreement reached the contractor about six months late and still needs approval from a joint technical committee and the Project Authority. The cost estimate is about Nu 170 billion, against an approved Nu 93.75 billion.
5. Bhutan and China are **very unlikely** (~8%) to sign a boundary agreement or establish diplomatic relations before November 2027. Boundary experts who met in Thimphu on 13 to 15 October 2026 reported progress on their three-step roadmap but set no date for the next meeting. Turquoise Roof counted 22 Chinese villages and settlements inside what it calls Bhutan's customary borders in October 2024, and argues that Bhutan cannot in practice cede Doklam without India's consent.

## Drivers
### Political
The King sets the long-term agenda, above all the Gelephu Mindfulness City (GMC) on the Indian border. A royal decree reported on 2 November lets GMC's financial services office become a central bank that may invest in cryptocurrencies, with a path to its own currency subject to the King's concurrence. Gelephu's new airport has been under construction since July 2025, but India wants the flight information region over Bhutan settled first. India's foreign minister, S. Jaishankar, reaffirmed support for GMC in September, and the two sides welcomed a concessional INR 4,000 crore credit line for energy projects.

In elected politics, Home Minister Tshering resigned in August, blaming an anonymous Facebook campaign over a personal matter, and the energy minister also resigned. Speaker Lungten Dorji stepped down to move to the home ministry, and the PDP nominated Health Minister Tandin Wangchuk to replace him. On 19 August the BTP's Tempa Dorji beat him by 24 votes to 23. The opposition now holds two of the five seats on the committee that nominates constitutional office holders, and the PDP lacks the 32 votes needed to remove the Speaker.

### Security
The model records no battle deaths in 2024, and the main physical threat is natural hazards. On 29 October an outburst from Shintaphu, one of 17 potentially dangerous glacial lakes, flooded the Mo Chhu through Gasa and Punakha, washing away bridges and leaving at least 128 yaks and 12 horses thought dead; by 30 October no threat to human life had been reported. An ICIMOD specialist said another outburst from the water left in the upper lake could cause further floods, though this still needs checking on the ground.

### Economic
Public debt was 118% of GDP in June 2025; 91% of it is external, hydropower makes up 55% of external debt, and 69% of public external debt is in Indian rupees, to which the ngultrum is pegged. Between 11 March and 1 September the government spent Nu 2.047 billion holding down fuel prices, almost all on diesel, after moving Nu 2.5 billion out of the Economic Stimulus Programme.

Reserves were $1 billion, six months of imports, in October 2025, below the IMF's adequacy benchmark of seven months. Non-performing loans reached a record Nu 11.73 billion (4.07%) in August 2026, or 8.89% counting older loans already provisioned for. Druk Holding and Investments said in July that it holds over 10,000 bitcoin, the amount the King committed to GMC, and the IMF warns that the Royal Monetary Authority's assets are exposed to falls in crypto prices.

### Social
Australia granted 10,167 visas to Bhutanese students and dependants in the year to June 2026, second only to 15,552 in 2022-23. Voluntary civil service resignations rose 40.5% to 891 in the first half of 2026, led by education staff. The model puts youth unemployment at 17.05% in 2025.

## Indicators to watch
- Fuel support passes Nu 3 billion.
- Reserves fall below five months of imports, or the NPL ratio passes 5%.
- The Pay Commission's recommended rise.
- A motion to remove the Speaker, more PDP defections or a request to dissolve the Assembly.
- An India-Bhutan agreement on Gelephu air routes.
- Approval of the Punatsangchhu-I supplementary agreement.
- Australia's visa grant rate for Bhutanese students.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 34 (Moderate), 149th of 203 countries and down 0.1 on last year. The score has been Moderate every year since 2015 except 2021 (42, Elevated). Stability (19) is the lowest pillar, resting on a political stability indicator of about 90 in 2025 (about 89 in 2022) and zero battle deaths in 2024; governance is 31 and social 44.

The economic pillar (51) carries both red flags: debt of 116.2% of GDP and a current-account deficit of 16.3% of GDP, both for 2026. They overstate near-term risk, because hydropower loans, mostly from India, make up 55% of external debt and are repaid from power sales to India. Gaps elsewhere in the data point the other way. The reserves figure (6.61 months of imports) is from 2024 and predates the fuel shock, the model has no measure of bad loans, and its displacement indicator (0.92% in 2025) misses students leaving for Australia. The 2025 governance indicators predate the August rift, and the app's watchlist holds no Bhutan events. I read these errors as roughly cancelling.

## Implications
GMC investors should note the IMF's view that the legal framework separating GMC from the rest of Bhutan is at an early stage and needs clarity on the movement of labour, goods and capital. Crypto-asset activity is permitted there, which the IMF says makes strong enforcement and supervision more important, so counterparties need anti-money-laundering checks. Lenders should expect slower repayment from contractors and traders, whose NPL rates were 7.83% and 10.94% in August. Employers and donors relying on civil servants should expect more resignations.

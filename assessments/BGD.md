## Bottom line up front
Bangladesh's political risk is Elevated and stable over the next 12 months. Prime Minister Tarique Rahman's Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP) holds 209 of 300 seats, enough to amend the constitution alone, so the government is secure. The risk sits outside parliament: the Jamaat-e-Islami-led opposition has promised a street campaign over the July Charter reforms, garment wage protests have spread across greater Dhaka, and a third of bank loans are in default with no IMF programme in place. I would move the outlook to Deteriorating if wage stoppages lasted for weeks or opposition protests turned deadly.

## Key judgments
1. Tarique Rahman is **very likely** (~85%) to still be prime minister on 1 November 2027. On 29 October the BNP kept Thakurgaon-1, vacated when Mirza Fakhrul Islam Alamgir became president, by 227,917 votes to 96,513 for Jamaat. The realistic threat would be a split inside the BNP, and I see no sign of one.
2. Parliament is **likely** (~60%) to pass a constitutional amendment on the July Charter reforms under Article 142 before November 2027 without opposition votes. The parliamentary committee drafting the amendments hopes to place a bill before parliament in January, and the BNP's 209 seats clear the 200-vote threshold. The opposition has left its five committee seats empty, though, and a boycotted process invites delay.
3. The ban on Awami League activities is **very likely** (~85%) to remain in force on 1 November 2027. The law passed on 8 April keeps it until the International Crimes Tribunal (ICT) completes its trial, and on 4 October the prosecutor said investigators in the case against the party were still gathering evidence nationwide, with no timeline for a report.
4. Sheikh Hasina is **very unlikely** (~10%) to be in Bangladesh on 1 November 2027, by return or extradition. The ICT sentenced her to death in absentia in November 2025. Dhaka has repeatedly asked India to send her back, and New Delhi says it is examining the matter through legal procedures. I read her stated plan to return in December as a message to supporters.
5. There is a **roughly even chance** (~50%) that Bangladesh reaches a staff-level agreement with the IMF on a new programme before November 2027. The government decided in May to leave the 2023 programme, and the IMF confirmed its request for a new one on 3 June. Finance Minister Amir Khosru Mahmud Chowdhury said of the Fund on 4 October, "Their conditions are not acceptable to us", but on 12 October The Daily Star reported him saying that discussions were going on. Strong remittances reduce the pressure to accept terms on banks and tax.
6. The government is **likely** (~70%) to gazette a new garment minimum wage before 1 July 2027 that is below the Tk 30,000 a month protesters in Mirpur are demanding. The wage board has just been formed, and the general secretary of the IBC, whose unions asked for Tk 32,000, says a new wage structure could take effect in January.

## Drivers
### Political
The BNP-led alliance won 212 of the 297 seats declared after the 12 February election and the Jamaat-led alliance 77, on turnout of 59.88%; the National Citizen Party (NCP), which grew out of the 2024 protests, won six. Voters approved the July Charter in a referendum the same day. Appointed administrators hold local government posts, and an NCP MP told a UK Home Office fact-finding mission in June that local elections would be held the following year.

The fight is now over process. A commentator quoted by bdnews24 says the two sides are not visibly divided over restoring the caretaker government system or adding a second chamber. The opposition wants a separate Constitutional Reform Council, as the presidential implementation order of November 2025 provided; the government is drafting amendments in a special parliamentary committee and has said it will implement the charter without the notes of dissent. On 13 October Home Minister Salahuddin Ahmed said the government could not wait indefinitely, and Jamaat says its campaign will continue in parliament and on the streets.

The Awami League remains banned. The ICT has sentenced Hasina to death, then seven leaders including general secretary Obaidul Quader on 15 September and joint general secretary Mahbubul Alam Hanif on 29 October, all in absentia. The party still mobilises: several hundred people marched in Gulshan in September, and its student wing marched in Sylhet on 28 October. The BNP and Jamaat accuse each other of colluding with it, and Tarique has claimed, citing intelligence, that the "fugitive autocrat" is supplying money from across the border to fuel the labour unrest. None of the leaders making these claims has produced evidence in public.

### Security
Bangladesh has no armed conflict on the model's measure, which records no battle deaths in 2024. People interviewed by the Home Office mission described most political violence as local fights over land and business interests, much of it inside the BNP. A human rights adviser told the mission that 56 or 57 people had been beaten by mobs in the previous three to four months and that 98 shrines had been destroyed, damaged or looted. Interviewees said enforced disappearances had nearly stopped but that the police still favour the government, and on 2 and 3 November police clashed with garment workers blocking highways in Gazipur.

### Economic
Inflation was 8.34% in September, according to the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics, and IMF staff said in July that growth could slow to 3.5% in FY27 without reforms. The Middle East war has raised import costs and subsidy spending. A Bangladesh Bank study in April warned that gross reserves could fall to about $26 billion if the war persisted; instead, October remittances rose 12.1% to $2.87 billion and gross reserves were $36.33 billion on 29 October ($31.46 billion on the IMF's BPM6 measure). Gas is tight: LNG terminal maintenance cuts supply by about 12.5% from 4 to 7 November.

The banks are the weak point. Default loans reached Tk 6.07 trillion at the end of June, 32.78% of outstanding loans, up from 32.26% in March. When it confirmed Bangladesh's request for a new programme on 3 June, the IMF pointed to banking-sector weakness and low revenue.

Since 24 July the United States has charged a 10% Section 301 duty on Bangladeshi goods, under a measure aimed at countries it judges to lack safeguards against goods made with forced labour. It replaced a temporary tariff imposed after the Supreme Court struck down the reciprocal tariffs in February. With the 15.62% most-favoured-nation rate, garments face 25.62%, and China, Vietnam and Thailand are in the higher 12.5% band. In late July Dhaka was seeking clarity on whether the 19% rate in its 9 February trade agreement still applies. Bangladesh exported $7.74 billion of garments to the US in FY2025-26.

### Social
The wage protests began in Mirpur in late October and have spread to Gazipur, Savar, Ashulia and Narayanganj. Workers cite prices, rents and unpaid wages, and many protests have no union behind them. The BGMEA says disruption has kept factories from operating for seven days, and opposition leader Shafiqur Rahman claims nearly 600 have been declared closed and blames conspirators.

Bangladesh hosts 1,206,528 Rohingya refugees (as of 30 September), 1,173,291 of them in Cox's Bazar; 155,560 newer arrivals have been biometrically identified but have family cards instead of individual refugee identity documents. Home Office interviewees also described more violence against religious minorities, Hindus among them, before the election, and the attacks on the Prothom Alo and Daily Star offices under the interim government.

## Indicators to watch
- The constitutional amendment bill reaches parliament (targeted for January), and whether the opposition fills its committee seats first.
- Jamaat or the NCP call a nationwide hartal or blockade, or a protester is killed.
- The gazetted minimum wage, and whether highway blockades return to Gazipur and Ashulia after it.
- An IMF negotiation mission to Dhaka, and any staff-level agreement.
- Bangladesh Bank's next quarterly default loan ratio, and whether it passes 35%.
- BPM6 reserves below $28 billion, or monthly inflation above 10%.
- Whether Hasina attempts the December return she has announced, and India's answer to Dhaka's requests to send her back.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 51 (Elevated), 57th of 203 countries, with no red flags and full data coverage. The score fell 1.4 points on last year and has been Elevated every year since 2015, peaking at 53 in 2024, the year of the uprising.

The economic pillar (50) uses IMF figures for 2026 that fit what I see (inflation of 8.7%, growth of 3.6%), but no indicator measures bank asset quality, and the reserves figure (3.91 months of imports) is for 2025. The social pillar (25, the lowest) understates the risk more: its displacement indicator (0.07% in 2025) counts people who fled Bangladesh and leaves out the 1.2 million refugees it hosts, and youth unemployment (9.38% in 2025) says nothing about this month's unrest.

The governance pillar (75) is the highest, but its 2025 WGI figures, including control of corruption at 28, describe the interim period before an elected government took office. The political stability indicator moved only from about 47 in 2022 to about 49 in 2025. Homicide data are from 2018, and the app's watchlist holds no Bangladesh events. These gaps point to more risk than the score shows, but a government with a two-thirds majority offsets them, so I keep Elevated with a Stable outlook.

## Implications
Apparel buyers should expect stoppages around the wage decision and monthly pay dates, build extra lead time into orders from Gazipur, Savar and Ashulia, and confirm whether the 19% agreement rate applies before pricing 2027 contracts. Lenders need to screen Bangladeshi bank counterparties closely, and energy-intensive plants should plan for gas shortfalls when an LNG terminal goes offline. For India, Dhaka's requests to send Hasina back will keep straining relations, and a return by Hasina would be the most destabilising single event I can foresee in the next 12 months.

## Bottom line up front
Somalia's political risk is severe and rising. The federal parliament extended the government's mandate in March 2026, and the electoral commission has scheduled federal elections for April and May 2027 without the opposition, Puntland or Jubaland on board, while al-Shabaab retakes rural ground and the African Union force loses US funding on 31 December. A deal that brings Puntland and Jubaland into the vote would move my outlook to stable; a rival presidency or fighting inside Mogadishu would confirm the downside.

## Key judgments
1. Neither Puntland nor Jubaland will take part in the federal parliamentary vote set for 15 April 2027, whenever it is held before November 2027: **very likely** (~85%)
2. The presidential vote will be held on or before 15 May 2027, as scheduled: **unlikely** (~35%)
3. Government forces and armed units aligned with the opposition will clash inside Mogadishu before November 2027: **unlikely** (~30%)
4. Before November 2027, al-Shabaab will take, and hold for at least a week, a district capital in Hiraan, Middle Shabelle, Bay, Bakool or Galguduud that government or allied forces held on 1 November 2026: **likely** (~70%)
5. Before November 2027, al-Shabaab will control Mogadishu or a federal member state capital (Baidoa, Jowhar, Dhusamareb, Kismayo or Garowe) for more than 48 hours: **very unlikely** (~10%)
6. The Security Council will extend AUSSOM's authorisation past 31 December 2026 without UN-assessed funding under resolution 2719: **very likely** (~85%)
7. The IPC will classify Famine (Phase 5) in at least one area of Somalia before November 2027: **unlikely** (~25%)

## Drivers
### Political
The dispute is over mandates. On 4 March 2026, 222 of 329 members of both chambers approved constitutional changes that lengthen presidential and parliamentary terms from four years to five and delay elections by a year. When his original term ended on 15 May, President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud said he would stay until 15 May 2027, and opposition leaders withdrew recognition after US- and UK-mediated talks at the Halane compound collapsed.

On 15 October the electoral commission (NIEBC) set parliamentary elections for 15 April 2027 and the presidential vote for 15 May. The Somali Future Council, which groups opposition politicians with the leaders of Puntland and Jubaland, rejected the calendar, and former president Mohamed Abdullahi Farmaajo warned that it could lead to parallel elections and civil war. The NIEBC has run one-person-one-vote elections in Banadir, Galmudug and Hirshabelle, so polling in government-held towns is feasible. I still expect slippage, because registration and voting would have to reach the rest of the country in about five months, partly during the flood season, without Puntland or Jubaland.

Mogadishu has used the year to put allies in charge of three member states. The president's party took 177 of 390 seats in Banadir's local elections on 25 December 2025. His Justice and Solidarity Party (JSP) then won 67 of 89 seats in Galmudug's 30 September election rerun; Galmudug's parliament elected a JSP state president on 22 October, and Hirshabelle's re-elected the JSP incumbent on 24 October. South West suspended cooperation with Mogadishu on 17 March; federal troops took Baidoa in late March and the state president, Abdiaziz Laftagareen, stepped down. Puntland and Jubaland cut ties in 2024, and Puntland refuses to let the NIEBC operate on its territory. Somaliland stays outside; Israel recognised it in December 2025 and no other state has followed.

UNTMIS, the UN political mission, closed at the end of October and handed its functions to Somali institutions.

### Security
ACLED reported that al-Shabaab had regained more than 60 towns and villages in southern Somalia in the year to March 2026. On 26 October the group ambushed an army column near Jameeca in Burhakaba district, Bay. A surviving officer saw at least 20 dead soldiers; the defence ministry claimed about 75 militants killed and al-Shabaab claimed 321 soldiers. None of these figures could be verified.

The Hawadle clan militias (Ma'awisley) that held parts of Hiraan against al-Shabaab are now in open dispute with Mogadishu. On 30 October their fighters took over the Hiraan regional administration offices in Beledweyne as federal soldiers stepped aside. About 2,000 fighters are reported in the town, and units have left front-line posts in Mataban and Buulo Barde. Their leaders want an administration separate from Mogadishu and Hirshabelle, many Hawadle reject the Hirshabelle president's re-election, and there are fears that Hawadle soldiers in the federal army in Hiraan could defect.

The South West dispute has turned violent. Federal forces fought Laftagareen loyalists outside Baidoa on 3 September and again on 28 September, when the state claimed more than 70 attackers killed. In August, Puntland accused the federal government of sending arms and troops towards Galkayo.

US air power is growing while money for the AU force runs out. AFRICOM had carried out 68 strikes in Somalia by 22 June 2026, already more than the 63 of 2019, the annual record until 2025. Resolution 2809 authorises up to 11,826 AUSSOM uniformed personnel until 31 December 2026. On 1 July the US told the AU it would not fund the mission after that date and would block UN logistical support, though it would not veto renewal; it also opposes financing the mission under resolution 2719. A 10 September US letter proposed a 12-month drawdown of UNSOS; the troop contributors, meeting in Kampala in July, had asked for 18 to 24 months of support.

### Economic
In October 2025 IMF staff projected 3.3% growth for 2026, down from 4.1% in 2024, citing foreign aid cuts and weather shocks. On 8 December 2025 the IMF board completed the fourth Extended Credit Facility review and enlarged the programme by SDR 30 million (about US$40 million) as foreign aid kept falling. The model's 2026 snapshot shows 2.3% growth, 6.3% inflation and a current-account deficit of 25.4% of GDP.

Import costs are the new pressure. FEWS NET recorded a 34% rise in Mogadishu diesel prices between July and August, and warned that the Houthis' capture of coastal areas and islands near the Bab al-Mandeb in mid-September, together with piracy off Somalia, is raising shipping costs.

### Social
The model counts forcibly displaced people at nearly 23% of the population and youth unemployment at 34%. The IPC's April to June 2026 update put 6 million people in Crisis or worse, more than 1.9 million of them in Emergency. In September, FEWS NET saw a credible risk of Famine in southern Somalia from October 2026 to January 2027, which would become likely if extreme Deyr floods destroy crops and cut communities off, and expected food assistance to fall to about 125,000 people a month. OCHA's latest update, reported on 1 November, counts at least 175,000 people affected by El Niño floods in October and up to 3.4 million at risk through December. The 2026 humanitarian plan, which needs US$852 million, was 15% funded in May and 35% funded in that update.

## Indicators to watch
- Puntland or Jubaland registering voters for April 2027, or the Future Council issuing its own calendar
- The December AUSSOM decision, who pays for logistics after UNSOS support ends, and any troop withdrawal notice
- Renewal of the al-Shabaab sanctions regime (expires 30 November) and the Panel of Experts (31 December)
- Whether the Ma'awisley return to Mataban and Buulo Barde, and whether Jalalaqsi holds
- Further fighting in Baidoa or Galkayo
- Flood displacement in Bay, Hiraan and the Juba valley, and the next IPC analysis
- Completion of the fifth ECF review

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's Severe tier: a score of 90, third of 203 countries and up 1.6 points on last year, with red flags for war-level violence, mass displacement, a large current-account deficit and very high youth unemployment. The score's rise fits my deteriorating outlook, but the data understate how fast things are moving. The inputs are annual and lag: the WGI political stability score runs only to 2025 (29 in 2022, 28 in 2025), and the battle-death rate of about 12 per 100,000 predates this year's fighting. None of 2026's political shocks is in the data yet: the term extension, the takeover of Baidoa, the Beledweyne seizure, the UNTMIS closure and the end of US funding for AUSSOM. The app's watchlist still carries IFES ElectionGuide's unconfirmed date of 31 December 2026 for the presidential election, which the NIEBC calendar has overtaken.

The economic pillar, at 66, probably understates risk. Inflation of 6.3% and a fiscal deficit of 0.9% of GDP look moderate, but they miss Somalia's dependence on aid and its exposure to fuel and freight prices, and government debt and reserves have no data at all. The other pillars (stability 99, governance 97, social 96) sit near the ceiling, so the composite has little room to rise even if conditions worsen; the deteriorating outlook carries that signal instead. Confidence is moderate because casualty and territorial claims are rarely verifiable and much of the reporting rests on Somali outlets and the parties' own statements.

## Implications
Humanitarian agencies should expect access in Hiraan and Bay to narrow as militias leave front lines and floods cut roads, just as food assistance falls. Donors must decide whether to fund an electoral calendar the opposition rejects, and in December whether to bridge AUSSOM's funding gap; the second decision will shape security for the whole horizon. Firms in Mogadishu should plan for security lockdowns and possible clashes around the April and May 2027 votes, and for higher freight costs through the Gulf of Aden.

## Bottom line up front
I rate Bosnia and Herzegovina's political risk Elevated and stable over the next 12 months, the same tier as the model (54, 43rd of 203 countries). The 4 October general elections left Milorad Dodik's SNSD in a strong position even though his 2025 conviction bars him from office: its candidates won the Serb seat on the state Presidency and the Republika Srpska (RS) presidency. The main driver is Dodik's price for joining a state government, which includes annulling High Representative decisions and removing foreign judges from the Constitutional Court, at a time when the High Representative post has had only an acting holder since July and €373.9 million of EU money depends on a 31 December deadline. An RS assembly move against the state courts or the Constitutional Court would push me towards High; a state government by spring that passes the EU's judicial laws would support a lower rating.

## Key judgments
1. The state House of Representatives confirms a new Council of Ministers by 30 June 2027: **likely** (~60%). In the certified results SNSD has 6 of the 42 House seats and HDZ BiH 4, and BIRN describes Dodik and HDZ BiH leader Dragan Čović as key to forming a government at any level. On 3 November Dodik called cooperation with HDZ BiH the "unquestionable axis" of SNSD's plans and ruled out any partnership that includes the Party for BiH. I read his conditions as an opening position, since SNSD gets ministries and EU money only by joining. Confidence is moderate: Bosniak parties have not yet said what they would trade.
2. The European Commission confirms before November 2027 that Bosnia and Herzegovina has permanently lost at least part of the €373.9 million at risk under the Growth Plan: **very likely** (~85%). The grace period for the first 50 reform steps ends on 31 December 2026, new state authorities will probably not be in place by then, and the laws on the state courts and the High Judicial and Prosecutorial Council are still unadopted. In April the World Bank noted that it was the only country in the region to have drawn nothing from the Growth Plan.
3. Bosnia and Herzegovina holds its first intergovernmental conference with the EU before November 2027: **very unlikely** (~5%). The European Council decided to open negotiations in March 2024, but the Commission's 2026 report says the country still needs an operational chief negotiator and that the authorities' commitment to EU reforms remained partial.
4. The RS National Assembly adopts a law or constitutional amendment rejecting the authority of the state Constitutional Court, the state court or the High Representative, or calls a referendum on such a question, before November 2027: **unlikely** (~25%). The SNSD-led coalition holds 54 of 83 seats, just short of two-thirds, and Srpske Novine notes that whether it has a two-thirds majority will be clear only at the constitutive session. Against that, the US said it lifted sanctions on Dodik in October 2025 because RS had reversed laws that undermined Dayton, and repeating the exercise would put that relief at risk.
5. The Peace Implementation Council (PIC) Steering Board appoints a permanent High Representative before November 2027: **unlikely** (~35%). The US backs the Italian diplomat Antonio Zanardi Landi, while most Europeans and the UK back the French diplomat René Troccaz. ICDS suggests that keeping an American acting High Representative could be the best case for Washington, so it has little reason to compromise.

## Drivers
### Political
The results certified on 3 November kept the ethnic parties in charge. Željka Cvijanović (SNSD) and Denis Bećirović were re-elected to the Presidency, and Darijana Filipović of HDZ BiH won the Croat seat after Željko Komšić's two terms. Savo Minić (SNSD), the RS prime minister, was confirmed as RS president for four years; he took up the mandate on 9 November and handed his powers as prime minister to his deputy, Anđelka Kuzmić. In the 42-seat state House the SDA has 9 seats, SNSD 6, SDP 5, HDZ BiH 4 and the Party for BiH 3. On the preliminary count the SDA led the race for the Federation's House of Representatives with about 31%.

Dodik was convicted of defying the High Representative, given a one-year sentence converted to a fine and a six-year ban, and removed as RS president in 2025. The Central Election Commission annulled the early RS presidential vote of November 2025 at 136 polling stations over forged signatures and manipulated counts; SNSD's Siniša Karan held the post by March 2026. The RS assembly appointed three governments in the Commission's reporting period, and two resigned amid disputes over their legitimacy, Minić's most recently on 16 March.

Dodik's 3 November demands reach into the constitutional order. He wants High Representative decisions declared void through parliament and a Constitutional Court of three Serb, three Bosniak and three Croat judges with no foreign judges, and he described the EU path as "aimed at the dismantling of Republika Srpska". A report by House Democrats in September linked the US lifting of sanctions on him and Cvijanović in October 2025, and pressure on High Representative Christian Schmidt to resign, to a gas pipeline concession worth over $1 billion; the State Department rejects the link.

### Security
Violence is rare and the main risk is institutional. Schmidt announced on 11 May that he would step down and left on 1 July; Louis Crishock, his American deputy, has acted in the post since then with the full Bonn powers, an appointment Russia says lacks Security Council approval. The Security Council renewed EUFOR Althea's mandate at the end of October. Ratko Mladić's death in The Hague on 27 August led to a row with Serbia: his funeral in Belgrade on 7 September included military honours, the EU called the presence of high-ranking officials "deeply regrettable", and Bosnia expelled two Serbian diplomats the next day. On 25 September the Constitutional Court ruled that RS budget funding for a lobbying contract with unconstitutional aims breached the constitution; the foreign minister had told the Security Council in February that the contract named RS independence as a goal.

### Economic
Growth fell from 2.1% in 2025 to 1.7% in the first quarter of 2026, and the World Bank put inflation at 5% in March, with core prices rising. The EU's carbon border tax has applied to electricity exports since 1 January, which threatens a reliable source of revenue. The Commission's report notes that the country has been placed on the FATF grey list. EU grants and loans of up to €976.6 million stay out of reach while the reform steps go undone.

### Social
BIRN called the preliminary turnout of 42% a record low, and the Commission links the continued outflow of workers to labour shortages. Youth unemployment was 28.2% in 2025 in the model's data. ICDS cites polling that puts support for EU membership above 70%.

## Indicators to watch
- The constitutive sessions of the Presidency and the RS assembly (due within 15 days of the 3 November certification) and of the state House (within 30 days), and the new RS government, expected by 15 December.
- The Commission's ruling on the Growth Plan money after the 31 December deadline.
- Any RS assembly bill on the entity constitution, the Constitutional Court or High Representative decisions.
- Adoption of the laws on the state courts and the High Judicial and Prosecutorial Council, and the appointment of a chief negotiator.
- A PIC Steering Board decision on a High Representative, or a Security Council challenge to the acting appointment.
- Any reimposition of US sanctions on Dodik, which bipartisan US lawmakers urged in March.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier. The composite is 54 (Elevated), 43rd of 203 countries, with full data coverage and one red flag (youth unemployment of 28.2% in 2025). It rose 1.1 points on last year to the highest level in the app's history, which starts in 2015 at 49 and bottomed at 47 in 2021.

The social pillar (90) carries the score through youth unemployment, overall unemployment of 11.04% and displacement of 3.83% of the population, all from 2025. The governance pillar (62) reflects weak control of corruption (38.35) and government effectiveness (42.6), both 2025 WGI scores. The stability pillar (34) is low because the country had no battle deaths in 2024 and a homicide rate of 1.22 per 100,000 (2023), and the WGI political stability score has slipped only from 61.27 in 2022 to 59.65 in 2025.

The model misses recent events. Its WGI scores are from 2025 and predate the elections, Schmidt's departure and the fight over his successor. No indicator captures the FATF listing or the Growth Plan deadline, and the app has no watchlist events for the country. Its 2026 growth figure of 2.3% is above the 1.7% the Commission reports for the first quarter. These gaps add risk, but the absence of violence and general government debt of only 31.4% of GDP in 2026 keep the country short of High, so I agree with Elevated.

## Implications
Banks and investors should expect tighter due diligence on Bosnian counterparties while the country remains under FATF monitoring. EU-funded projects and municipalities should not count on Growth Plan money in 2027. Governments with programmes in RS should plan for a long government formation and a possible legal standoff between Banja Luka and Sarajevo, which would hit administration and the courts more than physical security.

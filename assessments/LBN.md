## Bottom line up front
Lebanon's political risk is High and roughly stable at the lower level of violence seen since the June framework agreement with Israel. Israeli forces still hold a zone in the south and kept up strikes and demolitions there through September and October, but the health ministry counted 36 deaths in those two months, against 4,333 between 2 March and 30 July. The main driver is Hezbollah's arsenal north of the Litani, whose verified disarmament the framework makes the condition for an Israeli withdrawal. I would move the outlook to Deteriorating if Hezbollah fired into Israel again, most likely if the US-Iran war resumes.

## Key judgments
1. Israeli forces are **very likely** (~85%) to still hold positions inside Lebanon in November 2027. The June framework lets Israel redeploy only after disarmament is verified, and at military talks in Tampa in late October Israel refused to widen the pilot area or accept third-party verification. Partial results from Israel's 27 October election gave neither bloc a majority, and Netanyahu's cabinet would stay on as caretaker if no government emerges.
2. Hezbollah is **unlikely** (~30%) to claim an attack on Israeli territory before November 2027. A senior Lebanese official told The National on 31 October that the group's entry into a renewed US-Iran war would depend on whether Iran's ruling system faced an existential threat. The figure is not lower because Israeli strikes continue and US-Iran hostilities may resume after the US midterms, and I hold it with low confidence.
3. The army is **unlikely** (~25%) to declare the second phase of its arms plan, north of the Litani, complete before November 2027. The extendable four-month timeframe the cabinet set in February has passed, Hezbollah refuses to disarm, and on 22 October the cabinet, reportedly on Speaker Nabih Berri's advice, put off sending a Hezbollah explosives case to the Judicial Council.
4. Lebanon is **unlikely** (~35%) to sign a staff-level agreement with the IMF before November 2027. The Fund first wants a 2027 budget, a medium-term fiscal framework and a financial gap law, which the government put forward in 2025 and parliament has not passed.
5. Nawaf Salam is **likely** (~75%) to still be prime minister in November 2027. His cabinet has survived the Hezbollah ban, the framework with Israel and the explosives row. The main threat is the resignation of the Shia ministers, or of the Lebanese Forces ministers for the opposite reason.
6. IOM is **likely** (~65%) to still count more than 150,000 people displaced inside Lebanon in its last figure before November 2027. The number fell by only 8% in September and October, to 304,520, and return to the border villages depends on the withdrawal I do not expect.

## Drivers
### Political
On 2 March, hours after Hezbollah fired rockets and drones at Israel to avenge the killing of Iran's supreme leader, the cabinet of President Joseph Aoun and Prime Minister Nawaf Salam banned the group's military and security activity and ordered it to hand over its weapons. AFP called it an unprecedented step. On 9 March parliament voted 76 to 41 to extend its own term by two years, postponing the May 2026 elections.

The explosives case is now the main test of the ban. On 5 October a checkpoint at Dahr el-Baydar, between the Bekaa and Mount Lebanon, stopped a car carrying hundreds of kilograms of explosives, including TNT and C-4. Hezbollah said the cargo was meant for use against Israeli forces and demanded it back. Judicial authorities have charged 13 alleged Hezbollah members, but only the two people caught with the shipment are in custody, and Lebanese Forces ministers walked out when the cabinet deferred the case's referral to the Judicial Council. On 13 October Hezbollah announced housing payments of up to $14,000 per destroyed home in Beirut and its suburbs, after Reuters reported a $200 million transfer from Iran, which the US disputes.

### Security
The war that began on 2 March had killed 4,333 people in Lebanon by 30 July. A ceasefire announced on 16 April was followed on 26 June by a framework signed by Lebanon, Israel and the US. Under it the army restores authority "pending the verified disarmament of non-state armed groups", pilot zones see a first Israeli pullback, and Israel redeploys only after verification. Hezbollah is not a party and rejects it.

The army deployed in a first pilot zone in July, but in Tampa Israel refused to widen the pilot area, asked for joint operations rooms that the Lebanese army rejected, and turned down Lebanon's call for third-party verification, possibly involving France, the US and UNIFIL. OCHA recorded airstrikes, shelling, incursions and demolitions through September and October in Tyre, Bint Jbeil, Nabatieh, Marjayoun and Hasbaya districts. The army says it finished the first phase of its plan, south of the Litani, in January; Netanyahu's office called that "far from sufficient".

UNIFIL's mandate ends on 31 December, and the force plans to withdraw all or substantially all of its roughly 7,500 troops by mid-2027, handing its positions to the Lebanese authorities. Direct US-Iran fighting, in a war that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran on 28 February, has eased at times, and The National reports growing fears that it could resume after the US midterms on 3 November.

### Economic
The World Bank expects the economy to shrink by 6.4% in 2026, after 4.2% growth in 2025, with inflation of 17.5%. It calls public debt unsustainable, and restructuring talks have not started more than six years after the state stopped paying its Eurobonds in March 2020. The IMF says rising energy costs have widened the current-account deficit.

Parliament amended the bank resolution law in August, but after an appeal by Aoun the Constitutional Council annulled Article 3 and parts of two other articles in late October, sending them back to committee. On 1 October the IMF listed its requirements for a staff-level agreement: a gap law consistent with international standards, a 2027 budget and a medium-term fiscal framework. The gap law would share out the losses from the 2019 crash, which the government put at about $70 billion in 2022, between the state, the central bank, the banks and depositors frozen out of their savings.

Reconstruction money is thin. The World Bank's Lebanon Emergency Assistance Project (LEAP) is a $1 billion framework with $250 million committed, and it was designed for damage from the 2023-24 war. The June framework makes internationally backed reconstruction in the southern border areas conditional on verified disarmament.

### Social
IOM counted 304,520 people still displaced inside Lebanon at the end of October, while 964,689 had returned to their home areas. About 11,200 remain in collective shelters, and 57 public education facilities are still used as shelters. Insecurity and explosive ordnance kept farmers from their land during the olive harvest, especially between Marjayoun and Hasbaya. The September to December response plan seeks $385.9 million. Hezbollah's base is mainly Shia, and Dania Arayssi of the New Lines Institute told Al Jazeera that the group has seen a decline in mass support.

## Indicators to watch
- Hezbollah's response if the US-Iran war resumes.
- Israeli evacuation orders north of the Litani, or strikes on Beirut's southern suburbs.
- Health ministry deaths above 100 in a month, or IOM's displacement count back above 330,000.
- Agreement on third-party verification or a second pilot zone at the next CENTCOM round.
- The UNIFIL handover after 31 December, and whether Italy keeps troops in the south.
- A referral of the explosives case to the Judicial Council, or resignations by Shia or Lebanese Forces ministers.
- Parliament's votes on the gap law, the returned bank resolution articles and the 2027 budget.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 72 (High), ninth of 203 countries, with three red flags (war-level violence, public debt above 90% of GDP and a current-account deficit above 5%). I reach the same level by a different route.

The score fell 0.4 points on last year and is five points below its 2024 peak of 77. The stability pillar (79) rests on 74.66 battle deaths per 100,000, the 2024 figure carried forward, so it reflects the 2023-24 war and not this year's. The model also misses what sits behind the current lull: an Israeli-held zone in the south, UNIFIL's exit and Hezbollah's arsenal. The political stability indicator (about 43 on the app's scale in 2025) predates the March war, and voice and accountability (about 47) does not reflect parliament extending its own term.

The economic pillar (55) credits Lebanon with 4.0% real growth in 2025, a fiscal surplus of 3.3% of GDP and reserves worth 13.6 months of imports, a 2023 figure. The World Bank expects a 6.4% contraction this year, and I read the surplus partly as the product of a state that stopped paying bondholders in 2020 and has not settled with depositors. The displacement indicator (1.42% of the population displaced abroad) misses the 304,520 people displaced at home, and the app's watchlist holds no Lebanon events.

## Implications
Companies should treat the districts south of the Litani as an active conflict zone, Nabatieh, Marjayoun and Hasbaya above all. In September and October 42.5% of humanitarian movements notified for hard-to-reach areas there were refused or came with limiting conditions. Until the gap law passes, banks cannot be relied on to release frozen deposits, and Eurobond holders should not expect a restructuring offer within the horizon. Reconstruction contractors need screening: the June framework commits Lebanon to keep reconstruction funds from non-state armed groups and connected entities, and Hezbollah's housing survey uses forms from Meamar Construction, which the US sanctioned in 2020. NGOs should plan access for after UNIFIL, whose patrols now remove improvised explosive devices daily. Donors who want the framework to hold will have to fund the army and close LEAP's $750 million gap.

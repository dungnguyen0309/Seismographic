## Bottom line up front
I rate Romania's political risk Elevated and stable over the next 12 months, in line with the model (44, 98th of 203 countries). More than five months of caretaker government ended on 20 October with Luca Niculescu's minority cabinet, which rests on Social Democrat (PSD) ministers and a National Liberal (PNL) pledge not to back a no-confidence motion before 1 March 2027. The main driver is whether that arrangement can pass a 2027 budget that keeps cutting the EU's largest deficit while the economy stalls and the nationalist AUR leads the polls. A downgrade to junk or a second collapse of the government in the spring would move me to High.

## Key judgments
1. Parliament adopts the 2027 state budget by 31 January 2027: **likely** (~75%). UDMR (the Hungarian minority party) has agreed to back it from outside the cabinet, and the PNL's pact with Niculescu runs until the budget is adopted. The figure equals my base and upside scenarios together.
2. Niculescu is still prime minister on 1 November 2027: **likely** (~60%). President Nicușor Dan needed four nominees to find a majority, which gives every party a reason to avoid another round. Against that, the PNL pledge lapses on 1 March, and AUR's Senate leader, Petrișor Peiu, said on 10 November that his party would vote for the first motion the PNL or USR tables.
3. At least one of Fitch, Moody's or S&P cuts Romania below investment grade before 1 November 2027: **unlikely** (~25%). All three rate Romania at the lowest investment grade with a negative outlook and review it in early 2027. The deficit for January to September fell to 3.81% of GDP from 5.31% a year earlier, and the new government's first eurobond, for €4.5 billion, drew demand of twice that amount. A downgrade belongs to my downside scenario.
4. Eurostat's April 2027 notification puts Romania's 2026 deficit at or below 6.5% of GDP: **likely** (~70%). The target is 6.2% and Moody's expects 5.8%. The risks are the second revision of the 2026 budget, due by the end of November, and an economy that shrank in the first quarter and stood still in the second.
5. AUR leads every national voting-intention poll published in October 2027: **very likely** (~85%). It had 34% with CURS and 35% with Avangarde in late October 2026, against 26% for the PSD in both, and it has led every poll since late June.
6. A Russian drone or its debris kills someone on Romanian land before 1 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~15%). In May a drone hit an apartment block in Galați and injured two people, the first incursion known to have hurt civilians. No death on land has been reported, and Romanian F-16s have shot several drones down since July.

## Drivers
### Political
Ilie Bolojan's coalition of the PSD, PNL, USR and UDMR broke on 20 April 2026, when Bolojan proposed listing minority stakes in state companies such as Hidroelectrica and Romgaz and the PSD withdrew its ministers. A joint PSD and AUR motion brought the cabinet down on 5 May with 281 votes. Dan's first nominee withdrew and parliament rejected the next two. Niculescu, an independent diplomat and former journalist, then won 280 votes to 58. His cabinet has 12 PSD ministers and seven independents, with Alexandru Nazare kept at finance.

The arrangement is short-term. Under the pact it signed with Niculescu, the PNL will not initiate or vote for a no-confidence motion until 1 March 2027, provided he keeps the deficit targets Romania agreed under earlier governments. Bolojan said the country needed a working government after more than five months of crisis. The programme still includes listing minority stakes in state companies, the issue that broke the last coalition, and on 10 November the PNL and USR accused the PSD and AUR of sharing out posts in state institutions. The PNL, USR and AUR hold 222 of the 462 occupied seats between them, short of the 233 votes a motion needs, so it would also need smaller nationalist groups or PSD defectors.

The presidential election was annulled in December 2024 over suspected Russian interference in favour of Călin Georgescu, and Dan won the rerun in May 2025. Georgescu is barred from running and is on trial in two cases, one of them for attempting to disrupt national security. On 12 November prosecutors indicted him over a €1.1 million fraud; his wife called the charges politically motivated. The next parliamentary election is due by 30 November 2028.

### Security
Russian attacks on Ukraine's Danube ports keep spilling over the border. By 21 August Russian drones had violated Romanian airspace 23 times in 2026, against 18 times from 2022 to 2025. On 14 and 15 October drones hit two tankers in or near Romanian waters off Sulina, and a NATO military source said both drones were Russian; seven of the Wine Trader's 12 crew were reported missing. Two days later F-16s shot down two drones near Sulina that Defence Minister Radu Miruță said were probably jet-powered Geran-4s. On 31 October two more crossed the north-east, setting off alerts in six counties including Iași and Suceava. In August the defence ministry said it could not conclude that the incursions were deliberate.

### Economic
Eurostat put Romania's 2025 deficit at 7.8% of GDP, the largest in the EU, down from 9.2% in 2024. Bolojan's austerity raised taxes and froze public wages and pensions. The economy shrank 0.1% quarter on quarter in the first quarter of 2026 and stood still in the second. CPI inflation fell from 10.42% in June to 6.17% in August as the 2025 tax rises dropped out of the base, then rose to 6.94% in October on higher fuel and energy prices. On 12 November the central bank held its rate at 6.50% and named the domestic political situation among the sources of uncertainty.

The revision adopted on 30 October kept the 2026 deficit target at 6.2%, and the state still has to borrow about RON 70 billion by the end of the year. For 2027 the programme aims at 5.2%, while Moody's expects 5.5%. Public wages and pensions are to be indexed in January 2027, within the limits of the EU's excessive deficit procedure.

### Social
Household consumption fell further in the second quarter, and ILO unemployment was 6.4% in the third. The model's youth unemployment figure is 24.54% for 2025; Niculescu says Romania has the EU's highest share of young people up to 28 without a job. In a business barometer presented on 3 November, about a third of companies planned to invest, against more than half in 2024.

## Indicators to watch
- The second revision of the 2026 budget, due by the end of November, and whether it keeps the 6.2% target.
- The 2027 deficit target in the draft budget.
- Any PNL statement on the pact before 1 March 2027, and any no-confidence motion that AUR signs.
- Rating reviews by Fitch, Moody's and S&P in early 2027.
- CPI inflation ahead of the central bank's 19 January meeting; a rise above 7% would delay rate cuts.
- Drone incursions away from the Danube border, like the one over the north-east on 31 October, and any casualties on land.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 44 (Elevated), 98th of 203 countries, 0.1 points lower than last year, with full data coverage. The score was Moderate in every year from 2015 (32) to 2023, crossed into Elevated in 2024 (43) and was 44 in 2025. It carries four red flags, including both deficits at 5% of GDP or more.

The economic pillar (73) carries the score, and most of its 2026 figures fit events: a deficit of 6.2% of GDP, equal to the government's target, growth of -0.5% and a current account deficit of 7.0%. Its average inflation of 8.2% is below the 9.0% twelve-month average the central bank reported for October. The stability pillar (26) uses the WGI political stability score, where higher means more stable, which fell from 75.16 in 2022 to 69.23 in 2025. It misses the drone campaign: battle deaths are 0 (2024 data), homicide is 1.11 per 100,000 (2023), and the app's watchlist has no Romania events. The governance pillar (38) uses 2025 WGI data, so it predates the April collapse and the caretaker months that followed. The social pillar (49) includes youth unemployment of 24.54% and overall unemployment of 5.99%, both for 2025.

These gaps push the risk up, while the falling deficit and demand for Romanian debt pull it down. Neither closes the 16 points to High, so I keep Elevated and Stable.

## Implications
Bond investors should treat the 2027 budget vote and the early 2027 rating reviews as the dates that decide Romania's investment-grade status. Companies interested in minority stakes in Hidroelectrica, Romgaz or CEC Bank should expect delays: the listing broke the last coalition, and the Senate passed a PSD moratorium on such sales after the cabinet fell. Shipping firms using Sulina, Galați and Tulcea should plan for drone strikes on vessels in Romanian waters.

## Bottom line up front
I rate Russia's political risk High, in line with the model (61, 25th of 203 countries), and expect it to stay there over the next 12 months. The Kremlin's hold on politics tightened with a stage-managed Duma win in September. The cost of the war is rising at home: Ukrainian drones have cut about 30% of refining capacity, and the 2027 budget leans on new taxes to pay for record military spending. A general ceasefire with sanctions relief would bring my rating down; a new mobilisation or a fiscal squeeze that forces deep cuts would push it towards Severe.

## Key judgments
1. A general ceasefire along the whole front line is in force on 12 November 2027: **unlikely** (~20%). The Abu Dhabi talks on 10-11 November produced a planned prisoner exchange, and the US said differences remained on territory. Putin has since said any European troops in Ukraine would be legitimate targets and repeated his claim to the four Ukrainian regions Russia declared annexed in 2022, which Ukraine regards as occupied. A ceasefire happens only in my upside scenario. Confidence is low, because US policy towards Moscow has changed within weeks this autumn.
2. The Bank of Russia's key rate is below today's 14% on 12 November 2027: **likely** (~70%). The Bank's baseline has the rate averaging 12.5-13.5% in 2027 and inflation back at 4%. The risk is that fuel shortages and fiscal stimulus keep inflation, 6.8% on 19 October, from falling.
3. Russia announces a new general or partial mobilisation before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~15%). Putin called talk of one "complete nonsense" on 1 September, and some observers note that the 2022 order, still in force, means a new call-up need not be announced at all. Losses are the pressure point: Ukraine's General Staff claims 54,400 Russian casualties in October, a figure Al Jazeera could not verify.
4. The Duma amends the 2027 budget to raise the federal deficit above the 5.44 trillion roubles in the draft before 12 November 2027: **likely** (~65%). The 2026 deficit reached 5.8 trillion roubles in the first eight months, already above the revised full-year target of 4.8 trillion, and the 2027-2029 draft assumes inflation of no more than 4% in each year.
5. The US removes Rosneft or Lukoil from its blocking sanctions before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~15%). Washington has eased at the edges, with a general licence for Russian diesel to 7 April 2027 and a waiver for Lukoil's petrol stations abroad to 25 April 2027, but delisting would need a peace deal, which comes only in part of my upside scenario. Congress also has a hand: the Graham sanctions law, signed in late September, lets the US sanction countries that buy Russian oil.

## Drivers
### Political
No national election falls within the horizon: the president was elected in 2024 and the Duma this autumn. In the State Duma election of 18-20 September, United Russia won 349 of 450 seats and close to 58% of the party-list vote, its best result ever, on an official turnout of 59.8%, the highest since 2011. The European Parliament's research service cites independent estimates that 14 to 25 million United Russia votes were fabricated. The Supreme Court removed Yabloko, the only registered party campaigning for an end to the war, from the federal list on 10 August after its polling rose above 5%, and its deputy chair Lev Shlosberg was sentenced a week later to 11 years and one month.

Repression keeps rising. In June OVD-Info counted at least 4,800 people imprisoned or prosecuted for political crimes, and Memorial found that people convicted in 2026 received an average of 11 years, against 6 years and 7 months in 2021. The co-chair of the Golos election monitor, Grigory Melkonyants, was sentenced to five years. Polls this year, the Kremlin's own included, show Putin's approval falling and more Russians wanting the fighting to stop, though no organised force can turn that into pressure on the government.

### Security
Russia's offensive has stalled. ISW counts 117.38 km² taken in the first ten months of 2026, against 5,304 km² in 2025, and losses of ground in September and October. Russia still launched 2,346 drones and more than 80 missiles at Ukraine in the week to 6 November.

Ukraine's strikes now reach deep into Russia. Two industry sources told Reuters on 10 November that Ukrainian strikes since late October had cut refining capacity by about 30%, with nine of 35 major refineries disrupted in a week; a drone hit Kirishi, the second-largest, on 9 November and killed one woman. Ukraine says it has hit the five largest refineries since 30 October. On 12 November, the day US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Putin in Moscow, a Russian serviceman was killed when his car was blown up in St Petersburg. The envoys came from the Abu Dhabi round, the first US-mediated direct talks between Russia and Ukraine in nine months.

### Economic
The Bank of Russia held its rate at 14% on 23 October, put underlying inflation at 5-6% and expects 7.1-7.6% at the end of 2026. It now sees growth of 0.5-1.0% in 2026 and 1.0-2.0% in 2027, and estimates third-quarter growth at 1.0% year on year.

The 2027-2029 budget passed its first reading on 29 October. It plans a 2027 deficit of 2.2% of GDP, against 1.2% in the current law, and record defence spending of 17.1 trillion roubles. New taxes include a 20-30% windfall levy on miners, rates of 13-22% on interest, dividends and property sales, and 22% VAT on cheap foreign goods bought online from 1 January. The liquid part of the National Wealth Fund has fallen to 1.7% of GDP, and the Kyiv School of Economics expects Russia's buffers to run out by early 2028 without more tax rises or cuts. Higher oil prices caused by the war in Iran have softened the blow: Brent settled at $105.8 a barrel on 15 October.

### Social
Unemployment is at a record low, which limits discontent. Consumers carry the costs: filling stations in Karelia rationed fuel over the summer, and tax and utility price rises followed within days of the election.

## Indicators to watch
- Any date for a Putin-Zelensky meeting after the Abu Dhabi talks.
- The EU listings expected on 16 November.
- The Duma's second and third readings of the 2027 budget, then any amendment in 2027.
- The Bank of Russia's decision on 18 December and its signal for 2027.
- Refining capacity offline, fuel export bans and rationing beyond the regions hit so far.
- Renewal of the US diesel licence (7 April 2027) and Lukoil waiver (25 April 2027), and any use of the Graham law.
- A mobilisation decree, or NWF liquid assets below 1% of GDP.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model: 61 (High), 25th of 203, with full data coverage and one red flag for war-level violence. The score fell 1.3 points from 62 in 2025, the first year it reached High; in the app's history, which starts in 2015, its low was 46 in 2021.

The governance pillar (83) is the highest of the four, driven by the 2025 WGI readings for voice and accountability (31.29) and control of corruption (28.21), and 2026 has only confirmed those readings. The stability pillar (78) rests on battle deaths of 5.34 per 100,000 in 2024 and a political stability score that fell from 52.31 in 2022 to 49.05 in 2025; neither captures the refinery strikes or the killing in St Petersburg, and the app's watchlist holds no Russia events.

The economic pillar (42) uses 2026 estimates: growth of 0.9%, inside the Bank's range, and a general government deficit of 3.2% of GDP. Its average inflation of 6.1% sits below the Bank's year-end forecast of 7.1-7.6%. Debt of 19.3% of GDP and a current account surplus of 3.8% look comfortable, but the reserves figure (16.78 months of imports) dates from 2024, and the liquid part of the National Wealth Fund is down to 1.7% of GDP. The social pillar (14) is low because unemployment was 2.13% in 2025 and few Russians are displaced; it misses fuel shortages and the risk of a call-up. Those gaps would add a few points, and the stalled offensive and US licences take some away, so I keep High and Stable.

## Implications
Firms still trading with Russia face more sanctions risk than the US licences suggest. Both licences expire in April 2027; Senator Richard Blumenthal, a co-author of the Graham law, warned a Greek shipping company on 27 October not to unload Russian diesel in the US; and the French minister Benjamin Haddad says the EU will list 150 more entities and 50 banks on 16 November. Western firms with Russian subsidiaries should note that Metro's local unit was taken under temporary state administration and made to pay 1 billion roubles to a war fund. Lenders and suppliers should plan for higher taxes from 1 January and for the Bank to hold rates high if fuel shortages push inflation up.

## Bottom line up front
I rate Germany's political risk Moderate, the same tier as the model (27, 170th of 203 countries), with a deteriorating outlook. Chancellor Friedrich Merz's CDU lost badly in the September state elections, the AfD has led every national poll published since mid-October, and an ARD poll on 1 October found only one in ten Germans satisfied with Merz. A coalition break or Merz's fall after the North Rhine-Westphalia election on 25 April 2027, or an AfD government in Saxony-Anhalt, would move me towards Elevated.

## Key judgments
1. Merz is still chancellor on 12 November 2027: **likely** (~75%). Short of resigning, he can be removed only if the Bundestag elects a successor, and the CDU has no obvious one. The threat is a party revolt after a poor North Rhine-Westphalia result: INSA had the CDU there at 26% on 16 October, against 35% in an Infratest dimap poll on 1 February.
2. A federal election is held before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~10%). Neither coalition party gains from an early vote with the CDU/CSU near 20% and the SPD near 14%, and Merz said after the September elections that he was "going nowhere".
3. An AfD politician is elected minister-president of Saxony-Anhalt before 12 November 2027: **unlikely** (~20%). The AfD holds 39 of 83 seats, three short of the 42 needed in the first two ballots. Unless the Landtag then dissolves itself, a further ballot needs only a simple majority of votes cast, and abstentions do not count. The BSW's five members have rejected electing Ulrich Siegmund. If they abstain rather than vote against him and the other four parties all vote no, the count is 39 to 39, so one defection or absence would decide it.
4. The AfD is ahead of the CDU/CSU in PolitPro's polling average on 1 November 2027: **likely** (~75%). The average stands at 27.3% to 19.8%, and every poll since mid-October has put the AfD ahead, by 3 to 11 points.
5. The coalition passes a law that ends or restricts full-pension retirement after 45 years of contributions before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~15%). The CDU's business wing wants the rule phased out, but the SPD leadership and state premiers want to keep it. Only my upside scenario has a pension reform passing.
6. An application to ban the AfD reaches the Federal Constitutional Court before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~7%). In mid-September the question was still with a federal-state working group. On 26 February the Cologne Administrative Court barred the domestic intelligence service from treating the federal party as confirmed extremist until the main case is decided, so it remains a suspected case.

## Drivers
### Political
The state elections went badly for the federal coalition. In Baden-Württemberg on 8 March the Greens (30.2%) beat the CDU (29.7%). In Rhineland-Palatinate on 22 March the CDU (31.0%) beat the SPD (25.9%), which had governed the state for 35 years, with the AfD on 19.5%. Then on 6 September the AfD won Saxony-Anhalt with 43.8% against 17.2% for the CDU, and on 20 September it came first in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania with 38.2%, where the CDU fell below 5% and lost all its seats. The same day the Left won Berlin with 25.7%.

The other parties still keep the AfD out of government. In Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania the SPD, Left and Greens announced a coalition agreement on 10 November. In Berlin the Left, Greens and SPD began talks on 2 November, complicated by disputes over how to tackle antisemitism. In Saxony-Anhalt Sven Schulze's CDU government stays on as caretaker, and the AfD's Tobias Rausch became speaker on 6 October with BSW support. Siegmund plans to stand for minister-president in December; the BSW wants a non-partisan candidate instead.

The federal coalition is divided over pensions, and in budget talks on 11 November it backed away from some tax and welfare plans, senior lawmakers told Reuters. Merz has nominated the CSU's Ilse Aigner for federal president, a choice the SPD has often said it supports; the Federal Convention votes on 30 January.

### Security
The model records no battle deaths (2024); the risk comes from Russia. Berlin blamed Moscow for a drone incident at Leipzig airport in August, which Russia denies, and on 26 September Sergey Lavrov told the UN General Assembly that Germany is aiming to be a top military power in Europe. Germany hosts a meeting of Coalition of the Willing leaders on Ukraine in Berlin on 19 November and plans to spend over €109 billion on defence in 2027.

### Economic
On 11 November the Council of Economic Experts significantly raised its growth forecast, its chair said. The 2027 budget plans spending of about €555 billion and core net borrowing of nearly €119 billion, €85 billion of it under the debt brake's exemption for defence; with the special funds, borrowing is close to €200 billion a year. The BDI expects interest costs to roughly double to about €80 billion by 2030.

### Social
Unemployment was 3.71% in 2025 and youth unemployment 6.86%. Migration remains contested: Merz's "Stadtbild" remarks, widely read as saying there were too many migrants on the streets, drew criticism again after the September results. In Rhineland-Palatinate the AfD won 39% of manual workers, against 21% for the SPD.

## Indicators to watch
- The Saxony-Anhalt ballots in December, the BSW's votes and any motion to dissolve the Landtag.
- The Berlin coalition talks and the housing chapter of any agreement.
- The North Rhine-Westphalia result on 25 April 2027, especially the CDU's lead over the AfD.
- Merz's ARD satisfaction rating and any challenge to his CDU leadership.
- A ruling in the AfD's main case in Cologne.
- Drone or sabotage incidents that Berlin attributes to Russia.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model: 27 (Moderate), 170th of 203, down 0.2 points on last year, with full data coverage. The score was 20.63 in 2015, when the app's series starts, and rose every year from its low of 20.29 in 2019 to 26.99 in 2025. The one red flag is political stability: the WGI score fell from 75.46 in 2022 to 69.26 in 2025.

The economic pillar (55) carries the score, through a 2026 deficit of 4.3% of GDP, 2026 debt of 64.6% of GDP and reserves of 3.11 months of imports in 2025, a figure that means little for a euro area member. It has growth of 1.3% and inflation of 2.7% for 2026 and nothing yet for 2027.

The model misses the politics. The WGI data stop in 2025, before the September elections, no indicator measures the AfD's lead or the coalition's strain, and the app's watchlist holds no German events. These gaps outweigh the overstated economic risk and put Germany in the upper half of Moderate, well short of the 40 needed for Elevated, so I keep Moderate with a deteriorating outlook.

## Implications
Residential investors in Berlin should read the housing chapter of any Left-led coalition agreement before committing new money. Defence suppliers can plan on a 2027 defence budget of over €109 billion. Companies in Saxony-Anhalt should expect months of caretaker government and, in the downside case, an AfD-led administration.

## Bottom line up front
I rate Armenia's political risk Elevated, the same tier as the model (51, 56th of 203 countries), and stable over the next 12 months. Nikol Pashinyan won a third term on 7 June 2026 and the border with Azerbaijan is quiet, but the peace treaty initialled in Washington in August 2025 is stuck on Baku's demand for a new Armenian constitution, which Civil Contract cannot put to a referendum without six opposition votes. At home the fight with the opposition and the Apostolic Church is getting harsher: Samvel Karapetyan was detained again on 4 November and a Strong Armenia candidate died in custody on 10 November. A Russian gas cut-off or Armenia's suspension from the Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU) would move me towards High; a scheduled referendum followed by a signed treaty would move me towards Moderate.

## Key judgments
1. Armenia and Azerbaijan sign the peace treaty before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~15%). Baku will not sign until the constitution changes, the draft had still not been published at the end of October and both opposition blocs refuse to send it to a referendum; Pashinyan has said signing could come "as early as 2027 or 2028". A referendum also needs "yes" votes from 25% of registered voters, when turnout at the last three parliamentary elections averaged 52%.
2. Armenia holds an early parliamentary election before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~10%). Pashinyan raised it on 22 October as a route to the 70 seats a referendum needs, then said there was a "very high probability" that it would not come to that. With about half the vote in June, Civil Contract has little reason to risk its majority.
3. A clash on the Armenia-Azerbaijan border kills at least one soldier or civilian before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~8%). There have been no serious incidents for a long time and some two-way trade has begun. The risk is Baku adding conditions, such as the apology Hikmet Hajiyev demanded on 26 October, and backing them with pressure on the ground.
4. Armenia submits a formal application to join the EU before 12 November 2027: **unlikely** (~35%). Pashinyan told Euronews on 3 November that applying in 2027 or 2028 was realistic. An application would invite the energy retaliation Moscow has threatened, so I expect him to wait while the treaty is unsigned.
5. Armenia is still a member of the EAEU on 12 November 2027: **very likely** (~85%). Pashinyan told Euronews that the choice between the EU and the EAEU belongs to Armenians, and on 11 November the Collective Security Treaty Organization (CSTO) stripped Armenia of its votes without expelling it, which suggests Moscow prefers demotion to expulsion. Exit belongs to my downside case.
6. Samvel Karapetyan is still in pre-trial detention, under house arrest or in prison on 12 November 2027: **likely** (~70%). He was arrested in June 2025 and moved to house arrest in December, and on 5 November the Anti-Corruption Court ordered a month's detention in a new case. A deal over the referendum, which his nephew says the government has in mind, is the likeliest way out.

## Drivers
### Political
Civil Contract won 64 of 105 seats on 7 June; Strong Armenia, the party Samvel Karapetyan founded, won 29 and Robert Kocharyan's Armenia Alliance 12. The international observation mission led by ODIHR found a "genuine choice" in a "well-run process", but also direct pressure from abroad through escalating trade restrictions and security threats, and criminal cases mainly against opposition candidates and supporters that led to a perception of selective enforcement.

Azerbaijan wants the Armenian constitution changed to remove what it sees as an implicit territorial claim. Signing is also tied to a ban on foreign forces on the border, aimed mainly at the EU monitoring mission. The draft constitution is promised "by the end of the year". On 29 October Foreign Minister Ararat Mirzoyan said Civil Contract would resign if it failed to secure the votes, but both opposition blocs reject changes they see as imposed by Baku.

Karapetyan was re-arrested on 4 November over alleged losses of more than AMD 64.5 billion at Electric Networks of Armenia, in a case involving 36 people. His nephew Narek, who leads the Strong Armenia faction, was charged in July and bailed. Arsen Gevorgyan, Strong Armenia's candidate in Stepanavan, was arrested on 21 October for alleged vote-buying and died in Nubarashen prison on 10 November; a criminal investigation is open. Civil Contract won 16 of 21 communities in local elections on 25 October, after opposition candidates were arrested in three of them.

Catholicos Karekin II and six senior clerics are charged with defying a court order over the defrocked bishop Gevorg Saroyan and face up to two years in prison; in April Civil Contract called for the Catholicos's removal. In October the finance minister said the government was discussing taxing the Church.

### Security
The border with Azerbaijan is quiet. On 28 October parliament ratified the TRIPP framework agreement with the United States by 63 votes to 32: a joint company, 74% US-owned for an initial 49 years, will develop the route through Syunik linking mainland Azerbaijan with Nakhchivan. The terms for Armenian transit through Azerbaijan are not yet settled.

Pashinyan said on 5 November that Armenia's "interest in the CSTO has run its course", and the Moscow summit on 11 November removed Armenia's votes and its right to fill senior posts. Russia still has two military bases in Armenia with thousands of servicemen and border guards.

### Economic
Economic activity rose 6.9% in January-September 2026, but only 5.0% year on year in September, the lowest since March 2025, with industrial output down 4.5%. The IMF put 2025 growth at 7.2% and expects about 5.25% in 2026; its stand-by arrangement of about $50 million is precautionary and the 2026 deficit target is 4.0% of GDP.

Russian pressure is the main risk. Before the election Russia restricted imports of Armenian flowers, mineral water, alcohol and farm products, and on 27 May it said it would revise the terms on which it supplies gas and oil products. EAEU leaders warned at their May summit in Astana that Armenia could be suspended over its Western-leaning policies. On 4 June the EU pledged more than €50 million in immediate aid and measures to help trade in the affected goods.

### Social
Unemployment is high: 12.9% overall and 26.2% among 15 to 24-year-olds in 2025. More than 115,000 Armenians have been displaced from Karabakh since September 2023. Gevorgyan's death brought Strong Armenia supporters to the EU delegation in Yerevan on 11 November. A committee set up in July has recorded 32 cases of alleged political persecution.

## Indicators to watch
- Publication of the draft constitution and the first parliamentary vote on a referendum.
- Any move to dissolve parliament or set an early election date.
- The court decision on Karapetyan's detention in early December, and the findings of the Gevorgyan investigation.
- Any Russian move to end cheap gas and fuel supplies, or an EAEU decision on Armenia's status.
- New Azerbaijani preconditions, and any shooting on the border.
- A TRIPP groundbreaking date.
- The next hearings in the case against Karekin II, and any EU application.

## Why my rating differs from the model
It does not. The model gives Armenia 51 (Elevated), 56th of 203 countries, with full data coverage and a 0.3-point rise on last year. Since 2015, where the app's series starts, it has ranged from 45 (2019) to 56 (2022). Its two red flags are a current account deficit of 8.2% of GDP for 2026 and youth unemployment of 26.2% in 2025.

The pillars split. Stability (39) and governance (49) look too benign to me: political stability is 58 in the 2025 WGI, against 60 in 2022, and battle deaths were zero in 2024. None of this data covers the events since June, from the opposition leader's re-arrest and the death in custody to the case against the Catholicos and Russia's coercion, and the app's watchlist has no Armenian events. The social pillar (73), driven by youth unemployment, leans the other way, and the 8.2% current account deficit is much wider than the 4.8% the IMF projected in June, so I treat that red flag with caution. The missed political risks and the possibly overstated external gap roughly offset, so I stay with Elevated.

## Implications
Exporters to Russia should expect its restrictions on Armenian goods to stay and should look for other buyers. Energy-intensive firms should plan for a gas price shock. Investors in TRIPP-linked logistics should note that the terms for Armenian transit through Azerbaijan are still open. European governments funding Armenia are backing a government that is prosecuting its main opponents, and should attach rule-of-law conditions to that support.

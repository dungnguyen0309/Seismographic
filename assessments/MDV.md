## Bottom line up front
The Maldives' political risk is Elevated and stable over the next 12 months. Repaying the US$500 million sukuk in April 2026 lowered the risk of default, and debt service falls from about US$1.9 billion this year to US$428 million in 2027 on Moody's figures. The main driver is now domestic: President Mohamed Muizzu lost a referendum in April, his party lost all five city mayoralties, and the main opposition party has voted to take to the streets unless he puts the Eagle Hills Rasmalé deal to a referendum. I would move the outlook to Deteriorating if usable reserves fell below US$150 million or the Rasmalé dispute led to mass arrests of opposition leaders.

## Key judgments
1. Muizzu is **very likely** (~93%) to still be president on 1 November 2027. His People's National Congress (PNC) won 66 of 93 seats in 2024, a November 2024 amendment costs MPs their seats if they change party, and the next presidential election is due in 2028.
2. The government is **very likely** (~88%) to meet every scheduled payment on external sovereign and guaranteed debt through November 2027 without a restructuring announcement. Debt service falls by more than three quarters in 2027, India replaced a US$400 million swap with a rupee facility in April, and Fitch and Moody's both upgraded the country after the sukuk was repaid.
3. Usable reserves are **unlikely** (~25%) to fall below US$150 million at any month-end before November 2027. They stood at US$222.5 million at the end of July, and the 2027 budget raises most of its financing at home, so the path turns on tourism receipts. I hold this with low confidence, since July is the latest month I could confirm.
4. Parliament is **likely** (~65%) to pass the special real estate tourism law that Rasmalé needs before November 2027. Muizzu has promised to submit the bill for about two weeks of public comment, and the PNC majority can pass it; the risk is delay if protests and religious objections grow.
5. The Maldives is **likely** (~70%) to rank lower than 108th of 180 in Reporters Without Borders' 2027 World Press Freedom Index. The 2026 index was out by 30 April, before two journalists from the news site Adhadhu were jailed in May and before the closed qazf trial of its chief executive and editor began.
6. The government is **very unlikely** (~15%) to raise the US$175 million international bond or sukuk planned in the 2027 budget before November 2027. Fitch judged market access prohibitively expensive in June, and the Abu Dhabi Fund, which rolled over a US$100 million private placement to 2031, is the more likely kind of lender.

## Drivers
### Political
Muizzu came to power in 2023 on the back of the "India Out" movement, and his supermajority has reached into other institutions. Parliament removed two of three suspended Supreme Court justices on 14 May 2025, and in September 2025 Muizzu ratified a media law creating a commission that can block news websites and fine journalists.

2026 went badly for him. On 4 April voters rejected his amendment to hold presidential and parliamentary elections together, by 68.7% to 31.3% on about 75% turnout, and the Maldivian Democratic Party (MDP) won all five city mayoral races. Ten of the 20 cabinet ministers resigned on 14 April. After the Adhadhu jailings in May, former presidents Mohamed Nasheed, Abdulla Yameen and Ibrahim Mohamed Solih agreed to discuss working together on press freedom.

The dispute now centres on Rasmalé. On 21 September the government signed an agreement in Dubai with Abu Dhabi developer Eagle Hills for a waterfront and marina project in Rasmalé, a new urban area on reclaimed land in Fushi Dhiggaru lagoon. Eagle Hills puts the investment at about US$12 billion, and properties will be leased for up to 99 years. Muizzu says buyers of any one nationality will be capped at 25% of the properties, with no limit for Maldivians, and Israeli citizens will be barred. A campaign led by former home minister Sheikh Imran Abdulla has called a rally in Malé for 6 November to demand a referendum on the deal. On 1 November the MDP national council resolved, by 57 of the 58 members present, to take to the streets if the government goes ahead without one, and former chairperson Fayyaz Ismail urged members to protest every night until 17 November if no referendum is announced by 15 November. Nasheed, now MDP chairperson, backs the project. On 2 November police arrested Abdulla Shahid, a former foreign minister seeking the MDP's 2028 presidential ticket, on suspicion of sexually abusing a minor; he calls the claims politically motivated.

### Security
There is no armed conflict, and the model records no battle deaths in 2024. The risks are protest policing and religious mobilisation: Sheikh Imran has called on the security forces to join his rally, and the Maldives Media and Broadcasting Commission condemned threats against journalists who went on an Eagle Hills media trip to Dubai. During an October state visit to Pakistan, Muizzu signed 20 memoranda, including on defence and maritime security, and former defence minister Mariya Ahmed Didi wrote that the trip will have strained relations with India. Mauritius suspended diplomatic relations on 27 February, citing the Maldives' refusal to recognise its sovereignty claim over the Chagos archipelago.

### Economic
According to Fitch, the sukuk was repaid with US$350 million from the Sovereign Development Fund and US$175 million from usable reserves. The Maldives Monetary Authority (MMA) repaid the US$400 million Reserve Bank of India swap on 23 April and in the same month drew INR 30 billion under a rupee facility, which Fitch excludes from usable reserves. By June the parallel dollar rate had reached MVR 20.50 against the official peg of 15.42, and banks must sell 90% of their foreign-currency proceeds to the MMA.

The Middle East war shut airspace and cancelled flights from European source markets. Finance Minister Hassan Zareer told parliament on 29 October that the war had slowed growth this year to between 0.6% and 1.8%, and that arrivals would reach only 2.17 million, against 2.4 million projected. GST on foreign tour operators, travel agents and booking platforms took effect on 1 October; three weeks later, after some European operators had suspended bookings, the government proposed repealing it. The 2027 budget projects a deficit of 7.3% of GDP and public debt including guarantees of 124.5% of GDP by the end of 2027; the World Bank expects debt above 140% over the medium term without major consolidation, and banks held sovereign and state-enterprise debt worth 40% of their assets at the end of 2025. China's role is growing: a free trade agreement took effect in January 2025, and Maldivian state institutions and companies signed five agreements with Chinese counterparts at an investment forum in late October.

### Social
Housing feeds the Rasmalé dispute: 58,505 applications have been filed under the "Housing for All" scheme, and Muizzu has promised that Rasmalé will not reduce housing for Maldivians. The religious NGO Jamiyyathul Salaf has warned that large numbers of long-term foreign residents of other faiths could challenge the country's Islamic identity. In October parliament approved pay rises for the speaker, ministers and island councillors. Police have also sent 21 migrant workers for deportation after they protested over unpaid wages at a resort construction site. The model puts youth unemployment at about 16% in 2025.

## Indicators to watch
- Whether Muizzu announces a Rasmalé referendum by 15 November, the deadline Fayyaz Ismail set, and how police handle the 6 November rally and any nightly protests up to 17 November.
- Charges or release for Abdulla Shahid, and any arrests of other opposition leaders.
- MMA usable reserves below US$180 million at a month-end, or the parallel rate above MVR 21.
- The vote on the real estate tourism law, and whether Eagle Hills discloses its own equity and a construction timetable.
- A verdict in the Adhadhu qazf trial, and the Supreme Court ruling on the 2025 media law.
- Monthly tourist arrivals against 2026, and further drawings on the Indian rupee facility.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 48 (Elevated), 73rd of 203 countries. The score rose 4.8 points on last year to the highest since the app's series begins in 2015 (the previous peak was 46 in 2017). All four red flags are economic: thin reserves, debt of 130.7% of GDP, a fiscal deficit of 11.9% of GDP and a current-account deficit of 18.6% of GDP, the last three IMF figures for 2026.

The economic pillar (84) is too pessimistic for the next 12 months, because debt service drops sharply in 2027 once this year's sukuk and swap repayments are made. The reserves figure (1.93 months of imports, for 2025) errs the other way: after the sukuk the Maldives Independent put cover at about 1.4 months.

Stability (22) and governance (58) understate the risk. They rest mostly on Worldwide Governance Indicators for 2025, with political stability at about 67, down from about 70 in 2022, and miss the referendum defeat, the jailing of journalists, the Rasmalé protests and Shahid's arrest. Homicide data are from 2019, and the app's watchlist holds no Maldives events. I read the errors as roughly offsetting, so I keep Elevated with a Stable outlook.

## Implications
Holders of Maldivian sovereign debt face CCC- and Caa1 credit backed by about US$220 million of usable reserves (end of July), and any new bond would price at distressed levels. Firms converting rufiyaa should budget at the June parallel rate of about MVR 20.50 and plan for the 90% surrender rule. Tour operators should expect tax changes at short notice. Buyers and lenders in Rasmalé carry legal and political risk until the special law passes. Media and NGOs face closed trials and court gag orders. Governments should expect India to watch the Pakistan defence memoranda closely.

## Bottom line up front
Political risk in the Central African Republic (CAR) is High and broadly stable. President Faustin-Archange Touadéra began a third term in March 2026 with his party in control of the new institutions, so the government in Bangui faces little threat. The danger is on the borders: armed groups raid Vakaga from Sudan, and the Azande Ani Kpi Gbe (AAKG) militia and the Lord's Resistance Army (LRA) attack civilians in the south-east. The UN mission, MINUSCA, has already sent home about 4,000 troops for lack of funds, and its mandate expires on 15 November 2026. I would move the outlook to Deteriorating if the Security Council cut MINUSCA's aviation and quick-reaction units while fighters from Sudan were still taking border towns.

## Key judgments
1. Touadéra is **very likely** (~90%) to still be president in November 2027. The Constitutional Council confirmed his re-election with about 78% of the vote, his Mouvement des cœurs unis (MCU) and its allies hold the majority in the National Assembly, and Russian paramilitaries and Rwandan troops back him.
2. The Security Council is **likely** (~70%) to set MINUSCA's authorised military strength below the current 14,046 at a renewal before November 2027. Only 10,323 troops were deployed on 1 June, so a lower ceiling would cost little on the ground, and in November 2025 the US abstained after asking for a six-month mandate and about 2,000 fewer troops.
3. Armed groups operating from Sudan are **likely** (~60%) to seize a locality in Vakaga or Haute-Kotto and hold it for at least 48 hours before November 2027. The RSF reportedly set up a base across the border at Umm Dafog in February, and by MINUSCA's account attackers held Am-Dafock for five days from 30 June before withdrawing into Sudan. I hold this with low confidence, because almost all reporting from the north-east comes from the UN mission.
4. The IMF Executive Board is **unlikely** (~35%) to approve a new lending arrangement for CAR before November 2027. The Extended Credit Facility (ECF) agreed in April 2023 ended in June 2026 with reforms unfinished. When reforms slipped in 2021, the Fund started with a seven-month staff-monitored programme and approved the next ECF only in 2023.
5. The UN count of combatants disarmed and demobilised since July 2025 is **likely** (~60%) to pass 3,000 in a report published before November 2027, up from 1,907 in October 2026. It rose by almost 600 between June and October, but liquidity cuts have already shrunk MINUSCA's community violence reduction work.

## Drivers
### Political
On a turnout of just over 52%, the Constitutional Council's final count on 19 January gave Touadéra about 78% against 13.5% for Anicet-Georges Dologuélé, and it rejected his fraud appeal. The main opposition platform, the BRDC, boycotted the race, and in April it restated its objection to the 2023 constitution that removed presidential term limits. Touadéra was sworn in on 30 March for seven years. The MCU won 63 of the 90 seats filled in the first legislative round, and every member elected to the Assembly's bureau on 11 May came from the MCU or its allies. The government of Prime Minister Félix Moloua, named in May, includes people associated with armed groups in the peace process.

The opposition has little room. On 5 May border police at Bangui airport stopped Dologuélé from boarding a flight to Addis Ababa and took his diplomatic passport; his legal adviser said no court order was shown. The electoral cycle is unfinished: by 1 October only 127 of 177 municipalities had installed elected councils, and the UN envoy said lack of money was holding up the remaining polls.

### Security
The UN recorded about as many security incidents in the four months to June 2026 as in the previous period, and most of the country was relatively stable. More than 1,900 combatants have been disarmed since July 2025 under the 2019 peace agreement and the 2025 N'Djamena accords, this year mainly from the UPC and 3R, but in April Touadéra was still urging the UPC, 3R and the Mokom anti-balaka wing to complete their self-dissolution. 3R elements still commit sexual violence and kidnap for ransom in the Yadé region.

The north-east is the most exposed region. Between 26 March and 11 April forces the UN describes only as "other security personnel", working with local self-defence groups, clashed four times with Sudanese armed elements in Vakaga and Haute-Kotto. On 30 June several armed groups attacked Am-Dafock, killing civilians and security personnel, and more than 17,000 people fled to a temporary MINUSCA base. The army adds risk of its own: soldiers in Ouanda Djallé killed three civilians on 27 February, and troops in Am-Dafock fired shots in a protest over unpaid allowances on 12 May. In Haut-Mbomou the AAKG and the LRA, active again there since April, abduct civilians and children; officials the AAKG seized in Bambouti in December 2025 were freed only on 19 October.

Foreign forces carry much of the load. To save money MINUSCA had repatriated 4,031 soldiers and closed 21 bases by 1 June. The UN envoy, Valentine Rugwabiza, told the Council on 20 October that areas where bases closed had not deteriorated significantly, but asked it to keep force levels; the Secretary-General recommended a one-year renewal. Russia has pressed Bangui since early 2025 to swap Wagner for the state-run Africa Corps and pay for it in cash; Bangui reportedly preferred to pay in minerals, and in October African Arguments reported the handover still incomplete.

### Economic
The model records inflation of 3.5%, government debt of 43.5% of GDP and a fiscal deficit of 3.5% of GDP. The IMF projects growth of 2.6% for 2026, down from 4.8% in 2025; the UN puts the slowdown partly down to reduced external finance and says fuel and electricity shortages threaten the recovery. The ECF had paid out $124 million by June 2025, when the Board waived missed targets on the domestic primary balance, domestic financing and new external arrears. The finance minister, Hervé Ndoba, asked for more time to finish the reforms in April 2026, and in October said the programme's halt in June did not weaken the government's commitment to them. France made the last €10 million of a €25 million AFD loan conditional on a favourable fifth IMF review, and in October 2026 the release of that tranche was still under discussion with the French Treasury.

### Social
The IPC found about 2 million people, 31% of the population, in crisis or worse between April and August 2026, including 262,000 in emergency. On 1 June there were 427,479 internally displaced people, 732,977 Central African refugees abroad and 36,114 Sudanese refugees in CAR. The 2026 humanitarian plan was 20.6% funded on 1 June and still less than half funded in October. Between February and May the UN documented 423 violations by state actors and other security personnel, most involving detention, against 254 abuses by armed groups, and the Global Centre for the Responsibility to Protect reports that these abuses fall disproportionately on Muslim and Fulani communities.

## Indicators to watch
- The Security Council vote due before 15 November 2026: the mandate's length, the troop ceiling, and whether quick-reaction and aviation units survive.
- An attack on Birao, Ouanda Djallé or Am-Dafock, or new RSF positions on the Sudanese side of the Vakaga border.
- Army protests over pay or rotation like the one in Am-Dafock on 12 May.
- An announced Africa Corps takeover, a payment deal with Moscow, or a visible cut in Russian personnel.
- An IMF staff-level agreement or staff-monitored programme, and release of the last French budget-support tranche.
- The disarmament count in each UN report, and installation of councils in the remaining 50 municipalities.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 72 (High), 10th of 203 countries, with red flags for active armed conflict and mass displacement. The score fell from 81 (Severe) in 2021 to 69 in 2025, then rose 2.7 points this year; the political stability indicator rose from about 32 in 2022 to about 36 in 2025 on the app's scale.

The governance pillar (92) is the highest, with government effectiveness at 19.3 and control of corruption at 21.3, and nothing in 2026 points to improvement. The stability pillar (76) includes a battle-death rate of 3.32 per 100,000 for 2024, which predates the five-day occupation of Am-Dafock and the LRA's return; homicide data are missing. I think the economic pillar (50) is too kind: the reserves indicator is missing, and the annual data do not show the end of the IMF programme or the held French tranche. The app's watchlist holds no CAR events.

The larger gap is that no indicator measures how far security depends on foreign forces. MINUSCA's 10,323 troops, Russian personnel and Rwandan soldiers hold the gains the score rewards, and MINUSCA is shrinking for lack of money while Moscow wants cash. For that reason I keep the rating at High even though the score has fallen since 2021, and give the downside scenario more weight than the upside.

## Implications
Vakaga and Haut-Mbomou remain unsafe for staff and road travel. Anyone in gold, timber or trading deals should screen for Russian links: the US sanctioned the Wagner-linked timber firm Bois Rouge in September 2023 and Broker Expert in March 2024, and the UK followed in June 2024. The AFD loan was partly meant to reduce the state's arrears on domestic debt service, so domestic creditors should expect slower payment while the last tranche is held. NGOs lose MINUSCA cover wherever bases close. Governments should watch the November vote: a cut to MINUSCA's aviation and quick-reaction capacity would leave the north-east with little means to answer another Am-Dafock.

## Bottom line up front
Mongolia's political risk is Elevated and stable, near the bottom of that band. The country is at peace and a mining boom is filling the budget, but it appointed its third prime minister in nine months in March, petrol queues since August have exposed its reliance on Russian refineries, and inflation was 12.9% in September. The main driver is whether Prime Minister Uchral Nyam-Osor can hold the Mongolian People's Party (MPP) together through the presidential election due in June 2027. A fuel shortage that reached the mines, or Uchral's fall before the election, would move my outlook to Deteriorating.

## Key judgments
1. Uchral is **likely** (~65%) to still be prime minister on 1 November 2027. He leads both the MPP and the government, was seen as a compromise between the party's factions and won 88 of the 107 votes cast, and is constitutionally too young to run for president. Against that, protests forced out Oyun-Erdene and party infighting brought down Zandanshatar after nine months, and a lost presidential race would reopen the leadership question.
2. The MPP's nominee has a **roughly even chance** (~50%) of winning the presidential election due in June 2027. I hold this with low confidence, because no party has chosen a candidate. The MPP can campaign on the 2027 budget's pension rises and income tax cut, but it has changed prime minister twice in nine months.
3. Filling stations in Ulaanbaatar are **likely** (~65%) to cap fuel sales per motorist again before November 2027. Russia supplies up to 97% of Mongolia's petrol and diesel imports, AI-92 stocks covered about 10 days of demand on 29 September, and the first domestic refinery is due only in 2028. A senior official told the government's fuel task force in September that the disruption could last two to three years.
4. Annual inflation is **likely** (~80%) to be above 7%, the top of the Bank of Mongolia's (BoM) 2027 target band, in the September 2027 reading. The IMF projects about 8.5% at the end of 2027, with fiscal expansion and rising wages keeping inflation above target through the year. The BoM is more hopeful: in August it expected a return to the band from mid-2027.
5. Oyu Tolgoi is **likely** (~75%) to pay its first dividend to the state's holding company, Erdenes Oyu Tolgoi, before November 2027. Rio Tinto and the government agreed in September to start distributions in 2027, and the draft budget counts on about MNT 3 trillion. A fall in copper prices, or objections in parliament like those that made the cabinet withdraw its spending bill on 23 October, could delay it.
6. A protest or dispute is **very unlikely** (~10%) to halt Oyu Tolgoi's concentrate exports for more than seven consecutive days before November 2027. The June blockade was brief and police cleared the road, and Oyu Tolgoi estimates that each lost week would cost the state about 35 billion tugrik in taxes and royalties.

## Drivers
### Political
Prime Minister Oyun-Erdene Luvsannamsrai resigned in June 2025 after weeks of anti-corruption protests by young people in Ulaanbaatar. His successor, Zandanshatar Gombojav, lost the MPP leadership to the speaker, Amarbayasgalan Dashzegve, whom Zandanshatar's supporters accused of corruption in the coal industry. Parliament voted to remove Zandanshatar on 17 October 2025, yet he stayed on, and in November Uchral took over as party leader and speaker.

In March 2026 the Democratic Party (DP) boycotted parliament over the concentration of power in the MPP, an MPP faction joined the boycott, and Justice Minister Enkhbayar Battumur, a Zandanshatar ally, faced corruption allegations. With too few members attending to hold votes, Zandanshatar resigned on 27 March. Uchral, confirmed on 30 March, was a compromise between the faction loyal to President Khurelsukh Ukhnaa and Oyun-Erdene's supporters. His cabinet has 16 MPP ministers, two from the HUN Party and one from the National Coalition.

Khurelsukh cannot seek a second term. Mongolia Weekly expected nominations to open in spring 2027 and reported in February that some saw Zandanshatar as the MPP's frontrunner.

### Security
Mongolia has no armed conflict: the model records no battle deaths in 2024 and 5.92 homicides per 100,000 people in 2023. The risks are disruption and protest. On 17 June 2026 the Radical Reform Movement, which wants a larger state share of Oyu Tolgoi's returns, blocked the mine's export road to China until police cleared it. On 1 October, after about 50 days of fuel queues, motorists marched towards parliament in Ulaanbaatar.

### Economic
Mining carries the economy. Growth was 7.7% in the first half of 2026, and the IMF expects 6.2% for the year, with weaker growth outside mining. The BoM credits higher coal and copper prices and export volumes for a balance-of-payments surplus, and gross reserves reached $8.9 billion at the end of September. All of Oyu Tolgoi's concentrate goes to China, to smelters in Inner Mongolia and Gansu.

Inflation rose from 11.2% in May to 13.0% in July on fuel and food, and was 12.9% in September. The BoM raised its policy rate to 12.5% at an unscheduled meeting on 12 August and held it in September. Its target falls to 5±2% from 2027, down from 6±2%.

Fiscal policy is loosening before the election. Spending rose 20% year on year to August. The 2027 draft budget, still before parliament in late October, plans MNT 43.6 trillion of spending against MNT 41.3 trillion of revenue, exempts monthly wages up to MNT 792,000 from income tax and raises basic pensions. On 8 October the IMF warned that the plans would raise deficits and debt and that draft laws would weaken the fiscal rules.

Oyu Tolgoi is 66% owned by Rio Tinto and 34% by the state, and should average about 500,000 tonnes of copper a year from 2028 to 2036. In September the partners agreed to cut shareholder loan interest and management fees, which is expected to add about MNT 13 trillion to Mongolia's returns, and to start dividends in 2027 instead of 2037. On 23 October the cabinet withdrew its bill on spending the dividend after six standing committees raised 235 objections.

Fuel is the main external exposure. Ukrainian drone strikes damaged Russian refineries through the summer and Russian deliveries fell in July, although Moscow's export ban exempts Mongolia's intergovernmental contracts. Extra cargoes have come from China and South Korea, but Chinese rail deliveries are slowed by the change of gauge at the border. AI-92 prices are held down under a price-stabilisation agreement.

### Social
Corruption is the main grievance. Control of corruption (about 39 in 2025) is the model's weakest governance indicator, and the 2025 protesters were angry at wealthy elites as well as graft. Teachers struck over pay in October 2025, and the budget's public wage and pension rises respond to that pressure. Unemployment is low (5.23% in 2025, 11.71% for young people), so the strain on households comes from food and fuel prices.

## Indicators to watch
- AI-92 stocks fall below 10 days of demand or purchase caps return, and whether Russia extends its export ban beyond 31 January 2027.
- Annual inflation above 13% in any monthly reading, or a BoM rate rise above 12.5%.
- The final 2027 budget, and any change to the fiscal rules.
- The date and size of Oyu Tolgoi's first dividend, and any new bill on spending it.
- New blockades of the Oyu Tolgoi export road or of coal routes to China.
- MPP and DP presidential nominations in spring 2027, and whether Zandanshatar runs.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 42 (Elevated), 110th of 203 countries, with no red flags and full data coverage. The score fell 0.3 points on last year and is down from 50 in 2021; its lowest point since 2015 was 41 in 2023. Governance (52) is the riskiest pillar, as I would expect, with control of corruption at about 39 and government effectiveness at about 48 in 2025; stability (35) and social (36) reflect a country without violence or mass unemployment.

The model has no measure of fuel import dependence or of export concentration on China. The political stability indicator barely moved between 2022 and 2025 (from 76.43 to 75.54 on the app's scale), and its latest year predates Uchral's appointment and the fuel crisis. In the economic pillar (47), the 11.0% inflation figure for 2026 is an IMF estimate of the annual average, below the 12.9% recorded in September, and the 2026 figures for the fiscal deficit (2.5% of GDP) and debt (44.1%) do not show the 2027 expansion the IMF now expects. The reserves figure (4.15 months of imports) is for 2025, and the app's watchlist holds no Mongolia events. I read these misses as small, so I keep Elevated with a Stable outlook, though the score is only 2 points above the Moderate line and the 2027 fiscal data could push it up.

## Implications
Mining companies should expect revenue sharing to stay in politics through the election despite the Oyu Tolgoi deal, given the 235 objections in parliament and the June blockade. Operators in the Gobi should hold their own diesel stocks and plan for export roads closed for a day or more. Lenders should watch the final 2027 budget, since the IMF warns that public wage rises could feed a wage-inflation spiral. For partner governments, Mongolia's fuel supply beyond Russia runs through Chinese rail transfers and South Korean cargoes, while China remains the main market for its minerals.

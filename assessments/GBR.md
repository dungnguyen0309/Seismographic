## Bottom line up front
I rate the United Kingdom's political risk Moderate and stable over the next 12 months, the same tier as the model (34). The Labour crisis that ended Keir Starmer's premiership has left Andy Burnham in Downing Street with a large majority, a small poll lead and no election due before August 2029. The risk has moved to the economy: the energy shock from the war with Iran has pushed the Bank of England into raising rates while the margin against the fiscal rules has shrunk. Inflation near 5% with Labour back behind its rivals, or a forced change to the fiscal rules, would make me consider Elevated.

## Key judgments
1. Andy Burnham is prime minister on 12 November 2027: **very likely** (~85%). He was declared Labour leader unopposed on 17 July with over 94% of MP nominations, and a Lord Ashcroft poll published on 10 November gave him a net job rating of +15, against -42 for Nigel Farage.
2. A UK general election is held before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~10%). None is required until August 2029, and with inflation forecast to peak in mid-2027 Burnham has little reason to call one early.
3. Labour leads in most voting intention polls published in October 2027: **likely** (~60%). Labour led all 15 polls with fieldwork between 25 October and 12 November, by 2 to 9 points, but a lead that narrow can disappear during a squeeze on real incomes.
4. Annual CPI inflation reaches 4.5% or more in at least one ONS release before November 2027: **likely** (~70%). The Bank expects about 4.5% in the first quarter of 2027 and around 5% by mid-year. It would take a quick reopening of Gulf shipping and a sharp fall in oil to keep it lower.
5. The government still uses the fiscal rules set in October 2024, without a formal change, on 12 November 2027: **likely** (~70%). Burnham committed in June to the existing borrowing limits. The weak point is the stability rule, whose forecast 2029/30 surplus of £15 billion could vanish if debt interest keeps rising, and Labour's 2024 pledge not to raise taxes on working people leaves few easy ways to refill it.
6. An attack in the UK that kills at least one person and that UK or US authorities attribute to Iran or a group it backs takes place before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~10%). The plots so far were disrupted or non-fatal. Confidence here is low.

## Drivers
### Political
On 7 May Labour lost about 1,500 councillors and 38 councils in England, while Reform UK won about 1,450 seats and 14 councils. In Wales, Plaid Cymru won 43 of the Senedd's 96 seats and Reform 34, with Labour down to nine; First Minister Eluned Morgan lost her seat and Plaid's Rhun ap Iorwerth replaced her. In Scotland the SNP won a fifth term with 58 of 129 seats.

About 70 Labour MPs had called on Starmer to go by 11 May. Burnham won the Makerfield by-election on 18 June with a majority of more than 9,000 over Reform, Starmer announced his resignation four days later and Burnham took office on 20 July. Labour then held Starmer's Holborn and St Pancras seat on 8 October with 45.2% against 32.8% for the Green leader, Zack Polanski.

The right is split. In July Ashcroft had Reform, the Conservatives and Labour level on 21%; in November he had Labour on 31%, the Conservatives on 26% and Reform on 18%. Rupert Lowe's Restore Britain competes for Reform's voters: in July nearly four in ten people who voted Reform in 2024 told Ashcroft they liked much of what it stands for. Burnham has made Europe a central issue. He wants a closer relationship with the EU, a UK-EU summit is set for 20 November, and 53% of Ashcroft's sample would vote to rejoin against 29%.

### Security
The US and Israeli war on Iran, which began on 28 February, has reached Britain, which let US bombers use RAF Fairford. Police arrested seven men after a farmer reported suspicious activity near the base, and in October the US moved all 12 of its B-1 bombers out. On 11 November US prosecutors charged three men they call Iranian-backed operatives over nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks in the US and Europe, including the Fairford plot and the non-fatal stabbing of two Jewish men in London in April; Iran denies involvement at Fairford. On 12 November a London court convicted two Iranian men of conduct likely to assist Iran's intelligence service; their targets included Iran International journalists.

On 13 October Argentina asked the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea to halt the Sea Lion oil project near the Falkland Islands, and the tribunal heard the request on 10 and 11 November. The UK says the islands' sovereignty is not up for negotiation. Argentina disputes it and says the resources belong to its people.

### Economic
GDP rose 0.7% in the third quarter. Inflation is the problem: CPI reached 3.5% in September, Brent averaged $108 a barrel in the 15 working days to 27 October, and on 5 November the Bank raised its rate to 4% by eight votes to one. Chancellor John Healey's first budget on 28 October was cautious, with a £100 payment to households in council tax bands A to D and an end to standing charges on gas bills. The OBR sees net financial liabilities peaking at 83.8% of GDP in 2027/28.

### Social
Net migration fell to a provisional 171,000 in 2025 from a peak of 944,000 in the year to March 2023, but small boats still arrive: 1,670 people crossed in the week to 1 November. On 2 November Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood set a default ten-year wait for settlement, and up to 30 years for small-boat arrivals.

## Indicators to watch
- The outcome of the UK-EU summit on 20 November.
- The Bank's decision on 17 December and the monthly CPI path towards the mid-2027 peak.
- Headroom against the stability rule in the OBR's spring forecast.
- Labour's lead in the polling average, and Reform and Restore Britain's combined share.
- Brent crude and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.
- Sentencing of the two Iranian men on 17 December, and any new arrests under the National Security Act.
- The tribunal's ruling on Argentina's request over Sea Lion.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 34 (Moderate), 148th of 203 countries, with full data coverage. The score has stayed between 29 and 35 in every year of the app's history, which starts in 2015, and fell 1.6 points this year from its 2025 peak.

The economic pillar (70) drives the score, through public debt of 102% of GDP in 2026 and reserves of 1.39 months of imports in 2025. The thin-reserves flag means little for a country with a floating currency that borrows in sterling. Stability (25), governance (10) and social (34) sit low, and political stability only slipped from about 72 in 2022 to about 71 in 2025.

The data miss this year's events. Governance scores stop at 2025, a year before the prime minister was forced out, no indicator records the Iran-linked plots, and the IMF's 3.3% inflation for 2026 is below the Bank's 5% for mid-2027. Against that, the leadership change was orderly and the new government leads the polls. These gaps add a few points, short of the six or so needed for Elevated, so I keep Moderate and Stable.

## Implications
Investors should expect higher rates through 2027. Employers who sponsor foreign workers face longer settlement routes for staff, and 1,800 have lost the right to sponsor. Companies tied to Falklands oil face Argentine President Javier Milei's threat to bar them from Argentina's oil and mining sectors.

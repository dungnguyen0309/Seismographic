## Bottom line up front
Spain's political risk is Moderate and stable, although the government will probably change. Pedro Sánchez called a snap election for 29 November after Congress rejected his housing decrees, and early November polls give the Popular Party (PP) and Vox about 190 to 200 of 350 seats. The risks are a slow investiture, anger over housing that produced the first general strike since 2012 on 11 November, and a government that depends on Vox. A hung parliament heading for a repeat election, or sustained unrest under a new government, would move me to Elevated.

## Key judgments
1. Alberto Núñez Feijóo is **likely** (~75%) to be prime minister on 12 November 2027. Ateneo del Dato's poll for elDiario.es (2 to 5 November) had the PP and Vox on 50.1% against 36.2% for the PSOE, Frente Amplio and Podemos, and SocioMétrica, 40dB and Sigma Dos gave the right about 190 to 200 seats. The PSOE gained a point after the transport minister resigned, so I stop short of very likely.
2. There is a **roughly even chance** (~50%) that Vox holds at least one ministry on 12 November 2027. Vox polled 18.6% in the same elDiario.es survey, and after early regional elections in Extremadura and Aragon the PP's leaders there had to meet Santiago Abascal's conditions to be invested. Feijóo may still prefer a pact that keeps Vox out of the cabinet.
3. Spain is **very unlikely** (~10%) to hold a repeat general election before 12 November 2027. That would need the investiture to fail, as in 2019, when Spain voted in April and again in November. Polls show a clear majority for the right.
4. The Cortes are **unlikely** (~35%) to approve a new general state budget before 12 November 2027. The government has not passed one since 2023. A PP government invested early in 2027 could pass one with Vox votes, but it would need months to draft it and Vox would set a price, so in my base case the bill reaches parliament only late in the year.
5. CCOO and UGT are **unlikely** (~25%) to call another general strike before 12 November 2027. The 11 November stoppage was only the eighth since the return to democracy, though a PP government that dropped tenant protections could provoke another.

## Drivers
### Political
Sánchez has been prime minister since 2018 and has led a minority government through three terms. On 2 October the housing bills, which would have renewed leases automatically and restricted evictions, failed by 172 votes to 178 and 166 to 184 when Junts, whose seven deputies the coalition relied on, voted with the PP and Vox. The cabinet approved similar measures on 6 October. With the Cortes dissolved, they need ratification by the 69-member standing committee, where the government planned a vote on 12 November.

In June the Supreme Court sentenced former minister José Luis Ábalos to 24 years and 3 months in prison and his adviser Koldo García to 19 years and 8 months in the face masks case. In July a Madrid court confirmed a jury trial for Begoña Gómez, the prime minister's wife, for influence peddling and embezzlement, which she denies; that week his brother David Sánchez was banned from public office for nine years for administrative misconduct. Former prime minister José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero is under investigation over the €53 million rescue of the airline Plus Ultra.

On 28 October a train collision near Urda, in Toledo province, killed 69 people, months after a crash near Adamuz killed 46 on the same Madrid-Seville line in January. Transport Minister Óscar Puente resigned on 3 November, and by 6 November the European Public Prosecutor's Office had opened a fraud inquiry into EU funds for the line. In Catalonia, Supreme Court judge Pablo Llarena revoked the national arrest warrant for Carles Puigdemont in early October, which removes one of the main obstacles to his return.

### Security
Battle deaths were zero in 2024 and the homicide rate was 0.69 per 100,000 in 2023; the risks are at the border and in the streets. About 80,000 people entered Ceuta in late July, and Italy responded with checks on non-EU travellers arriving from Spain. By 10 November 14,818 had gone back to Morocco and about 5,800 remained in temporary facilities. The general strike ended with more than 70 arrests. Infrastructure is a weak point: ENTSO-E's experts found in March that the April 2025 blackout followed gaps in voltage control and cascading generator disconnections.

### Economic
Growth is strong: INE estimates GDP grew 0.7% in the second quarter and 2.6% year on year. Unemployment was 10.18% in the third quarter, up 0.31 points on the quarter, while employment rose by 465,500 in a year. The model has 2026 public debt at 97.6% of GDP and a deficit of 2.4%.

### Social
Rents have almost doubled in a decade, and the average rent of €1,176 equals 98.7% of a young person's average pay, according to Spain's Youth Council. The central bank puts the housing shortfall at about 750,000 homes. Anger grew after Maricarmen Abascal, 87, was evicted in Madrid in September and later died. Youth unemployment was 24.75% in 2025.

## Indicators to watch
- PP and Vox seats on 29 November, above or below the 176 needed for a majority.
- Whether the standing committee ratifies the re-approved housing decrees.
- The investiture timetable and any demand by Vox for ministries.
- A rail strike at ADIF, which its works council has agreed to push for on 17 November.
- A date for Begoña Gómez's jury trial.
- Puigdemont's return to Spain.
- Unemployment above 11% in INE's quarterly survey.
- A new mass crossing into Ceuta or Melilla.

## Implications
Property investors should expect a PP government to drop the eviction limits and favour incentives to build, since the PP and Vox oppose wider intervention in housing; tenants' groups and unions would protest. Firms that rely on high-speed rail should plan for strikes at ADIF and disruption while the Urda inquiry runs. Javier Carbonell of the European Policy Centre expects a PP and Vox government to take a tougher line on migration and spend more on defence, which would bring Spain closer to the European right.

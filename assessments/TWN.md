## Bottom line up front
Taiwan's political risk is Moderate and stable over the next 12 months, one tier above the model. At home the risk is gridlock: clashes between the government and the legislature's Kuomintang (KMT) and Taiwan People's Party (TPP) majority delayed the 2026 budget by a record 266 days. The larger exposure is Beijing, whose coast guard has patrolled off the east coast since June. I would move to Elevated if it stopped a ship bound for a Taiwanese port.

## Key judgments
1. The People's Liberation Army (PLA) has a **roughly even chance** (~50%) of holding a named large-scale exercise around Taiwan before November 2027. It held at least one a year from 2022 to 2025 but none so far in 2026, a year of US-China summits. A new US arms sale is the likeliest trigger.
2. The China Coast Guard is **very unlikely** (~15%) to board, inspect or turn back a merchant ship sailing to or from a port on Taiwan's main island before November 2027. Its east coast patrols, which Taiwanese officials see as blockade rehearsal, have so far only radioed ships.
3. Washington is **likely** (~60%) to notify Congress of an arms sale to Taiwan worth more than US$1 billion before November 2027. A US$14 billion package has stalled, but Brookings' Ryan Hass expects smaller packages after this month's APEC summit and the G20 in Miami.
4. The legislature is **likely** (~65%) to pass the 2027 central government budget before 31 March 2027. The bill reached committee on 27 October, but disputes over cash handouts and over opposition laws the premier will not enact could stall it again.
5. Puma Shen of the ruling Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) is **unlikely** (~40%) to win the Taipei mayoralty on 28 November. Two October polls put him ahead, but TVBS had the KMT incumbent Chiang Wan-an eight points up, and the DPP last won the capital in 1994.
6. Exports for January to September 2027 are **likely** (~60%) to exceed the US$661.55 billion of those months in 2026. AI demand is still rising, but Taiwanese server makers are moving assembly to the US and Mexico, and US chip tariffs are undecided.

## Drivers
### Political
President Lai Ching-te won in 2024, but his DPP lost control of the 113-seat legislature, where the KMT and TPP hold a majority. Recall votes against 31 KMT legislators failed in July and August 2025. Lai's approval stood at 51.4% in a September 2026 poll by the Taipei Trust in Democracy Association, its highest since April 2025.

Lawmakers voted 56 to 50 to impeach Lai on 19 May, short of the 76 needed, after he declined to promulgate a law giving local governments a larger share of tax revenue. In December 2025 Premier Cho Jung-tai said he would not enact local government spending plans the opposition had passed, calling them fiscally unsound. The Constitutional Court has sat with eight of its 15 justices since October 2024, and three of them have refused to deliberate for more than 300 days. KMT chair Cheng Li-wun met Xi Jinping in Beijing in April, and The Wire China expects a poor KMT result in the 28 November elections for 11,051 local posts to end her presidential hopes.

### Security
Beijing considers Taiwan part of its territory; Taiwan's coast guard says China has "absolutely no jurisdiction" over its waters. In December 2025, after Washington approved US$11.1 billion in arms sales, the PLA held a drill that simulated a blockade of Keelung and Kaohsiung.

Since June the China Coast Guard has run "law enforcement patrols", usually 74 to 124 nautical miles off the east coast, in what Taipei calls its exclusive economic zone, and has radioed cargo ships for crew and destination details. In the 24 hours to 6am on 3 November the defence ministry tracked 49 PLA aircraft and six warships. It is watching for a large drill in the PLA's November to December peak season.

Washington is less predictable: after visiting Beijing in May, Trump said "I'm not looking to have somebody go independent". That month the legislature cut Lai's NT$1.25 trillion special defence budget to NT$780 billion, tying NT$480 billion of it to US arms not yet offered. The Cabinet's 2027 plan puts defence at 3.01% of GDP.

### Economic
GDP grew 14.36% year on year in the third quarter by preliminary figures. In August the statistics agency (DGBAS) forecast 11.05% for 2026, the fastest since 1987, and 6.04% for 2027. Exports set a record of US$87.22 billion in September.

Trade policy is the main external risk. In January Taiwanese firms pledged US$250 billion of US investment in return for a 15% tariff cap and chip-tariff quotas. The Agreement on Reciprocal Trade was signed on 12 February, but in April the TPP said it had yet to be sent to the legislature. Since 24 July a Section 301 action has set a 10% floor on combined US duties on Taiwanese goods, and new Section 232 chip tariffs and a Section 301 excess-capacity case covering Taiwan were still pending in mid-October.

### Social
Births fell to a record low of 107,812 in 2025, and 20.06% of people are 65 or older. The Cabinet's 2027 budget sets aside NT$235.7 billion for a NT$10,000 handout to each eligible person, which it says higher tax revenue from technology industries will pay for; a KMT bill would make it NT$20,000. The 28 November ballot includes a referendum on ending the "nuclear-free homeland" policy, after an August 2025 vote to restart the Maanshan plant won 74% support but missed the turnout threshold.

## Indicators to watch
- A China Coast Guard demand to board, inspect or redirect a merchant ship east of Taiwan.
- A named PLA drill, especially one with live-fire zones near Keelung or Kaohsiung.
- Trump's decision on the US$14 billion package, or a US statement opposing Taiwan independence.
- The 28 November results in Taipei, New Taipei and Taichung.
- The 2027 budget vote, and any new refusal by the premier to enact a law.
- New US chip tariffs, or exports falling year on year after 35 straight months of growth.

## Why my rating differs from the model
The model scores Taiwan 11 (Low), 203rd of 203 countries and the lowest risk in the ranking, down 1.2 points on last year to its lowest since 2020, with no red flags. The score was 15 in 2015, peaked at 17 in 2016 and has fallen each year since 2023. I rate Taiwan one tier higher.

The stability pillar (10) rests on zero battle deaths (2024), zero displacement (2025) and a political stability indicator of about 83 in 2025, little changed since 2022. Coercion that kills no one does not register, so neither the patrols nor the December 2025 drill moves it. The governance pillar (13), built from 2025 scores between 72 and 83, shows little of the stalled court. The economic pillar (8) captures the boom but not the tariff or AI-demand risk behind it. Homicide, reserves and youth unemployment data are missing (coverage is 82%), and the app's watchlist holds no Taiwan events.

## Implications
Shippers and insurers should plan for coast guard radio calls east of Taiwan and for drill closures near Keelung and Kaohsiung; Taiwan expected December's drill to affect more than 100,000 passengers on international flights. Exporters face combined US duties of at least 10% under the July action, and firms in the January investment deal are best placed for chip-tariff exemptions. Governments should watch the APEC summit in Shenzhen on 18 and 19 November, where Taipei has protested at how Beijing delivered its invitation.

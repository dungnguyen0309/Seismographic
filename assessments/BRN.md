## Bottom line up front
Brunei's political risk is Moderate, at the low end of the band, and stable. The state has savings and almost no debt, but growth has stalled, and the IMF puts this year's deficit at 12.7% of GDP in a year when the US Energy Information Administration (EIA) expects Brent to average $96. The main risk is succession: Sultan Hassanal Bolkiah, 80, had a knee replacement in January and still holds the premiership, defence and finance. A succession coinciding with Brent below $70 would move me to Elevated.

## Key judgments
1. Hassanal Bolkiah is **very likely** (~90%) to still be sultan on 1 November 2027. He was hospitalised for fatigue at the ASEAN summit in Kuala Lumpur in May 2025, but since March he has opened the Legislative Council, attended the ASEAN summit in Cebu and appeared at a ceremony for his and the queen's 80th birthdays on 31 October. Confidence is moderate: little is published about his health.
2. The sultan is **very unlikely** (~12%) to give up the premiership, finance or defence before November 2027. He kept all three in the June reshuffle and left Crown Prince Al-Muhtadee Billah, 52, as Senior Minister. The Diamond Jubilee of his reign in 2027 is the likeliest moment for a change.
3. The first full-year estimate from the Department of Economic Planning and Statistics (DEPS) is **likely** (~70%) to put 2026 real growth below 1.5%, the bottom of the range of forecasts from financial institutions (1.5% to 2.4%) reported in March. Growth was 0.3% year on year in the first quarter and 0.02% in the second, and the fourth quarter faces a high base: 4.8% growth a year earlier.
4. The 2027/28 supply bill, due in March 2027, is **likely** (~70%) to total B$6.0 billion to B$6.6 billion, near the B$6.35 billion of 2025/26 and B$6.3 billion of 2026/27. Salaries and recurrent spending take three-quarters of the budget, so a deeper cut would probably need an oil price fall.
5. There is **almost no chance** (~3%) that Brunei carries out an execution, under the Penal Code or the Syariah Penal Code Order (SPCO), before November 2027. Amnesty International records none since 1996, and in October it classed Brunei as abolitionist in practice.

## Drivers
### Political
Brunei is an absolute monarchy with no elected representatives at national level, and the sultan has reigned since 1967. In his first major reshuffle since 2022, on 4 June, he created three coordinating ministers. Prince Abdul Mateen, 34, took the foreign ministry, which the sultan had held since 2015, and Prince Abdul Malik, 42, became a minister at the Prime Minister's Office. Reuters read this as possible succession planning. Power stays with the sultan, so his health is the main uncertainty: he made no public appearance between his knee operation in January and 12 March.

### Security
Brunei has no armed conflict; its exposure is to the Iran war. The foreign ministry called the US-Israeli strikes on Iran of 28 February a serious violation of sovereignty, and Royal Brunei Airlines suspended flights to Jeddah, Dubai and London. A national committee on the war's impact, set up in June, has since 9 July covered any crisis or external shock. In July the sultan said the government's priority was keeping strategic reserves of fuel and LPG.

### Economic
Oil and gas made up 54.1% of GDP at current prices in the second quarter, though the sultan said in July that the non-oil sector now provides more than half of GDP. In 2023 oil and gas provided about three-quarters of central government revenue. Second-quarter output barely moved: oil and gas shrank 0.2% during scheduled maintenance, and non-oil grew 0.2% after a 3.0% fall in the first quarter. Opening the Legislative Council in March, with oil above $100, the sultan said "the need to develop new areas is no longer a choice but a necessity".

The 2026/27 budget is B$6.3 billion, including B$2.31 billion for emoluments, B$2.45 billion for recurrent spending and B$480 million for projects under the 12th National Development Plan (RKN 12). The IMF puts the deficit at 17.7% of GDP in 2025 and 13.7% in 2027. With debt at 1.3% of GDP, savings cover the gap, though Anthony Tan and Poh Lynn Ng note that persistent deficits have drawn reserves down. Reuters reported rising subsidy costs, and since 1 April foreign-registered vehicles may buy only unsubsidised fuel. The EIA expects Brent to fall from $105 in the fourth quarter to $84 in 2027.

### Social
The model puts youth unemployment at 18.2% (2025). Press and assembly are tightly restricted. The SPCO took effect on 3 April 2019; it provides stoning for anal intercourse, whatever the religion of those accused, and amputation for theft. In May 2019 the sultan extended the moratorium on executions to the SPCO, but Amnesty found no sign that it covers amputation or caning. Brunei has consistently voted against UN General Assembly calls for a moratorium.

## Indicators to watch
- The sultan misses public events for over a month, is treated abroad, or a new procedure is announced.
- Any transfer of the premiership, finance or defence, especially around the Diamond Jubilee.
- Brent below $70 or above $110 for a month.
- The 2027/28 supply bill against B$6.3 billion.
- DEPS growth data for the second half of 2026.
- Any reported SPCO sentence of stoning or amputation.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 22 (Moderate), 188th of 203 countries, with one red flag for a fiscal deficit of 5% of GDP or more. The score fell 0.8 points on last year and has been Moderate every year since 2015, between 22 and 28. Coverage is 94%; homicide data are missing.

Stability, at 1, is the lowest pillar: no battle deaths (2024), WGI political stability up from about 86 in 2022 to about 92 in 2025, and no watchlist events. Governance (31) is held down by voice and accountability at 40, against 68 to 72 for the other governance indicators (2025).

The model errs in both directions. No indicator measures the risk of an 80-year-old ruler holding three main portfolios, and the 1.9% growth figure for 2026 looks high against a first half near zero. Against that, the fiscal red flag overstates the near-term danger: debt is 1.3% of GDP, the current-account surplus 23.7% (both 2026) and reserves 7.9 months of imports (2025). Neither changes the tier.

## Implications
Government suppliers face little payment risk while savings last; a price slump would show first in the 68 RKN 12 projects that were out to tender in March. Fuel traders should expect tighter controls if Brent stays high. Brands and employers with LGBT staff carry reputational exposure from the SPCO.

## Bottom line up front
Afghanistan's political risk is High and rising. The Taliban face no serious challenge at home, but they have fought Pakistan on and off since October 2025, and the fighting escalated after an attack in Kohat on 18 September, with Pakistani air strikes in five provinces and ground clashes up to 1 November. Aid is shrinking as needs grow: the 2026 humanitarian plan was 30% funded at the end of August, and 1.37 million people have returned to Afghanistan this year, nearly all from Iran and Pakistan. I would move the outlook to Stable if Islamabad and Kabul signed a deal with a monitoring mechanism and Torkham reopened to cargo.

## Key judgments
1. Hibatullah Akhundzada is **very likely** (~90%) to still be the Taliban's supreme leader on 1 November 2027, with no provincial capital lost to armed opponents. Armed opposition shows up as small cross-border incursions, and the ICC has no way to enforce the warrant against him that it made public on 5 October while the Taliban hold the country.
2. Pakistan and the Taliban are **very unlikely** (~15%) to sign a written settlement followed by six consecutive months without Pakistani air or drone strikes inside Afghanistan before November 2027. The Doha ceasefire of October 2025 broke down after talks in Istanbul collapsed the next month, China hosted talks in Urumqi in April but Pakistan struck again in June, and the Moscow Format meeting on 5 October produced no joint statement because the Taliban objected to wording on terrorism.
3. Pakistan is **unlikely** (~30%) to strike targets in Kabul city before November 2027. An air strike hit a drug rehabilitation hospital in Kabul on 17 March, but the strikes since September have been reported in Kunar, Paktia, Khost, Kandahar and Helmand. A strike on the capital would most likely follow a mass-casualty attack in Pakistan like the Islamabad mosque bombing of 6 February.
4. IOM and UNHCR are **likely** (~60%) to count more than 1 million returns to Afghanistan in 2027, after 1,366,140 in 2026 by 24 October. At the end of June, 1.55 million Afghan refugees and others in need of protection were still in Iran and 1.07 million in Pakistan. I hold this with low confidence, since it turns on Iranian and Pakistani deportation policy.
5. The 2027 humanitarian plan is **likely** (~75%) to be less than 40% funded at the end of August 2027 on OCHA's figures. The 2026 plan had received $513 million of $1.71 billion, or 30%, by the end of August 2026.
6. The afghani is **likely** (~75%) to trade between 60 and 75 to the dollar on Da Afghanistan Bank's rate on 1 November 2027, against 65.2 on 4 November 2026. The pressure comes from a current-account deficit the World Bank put at 36.1% of GDP in 2025, and from inflation, which rose to 7.6% by March 2026.

## Drivers
### Political
Russia recognised the Taliban government on 3 July 2025 and signed an unpublished military-technical agreement with it in May 2026. In early November France's foreign minister, Jean-Noël Barrot, said France has "no intention" of recognising the Taliban and makes better conditions for women and girls a precondition, though France holds technical talks with the Taliban on returning Afghans. On 5 October the International Criminal Court unsealed warrants, issued in 2025, for Akhundzada, chief justice Abdul Hakim Haqqani, education minister Habibullah Agha and higher education minister Neda Mohammad Nadim, on charges of gender persecution. UNAMA recorded at least 443 arbitrary arrests from July to September, mostly over morality rules, and the public flogging of 28 people.

Kabul accuses Pakistan of building an armed opposition. Islamabad hosted anti-Taliban figures at a conference in late September, and the Taliban say most of the 28 fighters they killed in Nuristan on 27 September were former Afghan security personnel recruited by Pakistani intelligence. Pakistan denies this.

### Security
After an Islamic State Khorasan Province (ISKP) suicide bombing killed at least 31 people at a mosque in Islamabad on 6 February 2026, Pakistan struck Nangarhar and Paktika on 22 February, and the two sides fought their worst battles in years. The current round began after three months of relative calm, with the 18 September attack in Kohat, which killed 31 people and was claimed by Ittehad ul Mujahideen Pakistan, an alliance of factions with roots in the Pakistani Taliban (TTP). Pakistan struck Afghanistan on 21 and 24 September and hit Kunar and Helmand on 30 September, where UNAMA said 10 civilians, most of them children, were killed. Ground clashes followed in Kurram on 10 October, in Paktia on 17 October and at Torkham in late October, and on 1 November a rocket attack the Taliban blamed on Pakistan killed a man in Paktika. UNAMA counted more than 1,700 civilian deaths and injuries from cross-border violence between October 2025 and September 2026.

China, Turkey, Qatar and Saudi Arabia have all tried to mediate, and Russia says it will help only if both sides ask. Islamabad wants "credible and verifiable action" against armed groups operating from Afghan territory, and Kabul denies sheltering the TTP. Violence inside Pakistan keeps up the pressure on Islamabad: the think tank CRSS counted 1,415 deaths from July to September, and on 11 October the federal government declared an emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, accusing the PTI-led provincial government of withdrawing from counterterrorism.

Inside Afghanistan, ISKP claimed an attack on a Chinese restaurant in Kabul on 19 January that killed seven people, and in late July the killing of Badakhshan's Taliban information chief, which AFP called a rare deadly attack on a Taliban official in the north-east.

### Economic
The World Bank estimates growth of 4.8% in 2025 and about 4% in 2026, but per capita income fell 5.6% in 2025 as the population grew faster than output. In April the Bank warned that the conflict in the Middle East could slow growth, with routes through Iran carrying about 60% of Afghan trade. Torkham closed to cargo in October 2025, and in August the Pakistani crossings were open only for returning Afghans. Pakistan's interior minister announced on 8 August that all crossings would reopen, but the government did not confirm it at the time. Da Afghanistan Bank quoted 65.2 afghanis to the dollar on 4 November.

### Social
OCHA counts 21.9 million people in need and a $1.2 billion funding gap for 2026. It said on 20 October that about a third of nutrition centres had closed or suspended services, and that the number of people in crisis-level food insecurity or worse, 15.9 million in September and October, was projected to rise to 19.2 million over the winter. Of this year's returns, 721,700 came from Pakistan and 643,000 from Iran, and Iran carried out 547,100 of the 619,030 deportations. Girls are barred from school beyond sixth grade and women from universities and many jobs, and UNAMA still documents women refused services without a male guardian.

## Indicators to watch
- A Pakistani strike on Kabul or Kandahar city.
- A mass-casualty attack in Pakistan claimed by the TTP, Ittehad ul Mujahideen Pakistan or ISKP, and how fast Islamabad blames Kabul.
- A signed Pakistan-Taliban text with a monitoring mechanism.
- Commercial cargo crossing Torkham or Spin Boldak.
- Da Afghanistan Bank's rate passing 70 to the dollar, or inflation reaching double digits.
- Monthly returns from Iran or Pakistan above 100,000.
- The size of the 2027 humanitarian appeal and its funding by mid-year.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 69 (High), 11th of 203 countries, with red flags for active armed conflict, mass displacement and a large current-account deficit. The direction differs. The score fell 1.2 points on last year, from 71 in 2025, during the worst fighting with Pakistan in years. It was 73 in 2015 and 75 in 2021, and dropped to 65 in 2023 after the insurgency ended.

The stability pillar (59) lags events. Battle deaths (0.61 per 100,000) are for 2024, before the fighting with Pakistan, and the political stability indicator improved from about 22 in 2022 to about 30 in 2025, which tracks the Taliban's grip at home. The economic pillar (49) is the lowest of the four. The model has the current-account deficit at 37.9% of GDP, but debt (7.6% of GDP) and the fiscal deficit (0.5% of GDP) are small for a state that cannot borrow, and inflation (3.6% in 2025) predates this year's rise. The model's 2025 growth figure (1.5%) is far below the World Bank's 4.8%. Reserves data are missing, so coverage is 94%, and the app's watchlist holds no Afghanistan events. Governance (96) is near the ceiling and social (85) is close behind.

I keep the rating at High because the Taliban control the territory and collected revenue worth 19.8% of GDP in 2025, and the war with Pakistan does not threaten Taliban rule in Kabul. My Deteriorating outlook rests on the escalation since 18 September, which the annual data do not yet show.

## Implications
Traders using Pakistan face closed or unreliable crossings, and the Iranian route is exposed to the Middle East conflict. Operators in Kunar, Nangarhar, Paktia, Paktika, Khost and Helmand face cross-border fire. Dealings with the two education ministries, the supreme court or the supreme leader's office now involve ICC suspects. NGOs should plan 2027 budgets for less money and more returnees, under mahram rules that limit women's access to services. Governments negotiating returns, as France is, deal with a leader wanted by the ICC. Pakistan chairs the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation for 2026 to 2027 and can use it to press its case against Kabul.

## Bottom line up front
Nepal's political risk is Elevated and stable. The anti-corruption uprising of September 2025 killed at least 77 people and brought down KP Sharma Oli's government, and on 5 March 2026 Balendra (Balen) Shah's Rastriya Swatantra Party (RSP) won 182 of 275 seats. The main risks now are how Shah governs, often by ordinance and with few public statements, and the cost of the 26 August Bhotekoshi flood, which killed at least 1,455 people and left recovery needs of about $4.7 billion, close to 12% of GDP. Remittances of $16.2 billion in the last fiscal year keep the external accounts strong. I would move the outlook to Deteriorating if local terms ran out in May 2027 without an election, or if security forces again killed protesters.

## Key judgments
1. Balendra Shah is **very likely** (~85%) to still be prime minister on 1 November 2027. The RSP holds 182 seats against 38 for the Nepali Congress and 25 for Oli's CPN (UML), and the constitution bars a no-confidence motion in a prime minister's first two years, which for Shah run to March 2028. The plausible threats are internal: a break with RSP chairman Rabi Lamichhane, or a scandal of the kind that cost two ministers their posts in the government's first month.
2. The government is **likely** (~70%) to hold nationwide local elections by 31 May 2027. Local terms expire on 13 May 2027, the Election Commission was preparing in September for a vote in Falgun (February to March 2027), and the RSP set up a candidate selection panel in April. As of 8 October the government had not set a date.
3. Prosecutors are **unlikely** (~30%) to file charges in court against Oli over the September 2025 killings before November 2027. I hold this with low confidence. A commission recommended investigating him for negligence and police arrested him on 28 March, but the Supreme Court refused to extend his custody and he was freed on bail on 9 April. In July the investigation was still open with no charges filed, and his arrest had led the UML to call two weeks of protests.
4. Security forces are **very unlikely** (~10%) to kill 10 or more people while policing protests in any single month before November 2027. The government came out of the protest movement, and the call to investigate a former prime minister and a police chief for the 2025 shootings has raised the cost of an order to fire. The likeliest triggers are squatter evictions and any move to restructure the provinces.
5. Remittance inflows in FY 2026/27 (mid-July 2026 to mid-July 2027) are **likely** (~70%) to exceed the Rs 2,363 billion that Nepal Rastra Bank recorded for FY 2025/26. Inflows rose 37.1% last year and re-entry labour approvals rose to 385,783, but first-time approvals fell by about a fifth, and a wider war in the Gulf would hit jobs and transfers.
6. Shah is **likely** (~65%) to make an official bilateral visit to India before 31 March 2027. Officials were preparing a three-day trip for late November, but neither government had confirmed dates by mid-October. Shah has kept foreign envoys at a distance, and India had named no successor to its ambassador, who left by 1 October.

## Drivers
### Political
Sushila Karki, a former chief justice, led a caretaker government for six months until Shah was sworn in on 27 March 2026. Oli lost his own Jhapa-5 seat to Shah by about 50,000 votes. A commission under former judge Gauri Bahadur Karki recommended investigating Oli, his home minister Ramesh Lekhak and the then police chief under the National Penal Code's sections on negligence and carelessness.

The government says 70 points of its 100-point reform agenda are done, and it has used ordinances despite its majority, including one that lets the constitutional council, which Shah chairs, decide appointments, judicial ones among them, by simple majority. Proposals to amend the constitution and restructure the federal system have drawn a backlash. With 182 seats the RSP is two short of the 184 needed for a two-thirds majority in the House. On 31 May opposition MPs tried to storm the rostrum as the House passed its rules, at a sitting where Shah had also spoken of mutual encroachment along the border, and the House stayed deadlocked for ten days until the foreign minister clarified his remarks. Shah dismissed his labour minister for getting his wife a seat on the Health Insurance Board, and Home Minister Sudan Gurung resigned on 22 April over questions about his investments.

The old parties are regrouping. Gagan Thapa now leads the Nepali Congress after its 15th general convention, and he says the party has lost public trust. Oli was re-elected UML chairman in December 2025. The provincial assemblies, elected in 2022 when the RSP won only 20 House seats, sit until 19 November 2027.

### Security
Nepal has no armed conflict, and the security risks are protest policing and disasters. A committee led by former judge Baburam Regmi has sent the Home Ministry its report on whether criminal cases linked to the 2025 Gen Z protests can be withdrawn. More than 21,000 security personnel were mobilised after the Bhotekoshi flood, and other disasters killed 395 people between 14 April and 26 October.

### Economic
An ice and rock avalanche from Langtang Lirung, near the border with China, sent the flood down the Bhotekoshi-Trishuli river system, which flows on into India. Bagmati Province's preliminary report of 8 October counted 1,455 dead and 5,285 missing. A government report put damage and losses at about $2.7 billion (6.69% of GDP) and recovery needs at $4.7 billion. Thirteen hydropower projects with a combined 759 MW were affected, and 55 km of the Betrawati-Rasuwagadhi road, one of Nepal's main trade links with China, was destroyed.

Remittances in FY 2025/26 exceeded the Rs 1.964 trillion budget implemented that year. The model's IMF figures for 2026 show a current-account surplus of 13.9% of GDP, growth of 3.6%, inflation of 3.1% and debt of 49.4% of GDP, and the model puts reserves at 15.5 months of imports in 2025. Strains show elsewhere. The passport server went down on 1 October as its German suppliers, owed about Rs 1.2 billion, refused to keep working without payment, and attacks around Hormuz and Bab al-Mandeb had pushed crude above $100 by mid-October.

### Social
The model puts youth unemployment at 21% in 2025, and first-time approvals for work abroad fell last year. The flood damaged 7,570 houses, and survivors were still in holding centres in late October. The Land Problem Resolution Committee has put 1,311,197 applications into one database for verification, and the cabinet has set a 1,000-day target for resolving the landless settlers' issue.

## Indicators to watch
- A local election date, and whether it leaves the Election Commission enough time before 13 May 2027.
- Any ordinance or amendment bill that changes the number or powers of the provinces.
- Charges against Oli or Lekhak, and what the government does with the Regmi committee's report on protest-related cases.
- Public disagreement between Shah and Lamichhane, or more ministers leaving the cabinet.
- Monthly remittance and labour approval data from Nepal Rastra Bank, and fuel or LPG shortages.
- The reopening of the Betrawati-Rasuwagadhi road, and donor pledges for flood recovery.
- Whether Shah's India visit takes place, and what either side says about Kalapani and Lipulekh, which both countries claim.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 45 (Elevated), 90th of 203 countries, with no red flags. The score fell about 2 points on last year to the lowest in the app's history, which starts in 2015. I agree with the level, but I would not read the fall as lower risk.

The stability pillar (39) barely registers the uprising. The political stability indicator was about 58 in 2025, much as it was in 2022, battle deaths (0 in 2024) do not count protest killings, and the homicide figure dates from 2020. The economic pillar (25) keeps Nepal below the middle of the tier. The surplus and the reserves are real strengths, but the IMF's 2026 estimates may not fully reflect flood losses of about 6.7% of GDP. The governance pillar (67) is the highest of the four, with control of corruption at about 32 and government effectiveness at about 37 in 2025; neither the anti-corruption drive nor rule by ordinance is in those numbers yet, and the app's watchlist holds no Nepal events. These gaps argue for a score somewhat above 45 but still inside Elevated, so I keep the tier with a Stable outlook.

## Implications
Hydropower investors on the Trishuli and Bhotekoshi face long outages, and firms trading with China through Rasuwagadhi need other routes until the road is rebuilt. Contractors to the state should plan for late payment, as the passport suppliers found, though a new price adjustment covers public construction contracts hit by higher cement and steel costs. NGOs face a long recovery in Rasuwa, Nuwakot and the districts downstream, and the 2027 monsoon. Nepal expects to need international help to rebuild, so donors, India and China should expect requests to fund much of the $4.7 billion.

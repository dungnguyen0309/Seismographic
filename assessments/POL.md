## Bottom line up front
Poland's political risk is Moderate, the same tier as the model, and I expect it to rise before the parliamentary election due in October or November 2027. The main driver is the conflict between Donald Tusk's government and President Karol Nawrocki, which moved in late October from vetoes to a fight for control of the Constitutional Tribunal, where government-backed judges took over the court's management on 30 October. Russian sabotage and a deficit of about 7% of GDP add to it. A disputed election result, or a sabotage attack that kills people, would move me to Elevated.

## Key judgments
1. The parties of Tusk's current coalition win a majority of the 460 Sejm seats in the 2027 election: **unlikely** (~35%). Mid-October poll averages put parties aligned with the government at about 47% and the right-wing opposition at about 48%, and two coalition partners, the Polish People's Party (PSL) and Poland 2050, polled below the 5% threshold. Confidence is low with a year to go.
2. Poland adopts no constitutional amendment on the Constitutional Tribunal before 12 November 2027: **very likely** (~90%). The "reset" that Deputy Prime Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz proposed on 5 November needs three-fifths of the Sejm, and senior figures in Tusk's Civic Coalition (KO) and the Left have said they will not change the constitution together with Law and Justice (PiS).
3. A sabotage attack that Polish authorities attribute to Russia kills at least one person in Poland before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~10%). The November 2025 rail attack used military-grade C4 but hurt no one, and the security services keep making arrests.
4. The first official estimate of Poland's 2026 general government deficit is 6.0% of GDP or higher: **very likely** (~85%). The finance ministry projects 6.7%, and the 2025 deficit came in at 7.3%, above the 6.8% the European Commission had estimated.
5. At least one of S&P, Moody's and Fitch lowers Poland's foreign-currency rating before 12 November 2027: **roughly even chance** (~50%). Moody's cut Poland to A3 in September 2026, S&P moved its outlook to negative on 6 November and Fitch has had a negative outlook since September 2025. The agencies may wait for the first post-election budget.

## Drivers
### Political
Nawrocki succeeded Andrzej Duda in August 2025 and vetoed more bills in his first ten months than any earlier president did in a full term, including tax increases and crypto market regulation. The government lacks the three-fifths majority needed to override him.

The tribunal is now the main front. Parliament elected eight judges between March and September 2026, and when Nawrocki refused to swear in five of them, the government had them sworn in before parliament. On 30 October Anna Korwin-Piotrowska, one of the eight, declared chief justice Bogdan Święczkowski and his deputy suspended and took control. Nawrocki cut short a visit to Senegal and clashed with her inside the building early on 31 October. On 3 November the eight judges declared a quorum by excluding three judges appointed under PiS and moved ahead with disciplinary proceedings against Święczkowski, who calls it a coup. The EU establishment backs the government; the US embassy has criticised it.

Scandals touch both camps. The collapsed crypto platform Zondacrypto sponsored the 2025 Polish CPAC conference, which Nawrocki attended as a candidate, and donated to foundations linked to former justice minister Zbigniew Ziobro, who is wanted by Polish prosecutors and now lives in the US. On 10 November prosecutors charged PSL deputy foreign minister Władysław Teofil Bartoszewski with misappropriation, which he denies.

Nawrocki will set the election date, which falls on one of four Sundays between 17 October and 7 November 2027 if the term runs its course. Mid-October averages had KO at 32%, PiS 20%, Confederation 14%, Grzegorz Braun's Confederation of the Polish Crown 9% and the Left 7%. Jarosław Kaczyński has ruled out Braun joining a government, so a right-wing majority may be hard to turn into a cabinet.

### Security
On 10 September 2025 Poland and its allies shot down Russian drones over Polish territory, which Tusk called the first such shoot-down over NATO territory. A report by the International Centre for Counter-Terrorism published in early 2026 called Poland the "primary focus" of Russian sabotage in Europe. After sabotage warnings, police began an operation on the Kaliningrad border on 2 November, with officers on full readiness until 8 November. Prosecutors have indicted a Ukrainian accused of helping the 2025 rail attack; the two alleged bombers remain at large.

Defence takes 4.8% of GDP in the 2026 budget and 4.5% in the 2027 draft. Trump announced 5,000 more US troops in May, and a White House decision on a permanent base is expected in early December.

### Economic
The model has growth of 3.4% and inflation of 3.2% in 2026. The 2027 draft budget plans a deficit of 7.1% of GDP, against the 3.7% the government had committed to reach by 2027. S&P calls Poland's position "one of the most significant fiscal deteriorations globally".

### Social
On 7 November a gunman killed six people in Kraków's Main Market Square, and the justice minister cited "very strong traces of right-wing extremism". The Independence March on 11 November drew about 250,000 people by the city's count, and Kaczyński called it a springboard for the campaign. Youth unemployment was 10.7% in 2025.

## Indicators to watch
- Which judges control the tribunal, and whether the disciplinary case against Święczkowski goes ahead.
- New presidential vetoes, especially of tax or budget bills.
- Whether PiS joins Confederation in backing a constitutional reset, and whether KO and the Left drop their objections.
- The White House decision on a permanent US base, expected in early December.
- Sabotage arrests, and any attack that hurts people.
- The official 2026 deficit and any rating action by Fitch or S&P.
- The election date, and whether PSL and Poland 2050 join a KO list or run alone.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: 28 (Moderate), 166th of 203 countries, with full data coverage and one red flag, a fiscal deficit of 5% of GDP or more. The score fell 0.9 points on last year and has been Moderate in every year of the app's history, which starts in 2015 at 24 and peaked at 32 in 2023.

I differ on direction. The governance pillar (25) uses 2025 WGI scores, such as rule of law at 68.01, which predate the October dispute over the tribunal. The stability pillar (17) rests on political stability rising from 74.86 in 2022 to 77.76 in 2025 and no battle deaths in 2024; it misses sabotage and the Kraków shooting, and the app's watchlist has no Poland events. The economic pillar (51) carries the score: its 2026 deficit of 7.2% of GDP is above the ministry's 6.7%, with debt at 66.6%. These gaps add a few points, well short of the 12 needed for Elevated, so I keep Moderate with a Deteriorating outlook.

## Implications
Firms should treat Constitutional Tribunal rulings as contested through 2027, since one camp will reject them whoever controls the court. Rail, energy and logistics operators near the eastern border should plan for sabotage. Bond investors face wide deficits through the election and a real risk of a further downgrade.

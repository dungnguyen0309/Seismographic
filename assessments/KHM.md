## Bottom line up front
Cambodia's political risk is Elevated and stable. Prime Minister Hun Manet's Cambodian People's Party (CPP) faces no organised challenge, and the ceasefire with Thailand of 27 December 2025 has largely held. The border dispute is unresolved: Thai troops hold ground Cambodia claims, more than 20,000 Cambodians are still displaced, and on 31 October Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul said the crossings would stay shut until Thailand "gets what we believe it deserves". Growth is projected at 3 to 3.5% this year. A third round of fighting would move my outlook to Deteriorating; a reopened border would move it to Improving.

## Key judgments
1. Cambodia and Thailand are **unlikely** (~25%) to fight another round of clashes, with artillery, rocket or air strikes on more than one day, before November 2027. The December joint statement froze both armies in place, and in May Anutin said there had been no violent incidents for five months. The Joint Boundary Commission (JBC) has been frozen since June, though, and Thai barricades still keep villagers from their homes; Cambodia protested again on 24 October over Thai road building in Oddar Meanchey.
2. Thailand is **unlikely** (~30%) to reopen any main land crossing with Cambodia to cargo before November 2027. Anutin tied reopening to the land and maritime claims and to guarantees against scam networks, and said economic pressure would not move him. Progress at the maritime conciliation talks, next due in Kuala Lumpur on 24 to 27 November, is the likeliest route to a partial reopening.
3. The CPP is **very likely** (~90%) to win at least 1,600 of the 1,652 commune and sangkat chief posts in the elections on 6 June 2027. It won 1,648 in 2022 with 74.3% of the vote, and on 22 October the Interior Ministry refused to recognise an opposition alliance of the Candlelight and Khmer Will parties. Most of the remaining 10% is a postponement after renewed fighting.
4. Cambodian courts are **very unlikely** (~10%) to convict a sitting minister or senator, or a sanctioned tycoon, on scam-related charges before November 2027. The US trafficking report of 8 October kept Cambodia at Tier 3 and found that some senior officials profited from scam centres and that high-level perpetrators are rarely held to account. The 15 suspects in four corruption cases over shielding scam operators include a deputy prosecutor, the deputy director-general of immigration, a deputy police chief in Phnom Penh and a former senior foreign ministry official.
5. The extra 10% US duty on Cambodian goods under Section 301, in force since 24 July, is **very unlikely** (~15%) to rise above 10% before November 2027. Cambodia is in the 10% group, which covers partners that have pledged to ban imports made with forced labour. Ties have warmed: a US warship moored at Ream in January, and Marco Rubio reaffirmed support for the Kuala Lumpur accord in October.
6. There is a **roughly even chance** (~50%) that the IMF's October 2027 World Economic Outlook puts Cambodia's 2027 growth at 4% or more. The World Bank expects 4%, but arrivals fell 44% in the first eight months of 2026 and banks are exposed to a weak property market. I hold this with low confidence, because the IMF lists labour, remittance and property data among its gaps.

## Drivers
### Political
Hun Manet succeeded his father Hun Sen as prime minister in 2023. After an appeal court upheld the former opposition leader Kem Sokha's 27-year treason sentence on 25 May, Hun Sen, acting for the king, pardoned him. Other cases went on: in May a court jailed Chan Cheat, a former Khmer Will candidate, for 10 years for photographing Thai barriers on the border, and in June the Supreme Court upheld the incitement conviction of Rong Chhun, who remains barred from politics for five years.

The border has narrowed public debate. More than 30 people have been prosecuted over comments on border policy or the military, and on 21 October a minister delegate, So Naro, called it treason to say that Cambodia had lost land to Thailand. In June Hun Sen himself had told displaced families in Banteay Meanchey that Cambodia lost land.

Voter registration for the June 2027 commune elections runs to 30 November. A day after rejecting the opposition alliance, the Interior Ministry withdrew its recognition of Seng Mardy as Khmer Will president.

### Security
Two rounds of fighting in July and December 2025, either side of the Kuala Lumpur Peace Accord of 26 October, killed more than 100 people and displaced hundreds of thousands on both sides. The joint statement of 27 December keeps both forces where they stood and leaves demarcation to the JBC. Thailand says the areas it holds are Thai land settled by Cambodians who fled the civil war of the 1970s, or front lines frozen by the ceasefire; Cambodia calls them occupied. Cambodia's government says nearly 4,000 square kilometres in seven border provinces hold unexploded ordnance, much of it from cluster munitions it says Thai forces used. Cambodia took the maritime dispute to UN conciliation in June, after Thailand scrapped a 2001 memorandum on overlapping claims.

Scam operations have shrunk and dispersed. Between July 2025 and July 2026 the authorities shut 605 sites, sent 4,147 suspects to court and processed 21,666 foreigners for deportation. The anti-scam commission says no large compounds remain, though officials concede some operators have moved into rented rooms, guesthouses and condos. The US and UK sanctioned the Prince Group in October 2025, when FinCEN also cut the Huione Group off from the US financial system; by November 2025 Huione Pay's branches had been rebranded as H-Pay.

Hun Manet and a People's Liberation Army delegation inaugurated the Chinese-renovated Ream naval base in April 2025. Two Chinese warships were docked there when the USS Cincinnati moored on 24 January 2026.

### Economic
The IMF expects growth to fall from 5.3% in 2025 to 3% in 2026 and inflation to rise from 2.5% to 5.6%. Remittances fell as migrants came home, and the current account moved into deficit in 2025. The IMF counts weak real estate and worsening bank asset quality among the domestic risks, given banks' exposure to property; reserves cover about eight months of imports. In October the World Bank cut its 2026 forecast to 3.5%, citing the slump in tourist arrivals and higher fuel costs; diesel prices had risen 46.7%.

Exports of garments, textiles, footwear and travel goods rose 7.2% to $11.76 billion in January to August, 48% of all exports. The US ended its emergency-powers tariffs in February and since 24 July has charged an extra 10% on Cambodian goods under Section 301, against 12.5% for Vietnam and Thailand. USTR plans tariff-rate quotas that would exempt a set volume of Cambodian textiles and apparel, based on Cambodia's imports of US inputs, including cotton.

The government says China will cover 49% of the $1.2 billion Funan Techo canal, whose second section broke ground on 11 April. Public debt was $13.30 billion at the end of March, $4 billion of it owed to China. The draft 2027 budget raises spending by 3.2%, and the government has published no breakdown by sector, so defence spending is not shown.

### Social
A UN human rights expert says Thai forces and barricades have kept more than 20,000 Cambodians from going home. The government has allocated nearly $60 million for about 5,000 temporary homes, and pays displaced families 100,000 riels (about $25) a month plus 28,000 riels for each family member. Residents of the government-built settlements describe scarce jobs and unreliable water and power.

## Indicators to watch
- Exchanges of artillery, rocket or air fire on the border, or a mine casualty that either side blames on the other.
- The conciliation meeting in Kuala Lumpur on 24 to 27 November, and whether Thailand agrees a JBC date.
- Anutin reopens a crossing, or adds conditions for doing so.
- The displaced count falls below 10,000, or rises again.
- Rulings that bar Candlelight or Khmer Will candidates before 6 June 2027.
- New US or UK sanctions on Cambodian officials, senators or banks.
- USTR sets the garment quotas, or changes Cambodia's Section 301 rate.
- Monthly tourist arrivals, garment exports and central bank data on bad loans.

The economic pillar (46) carries the IMF's 2026 figures (growth of 3.0%, inflation of 5.6%, debt of 25.1% of GDP), but no indicator measures the scam economy, the closed border or the fall in arrivals. I would put Cambodia nearer 50 than 43, still inside the Elevated band.

## Implications
Garment buyers pay the 10% Section 301 duty on top of normal tariffs, less than on goods from Vietnam or Thailand, and the planned quotas reward US cotton and other US inputs; Cambodia's pledge to ban imports made with forced labour means more supply-chain checks. Exporters need other routes while the Thai crossings stay shut. Banks and payment firms should screen counterparties against the Prince Group designations and the FinCEN rule on Huione, including successors such as H-Pay. NGOs working with displaced families in Banteay Meanchey, Preah Vihear and Pursat face unexploded ordnance, and staff who comment on the border or the army risk prosecution. Governments should watch the November conciliation round and the June 2027 commune elections.

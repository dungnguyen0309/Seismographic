## Bottom line up front
Timor-Leste's political risk is Moderate and stable over the next 12 months. Offshore oil and gas production ended in 2025. The proposed 2027 budget would draw up to $1.89 billion from a Petroleum Fund that held $18.29 billion at the end of August 2026, and withdrawals on that scale would empty it in the late 2030s. The nearest test is the presidential election, expected in March 2027; a disputed result or deadly unrest would move me to Elevated.

## Key judgments
1. The first round of the presidential election is **very likely** (~90%) to be held by 30 April 2027, with the winner sworn in by 31 May 2027. José Ramos-Horta's five-year term began on 20 May 2022, and a STAE official projected a March vote, though no date had been decreed as of 21 October.
2. The next president is **likely** (~60%) to be a candidate endorsed by Prime Minister Xanana Gusmão's CNRT. CNRT won 288,289 votes in 2023 to Fretilin's 178,338, and Fretilin will not field a candidate; the judgment fails if CNRT splits or endorses no one.
3. The 2027 budget is **very likely** (~85%) to be promulgated before 1 January 2027 with Petroleum Fund transfers above $1.5 billion. The CNRT and Democratic Party (PD) coalition holds 37 of 65 seats, and the 2026 budget passed seven weeks after it was tabled.
4. The fund balance the central bank reports for 30 September 2027 is **likely** (~70%) to be below $18 billion. The 2027 proposal withdraws three and a half times the fund's estimated sustainable income.
5. TIMOR GAP is **likely** (~70%) to complete its purchase of Woodside's 33.44% stake in Greater Sunrise before November 2027. Canberra calls the sale a commercial decision, but it still needs government, regulatory and joint-venture approvals.
6. Political or protest violence is **very unlikely** (~10%) to kill anyone, or to bring a state of emergency or army deployment for public order, before November 2027. The 2025 protests over MPs' perks ended after three days, when MPs agreed to scrap the car purchase and their lifetime pensions.

## Drivers
### Political
Ramos-Horta, who won the 2022 runoff with 62%, said in March that he had not decided whether to run and that the next president should be loyal to Gusmão. Article 75(3) of the constitution allows re-election only once; a constitutional lawyer argues that his non-consecutive 2007 to 2012 term does not count, and a Fretilin MP and a KHUNTO MP also say he may stand. Lere Anan Timur, a retired general on Fretilin's central committee, plans to run as an independent. A September MSRI poll of 575 people put Vice Prime Minister Francisco Kalbuadi Lay (8.66%), Lere (8.62%) and Ramos-Horta (8.55%) almost level on first preference.

In September 2025 thousands marched in Dili against a $4.2 million purchase of SUVs for the 65 MPs; police fired tear gas, and on 26 September parliament voted unanimously to end lifetime pensions for MPs and former office-holders. After students threatened protests and Ramos-Horta promised a veto, parliament suspended a bill to criminalise defamation in July 2026.

Oversight is weakening. Law 11/2026, in force since 15 October, ended the Court of Audit's prior review of contracts above $5 million. On 28 October the government approved a draft decree allowing direct award, at any value, for works on nine Dili road sections that must be finished before Timor-Leste, an ASEAN member since October 2025, chairs the bloc in 2029.

### Security
The main security threat is transnational fraud. UNODC warned in September 2025 that scam networks were moving into the Oecusse enclave. OCCRP reported on 16 October that police had detained 448 people since July, 211 of them Chinese and 181 Indonesian, but only 53 remained in custody awaiting trial. Diligente found no public court record showing whether any organiser has been charged. Fretilin's central committee warned in August that criminal groups could capture the state.

### Economic
The $2.57 billion budget proposed for 2027 would withdraw up to $1.89 billion from the fund, $1.35 billion of it above the estimated sustainable income, against domestic revenue of about $299 million. It authorises up to $1.6 billion of loans, including for oil and gas, and assumes a 5% VAT from 2028. Plenary debate opens on 9 November. The IMF, and official projections cited by the World Bank, put depletion in the late 2030s.

In Greater Sunrise, the government's bet on new revenue, TIMOR GAP holds 56.56%, agreed in September to buy Osaka Gas's 10%, and on 27 October signed a deal to buy Woodside's 33.44% for up to $200 million, $175 million of it payable only after the first LNG cargo. The state would then own the field and, until it finds partners, the cost of a pipeline to Natarbora on the south coast. No operator or financier has been named, and the petroleum minister said in September that he expects a final investment decision in 2029. Development decisions need consensus with Australia under the 2018 maritime boundary treaty.

### Social
Poverty fell from 48.4% in 2014 to 37.0% in 2024 on a comparable measure, but it is 44.45% in rural areas and the Gini coefficient rose from 0.31 to 0.36. Unemployment was 1.6% in 2025, but labour force participation was only 30.5%.

## Indicators to watch
- The decree setting the election date, CNRT's endorsement, and any court ruling on a candidacy under Article 75(3).
- The central bank's monthly fund balance falls below $17.5 billion.
- Loans signed under the $1.6 billion authority, especially for oil and gas.
- Completion of the two Sunrise sales, and any named operator or financier.
- Police use tear gas on protesters again, or scam-centre trials reach the organisers.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 34 (Moderate), 147th of 203 countries and up 0.1 points on last year. It was last Elevated in 2023 (42); the app's series starts at 45 in 2015.

The two red flags, a fiscal deficit of 55.9% of GDP and a current-account deficit of 38.5% (IMF figures for 2026), are real, but the fund pays for them, so the economic pillar (47) overstates the near-term risk. The social pillar (7) errs the other way: unemployment of 1.6% and youth unemployment of 3.7% (2025) reflect low participation, and the pillar has no poverty measure. The governance pillar (63) uses 2025 WGI scores that predate the October audit and procurement changes. The stability pillar (17) reflects a political stability score that rose from about 72 in 2022 to about 77 in 2025 and zero battle deaths in 2024; homicide data are missing and the watchlist holds no Timor-Leste events, so the model cannot see the election. I read these errors as roughly cancelling, so I keep Moderate.

## Implications
Contractors bidding for Dili roadworks, or for Sunrise studies (the 2027 proposal sets aside $193.5 million for Sunrise negotiations and studies), should expect faster awards and fewer external checks. Banks, telecoms firms and hotels should screen for scam-centre activity. A company joining Sunrise would partner a state company that may own all of it, under a treaty that gives Australia a say.

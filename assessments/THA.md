## Bottom line up front
Thailand's political risk is Moderate and stable over the next 12 months. Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul won the February 2026 election and his coalition holds 293 of 500 seats, but a Senate vote-rigging scandal and criticism of his flood response have pushed his approval below 14%, and growth is slow. The land border with Cambodia has been shut since June 2025, and Anutin said on 31 October it would stay shut. Renewed fighting with Cambodia, or a court case that removed Anutin, would move my rating to Elevated.

## Key judgments
1. Anutin is **likely** (~75%) to still be prime minister on 1 November 2027. Kla Tham, an opposition party with 58 MPs, backed his budget in September, and the Election Commission left him out of its Senate collusion petition. The threat is legal: a court removed his predecessor, Paetongtarn Shinawatra, in August 2025.
2. All 18 ministers named in the censure motion are **very likely** (~85%) to survive the vote after the debate set for 7 to 11 December 2026, since Kla Tham is widely expected to vote with the government.
3. Thai and Cambodian forces are **very unlikely** (~12%) to resume fighting that kills 10 or more people in a week before November 2027. The dispute has moved to protest notes and the law of the sea. I hold this with low confidence, because troops still face each other on mined ground.
4. Thailand is **unlikely** (~25%) to reopen any land crossing with Cambodia to regular trade before November 2027. Asked when the crossings would reopen, Anutin said on 31 October: "Not until Thailand gets what we believe it deserves."
5. A second referendum on the charter rewrite is **very unlikely** (~10%) to be held before November 2027. It needs a Section 256 amendment backed by a third of senators, and a Senate spokesman said in May that the Senate had little reason to cooperate with the People's Party. I found no first-reading vote on the May drafts.
6. Bank of Thailand data are **very likely** (~90%) to show household debt above 80% of GDP at the end of June 2027, against 85.2% a year earlier. The ratio is falling mainly because banks lend less.

## Drivers
### Political
Bhumjaithai won 193 seats on 8 February, and on 19 March the House re-elected Anutin by 293 votes to 119 for Natthaphong Ruengpanyawut of the opposition People's Party. Pheu Thai, after its worst result on record, holds several cabinet posts in the 16-party coalition, and about two-thirds of senators are believed to have links to Anutin or to Newin Chidchob, Bhumjaithai's power broker. On 28 September the Constitutional Court rejected a challenge to the coded ballots that could have voided the election.

On 14 September the Election Commission petitioned the Supreme Court against 77 people, 26 of them sitting senators, over collusion in the 2024 Senate selection. It left out Anutin and other Bhumjaithai figures, saying the witness accounts against them were not credible. A NIDA poll reported on 1 October put Anutin's approval at 13.76%, down from 21.7% in June.

A referendum held with the election backed a new constitution, the first of three votes the Constitutional Court requires. Bhumjaithai filed a Section 256 amendment on 20 May for a 100-member drafting assembly chosen by parliament, and the People's Party filed two drafts a week later.

Thaksin Shinawatra was paroled on 11 May and freed in early June after a royal pardon. He has asked the committee that applies the new amnesty law to clear his record. The law excludes lese majeste, and his lese majeste case is before the Appeals Court.

### Security
Two rounds of fighting with Cambodia in 2025 killed close to 150 people and displaced hundreds of thousands, and a ceasefire has held since late December. Every Thai checkpoint on the frontier has been shut since 23 June 2025, and in February Thai troops still held the area along the K5 road in Sa Kaeo, which they took control of on 22 December. On 5 May the cabinet cancelled the 2001 memorandum on overlapping maritime claims, and Cambodia turned to compulsory conciliation under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea. On 10 August Cambodia protested against new Thai trenches in Pursat province and a plan to extend the Thai grid to the Ta Krabey temple and Phnom 333, which it described as consolidating an illegal Thai occupation of its territory.

In the far south, insurgents struck more than 50 sites with arson and bombs in August, a month after an ambush killed five soldiers. The 2027 budget cuts police allowances there by 314 million baht.

### Economic
GDP grew 1.9% year on year in the second quarter and shrank 0.2% on the previous quarter, and the head of the planning agency said the conflict in the Middle East had hit the economy hard. On 28 October the Bank of Thailand held its rate at 1.00% and forecast growth of 2.4% in 2026 and 2.1% in 2027. In July the Constitutional Court upheld a 400 billion baht emergency borrowing decree for co-payment and welfare schemes and the clean-energy transition, and public debt reached 67.94% of GDP at the end of August, against a ceiling of 70%.

### Social
September floods left the Klong Chan estate in Bangkok's Bang Kapi district under about 1.5 metres of water, and the government's handling of the floods drew heavy criticism. Unemployment is very low, but only about 5 million of 27 million borrowers are healthy enough to borrow more.

## Indicators to watch
- Whether the Supreme Court accepts the Senate petition and suspends the senators it names.
- How Pheu Thai and Kla Tham MPs vote in the December censure.
- Any deaths on the Cambodian border, or new trenches or grid work in disputed areas.
- A first-reading vote on the Section 256 drafts, and how many senators vote yes.
- The amnesty committee's ruling on Thaksin and the Appeals Court's lese majeste verdict.
- Public debt above 70% of GDP, or a move to raise the ceiling.

## Implications
Firms trading overland with Cambodia should plan for closed crossings through 2027. Investors face the December censure vote and a government with little room to borrow. Consumer businesses face weak spending while household debt stays above 80% of GDP. Tourism is slow to recover: the University of the Thai Chamber of Commerce expects 34 million foreign arrivals in 2027. NGOs in Yala, Pattani and Narathiwat face rising insurgent attacks, and parts of the region are under martial law.

## Bottom line up front
Sudan's political risk is Severe and still rising. Three and a half years into the war between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), the country is split between an army-run north, centre and east and an RSF-held west. Army chief Abdel Fattah al-Burhan ruled out talks again on 6 October 2026, and drones now hit power stations deep in army-held territory. I would move the outlook to Stable only if both sides accepted a US-backed 90-day truce and it held for a month.

## Key judgments
1. The SAF and the RSF are **very likely** (~85%) to reach November 2027 without a nationwide ceasefire that holds for 30 consecutive days. Burhan has made a truce conditional on the RSF's effective surrender, and both sides gained in 2026 (the army in North Kordofan, the RSF in drone reach), so each still expects more from fighting than from a deal.
2. The RSF is **likely** (~75%) to still hold both Nyala and El Fasher in November 2027. ACLED expects the army to reopen fronts in North Darfur, but as of August the RSF held all of West Kordofan, the ground between the army's lines and Darfur.
3. There is a **roughly even chance** (~50%) that the army takes En Nahud in West Kordofan by November 2027. The army has advanced through North Kordofan since July, but the RSF has been massing forces in West Kordofan.
4. The IPC is **likely** (~60%) to classify at least one area of Sudan as in famine in an analysis published before November 2027. I hold this with low confidence, because fighting limits the data the IPC needs. In May it found 14 areas at risk of famine; the humanitarian appeal was just over 40% funded in August, and WHO expects poor rainfall and crop failures to deepen food insecurity.
5. The street dollar rate in Khartoum is **likely** (~75%) to pass 15,000 Sudanese pounds before November 2027, up from about 10,100 on 21 October 2026. It rose from 6,400 to above 10,000 in under two months after the central bank let banks set their own rates.
6. The UN Security Council is **very unlikely** (~15%) to extend the arms embargo from Darfur to all of Sudan before November 2027. A US push failed in October, when about half the Council reportedly preferred a one-year renewal limited to Darfur.

## Drivers
### Political
Burhan remains Sudan's de facto leader. He appointed Kamil Idris prime minister in May 2025, and the government moved back from Port Sudan to Khartoum in January 2026. The RSF runs a rival "Government of Peace and Unity" from Nyala, sworn in on 30 August 2025 under a 15-member presidential council headed by Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo (Hemedti). The AU, the UN, the US and the EU rejected it, and power-sharing disputes have weakened its coalition.

Mediation has not produced a truce. In November 2025 Burhan rejected a three-month truce plan from the Quad (the US, Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Egypt), saying it left the RSF in its positions. The Quintet (AU, EU, IGAD, Arab League and UN) held consultations with Sudanese parties in late July and early August 2026, and the RSF-aligned Tasis alliance stayed away. On 22 September Reuters reported that Washington had tied Burhan's UN General Assembly visa to a 90-day ceasefire proposed by US adviser Massad Boulos, and the State Department said neither the SAF nor the RSF represents "legitimate, constitutional governance". The Quad itself is split: by early 2026 Saudi Arabia and the UAE were allegedly backing opposing sides.

### Security
ACLED recorded at least 21,863 conflict deaths between March 2025 and August 2026, and logged more incidents in North Kordofan, North Darfur, West Kordofan and South Kordofan than in any other state. The army says it broke the sieges of Dilling and Kadugli in early 2026 and took al-Mazroub, a town linking North and West Kordofan, on 2 October. The RSF has held all of West Kordofan since it took Babanusa and the Heglig oilfield in December 2025. Fighting spread to Blue Nile in January 2026, where the border town of Kurmuk has changed hands several times; the RSF and the SPLM-N entered it again on 13 August.

The war is increasingly fought at range. The UN fact-finding mission said October saw more drone attacks on civilians than any month since the war began, and the UN human rights office counted at least 306 people killed by drones in the first three weeks of the month. On 16 October the army said it had struck RSF positions in Nyala from the air; the RSF said an army drone killed 30 civilians at a livestock market. In the week to 2 November drones targeted 15 power stations from Khartoum to Kassala and Red Sea states, and the government complained to the Security Council and the AU, accusing Ethiopia of involvement. The mission is also investigating reported Egyptian airstrikes that killed at least 72 gold miners near the border.

The RSF's cohesion is the main uncertainty. ACLED reported in May that infighting among its allies had grown and that two senior commanders from its core Mahamid-Rizeigat constituency had defected, with a risk of wider ethnic conflict in Darfur and Chad.

### Economic
The pound traded at about 600 to the dollar before the war began in April 2023. On 24 August 2026 the Central Bank of Sudan let commercial banks set their own rates (Circular No. 16/2026); the street rate passed 10,000 in Khartoum on 21 October, while banks quoted about 4,200. The model adds inflation of 41.3%, government debt of 334.4% of GDP and a current-account deficit of 14.6% of GDP.

### Social
In May the IPC counted about 19.5 million people, some 40% of the population, in crisis or worse, and FAO, WFP and UNICEF expected 825,000 children under five to suffer severe acute malnutrition in 2026. IOM counted 8.6 million internally displaced people at the end of July, 65% of them in Darfur, plus 4.9 million returnees; about 4.6 million have fled abroad, mostly to Egypt, South Sudan and Chad. Donors had funded about $1.2 billion of a nearly $3 billion appeal by August. The fact-finding mission found "hallmarks of genocide" against the Zaghawa and Fur in El Fasher, and in October reported that SAF security cells had arrested thousands in El Obeid.

## Indicators to watch
- The army enters En Nahud or Al Khuway, or the RSF breaks through towards El Obeid.
- Drone strikes on Port Sudan or the Merowe Dam, or outages in Kassala and Red Sea states lasting more than a week.
- Sudanese military action inside Ethiopia, or a Security Council meeting on Sudan's complaint.
- The street rate passes 15,000 pounds, or the central bank reverses Circular No. 16/2026.
- A famine finding in any of the 14 at-risk areas.
- The Security Council vote before the Sudan sanctions regime expires on 11 December 2026, and whether the text widens the embargo.
- Further senior RSF defections or resignations from the Tasis presidential council.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 91 (Severe), second of 203 countries, with five red flags. I agree with the level but expect faster deterioration than the score shows.

The score rose only 0.2 points on last year because Sudan is near the ceiling: the social pillar is at 100, governance at 98 and stability at 95, so the Blue Nile front and the drone campaign against the eastern grid cannot push it much higher. The political stability indicator (about 29 on the app's scale in 2025) has fallen only from about 32 in 2022, the year before the war broke out. The battle-deaths figure (9.28 per 100,000) is annual and misses October, the worst month yet for drone attacks on civilians.

The economic pillar (72) is the softest part of the score. It credits Sudan with 7.0% real growth and a fiscal deficit of only 3.9% of GDP, and I read the low deficit as a sign of a state that collects and spends little. Reserves, homicide and youth unemployment data are missing, the annual figures predate the pound's fall since August, and the app's watchlist holds no Sudan events. My Deteriorating outlook rests on the October escalation, which the model does not yet see.

## Implications
Companies outside humanitarian supply chains should treat Sudan as closed apart from trade through Port Sudan, and even Red Sea state, where the port is, was among the grid targets. A dollar changed at a bank buys about 4,200 pounds, against more than 10,000 on the street. Firms dealing in Sudanese gold or fuel need sanctions and conflict screening, as do counterparties in Chad, south-eastern Libya, Somalia's Puntland and the UAE, where the fact-finding mission found individuals and entities in a network supporting the RSF. NGOs face drones in El Obeid and Darfur, and in El Obeid the mission reports SAF-affiliated security actors targeting people doing humanitarian and rights work. Governments should plan for more refugees in Chad, Egypt and South Sudan, and for the December sanctions vote.

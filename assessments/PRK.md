## Bottom line up front
North Korea's political risk is High and stable over the next 12 months, one tier above the model. Kim Jong Un looks secure after the Ninth Party Congress in February, and analysts say Russia pays for his troops and munitions with aid and military technology. The risk lies outside the regime: Pyongyang fired ballistic missiles three times in October, the IAEA reports a second enrichment building at Yongbyon, and a North Korean mine wounded three South Korean soldiers in the Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) on 21 September. A Trump-Kim meeting followed by a pause in long-range testing would move my outlook to Improving; an ICBM flight, a nuclear test or a deadly border clash would move it to Deteriorating.

## Key judgments
1. Kim Jong Un is **very likely** (~93%) to still be North Korea's leader on 1 November 2027. The February congress retired much of the old guard and promoted his sister. The residual risk is his health, which outsiders cannot observe, and he has no formally named successor.
2. Donald Trump and Kim are **unlikely** (~25%) to meet in person before November 2027. Trump said in mid-October that he might meet Kim on his trip to the APEC summit, and in August he had the Ulchi Freedom Shield exercise cut by half. Kim did not mention Trump's overtures at a Politburo meeting in August, Kim Yo Jong denied that the two leaders were in contact, and the State Department still seeks denuclearisation, which Pyongyang refuses to discuss. I hold this with low confidence because it turns on Trump's personal choices.
3. North Korea is **unlikely** (~25%) to flight-test an intercontinental ballistic missile before November 2027. The last ICBM flight I can confirm was the Hwasong-19 on 31 October 2024, the Hwasong-20 had not flown as of April, and none of the October launches was reported as an ICBM. A launch would probably end Trump's outreach.
4. A North Korean nuclear test is **very unlikely** (~10%) before November 2027. Kim says nuclear status is now written into the constitution, and the effort has moved to fissile material: the IAEA reported a new building at Yongbyon that could hold up to 28 centrifuge cascades.
5. North Korean military personnel are **likely** (~75%) to still be in Russia in November 2027, by South Korean or Western intelligence accounts. The NIS put the force in Kursk at about 11,000 in early 2026, and in April Russia offered a military cooperation plan for 2027 to 2031. A ceasefire would still leave work for engineers.
6. North Korea is **very unlikely** (~15%) to hold official talks with the South Korean government before November 2027. Its revised constitution drops unification, Kim Yo Jong dismissed Seoul's call for dialogue on 2 October, and less than a week after Seoul offered military talks, Pyongyang fired about ten missiles on 29 October.

## Drivers
### Political
Kim opened the Ninth Party Congress on 19 February, telling about 5,000 delegates that the country had overcome recession. The five-year plan for 2026 to 2030 puts consolidation and quality in industry and farming ahead of output targets. Daily NK counts 23 new members on the 39-seat congress presidium. Kim Yo Jong became a full department director and, by Daily NK's account, still handles relations with the South and other external affairs. Kim's daughter, Kim Ju Ae, appeared only at the closing parade, and KEIA's Robert King found no sign at the congress that she had been named successor.

In March Kim told the Supreme People's Assembly that the South was the "most hostile" state and that nuclear status was now in the constitution. The revised text, reviewed by AFP in May, removes all references to unification and adds a territorial clause that draws no inter-Korean line.

Washington has softened its tone. The White House said on 1 September that Trump would talk to Kim without preconditions. On his order the allies cut Ulchi Freedom Shield from 11 days to five in August, and he linked the cut to Seoul's refusal to join the US and Israel's war on Iran; Seoul learned of the order from his social media post. Kim's terms, as Daily NK reads the congress, are recognition as a nuclear state and an end to what Pyongyang calls US hostile policy.

### Security
By mid-October North Korea had tested missiles on more than ten occasions in 2026, by Reuters' count. On 3 October Kim oversaw a launch from the Wonsan area; state media said the missile was an intermediate-range model from a hypersonic weapon system. On 12 October at least one missile fired from near Pyongyang flew more than 900 km, and on 29 October about ten short-range ballistic missiles were fired from around Wonsan.

On 21 September a mine exploded near the Military Demarcation Line at Paju, wounding three South Korean soldiers. A joint investigation by Seoul and the UN found in October that North Korea had laid the mines more than a year earlier. Kim Yo Jong rejected Seoul's demand for an apology, and North Korea's UN ambassador wrote to the Security Council accusing Seoul of fabricating the blast.

The IAEA, which has had no inspectors in the country since 2009, reported on 28 August that a building at Yongbyon is a second enrichment facility and that there were signs an enrichment annex at Kangson began operating in January. In Russia, the NIS estimated in February that about 6,000 North Korean soldiers had been killed or wounded. On 21 October Putin agreed in principle to resume US-mediated talks with Ukraine after 10 November.

### Economic
The model has no IMF data for North Korea; the Bank of Korea estimated growth of 3.7% in 2024, the fastest in eight years. Analysts quoted by AFP say Russia pays for troops and munitions with money, technology, food and energy. Since October 2024 an 11-state Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team (MSMT), which works outside the UN, has tracked evasion. In September it estimated that 35,600 to 101,280 North Korean workers in at least 17 countries, mainly China and Russia, earned $450 million to $800 million in 2025. In October 2025 it found that North Korean hackers stole at least $1.65 billion in cryptocurrency from January to September that year, $1.4 billion of it from the Bybit exchange. In late October 2026, 11 governments and the EU welcomed new US, Australian and EU sanctions on cyber activity and overseas labour.

### Social
Food is the main social risk. State media reported a severe drought in April, and the UN special rapporteur said in February that shortages were already a concern. FAO's September brief, based on remote sensing, found average to above-average rain from May to early August and good vegetation across most of the country, which points to a favourable main harvest; the winter and spring harvest, completed in June, also had conducive weather.

## Indicators to watch
- Trump's trip to the APEC summit in Shenzhen on 18 and 19 November, any stop at Panmunjom, and KCNA's reaction.
- A first flight of the Hwasong-20, or preparations at the Punggye-ri test site.
- Pyongyang's answer to Seoul's offer of military talks, and further incidents along the Military Demarcation Line.
- The size of the spring 2027 US-South Korean exercise, and whether Trump cuts it as he did Ulchi Freedom Shield.
- US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks after 10 November, and whether a ceasefire text covers foreign troops in Kursk.
- New MSMT and IAEA reports, and FAO's estimate of the 2026 main harvest.

Two of the three remaining pillars read low for reasons I do not trust. Social (20) rests on unemployment of 3.5%, youth unemployment of 7.9% and a displaced share of zero in 2025, which I read as signs of a state that assigns jobs and shuts its borders. Governance (96) matches what I see, with voice and accountability at 19 and control of corruption at 17 in 2025.

The score fell from 55 in 2015 to 46 in 2022 and 2023 and has edged back to 49. No indicator captures the enrichment programme, the missile launches, sanctions evasion through workers and crypto theft, or the mined border, and the app's watchlist holds no North Korea events. Those risks fall on neighbours and counterparties, so I rate High. The Trump channel roughly offsets the border tension, so I keep the outlook Stable.

## Implications
Any trade with North Korea or use of its labour carries sanctions risk. For most firms the exposure is indirect: remote IT contractors who hide their nationality (the MSMT found North Korean IT workers in at least eight countries, mostly China), crypto platforms, and suppliers in China and Russia that hire North Korean workers. Airlines and shipping in the region should expect launches at short notice, often from the Wonsan area. Seoul has an ally that cut a major exercise without warning, and security observers warn that smaller drills could set back the planned transfer of wartime operational control to South Korea.

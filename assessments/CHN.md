## Bottom line up front
China's political risk is Elevated and stable over the next 12 months. The party's hold on the country is secure, but foreign exposure runs through a US-China trade truce that now lasts only to 10 January 2027 and a high command that Xi Jinping has purged down to himself and one general ahead of the 21st Party Congress in the second half of 2027. A broader trade deal settling tariffs and rare-earth supply would move me to Moderate; a lapse of the truce alongside a military crisis over Taiwan or the South China Sea would move me to High.

## Key judgments
1. Xi Jinping is **very likely** (~93%) to still be party general secretary, chairman of the Central Military Commission (CMC) and state president on 1 November 2027. The October plenum confirmed 33 expulsions and set the 21st Congress for the second half of 2027, and its communiqué shows no sign of a challenge; his health is the main remaining risk.
2. Washington and Beijing are **likely** (~70%) to extend the Busan truce again, or replace it with a broader deal, before it lapses on 10 January 2027. The two leaders plan to meet in Shenzhen in November and Miami in December, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says the US is open to continuing the arrangement. The short September extension and US complaints about rare-earth deliveries keep this below very likely.
3. A return to broad trade escalation is **unlikely** (~25%) before November 2027, meaning US tariffs on all Chinese goods above their October 2026 level or China reinstating the rare-earth restrictions it paused at Busan. The September agreement to cut tariffs on $30 billion of goods each way gives both sides something to lose.
4. There is a **roughly even chance** (~50%) that the People's Liberation Army (PLA) stages at least one large named exercise around Taiwan, like Justice Mission 2025, before November 2027. A new US arms package, which Thomas Christensen expects within 12 months in a CSIS commentary, is the likeliest trigger; the purge and Beijing's interest in a calm run-up to the Congress weigh against it.
5. National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) data for January to September 2027 are **likely** (~75%) to show property development investment falling again year on year. In the same months of 2026 investment fell 20.6% and developers' funds 21.6%.
6. The CMC is **likely** (~75%) to have at least four members again by 1 November 2027. The October plenum made no military appointments, so this rests on the Congress, or a plenum before it, refilling the commission; a Congress held in November would leave it at two members until then.

## Drivers
### Political
The fifth plenum of the 20th Central Committee, held in Beijing from 26 to 29 October, set the 21st National Congress for the second half of 2027 and added General Zhang Shengmin to the Politburo. It confirmed the expulsion of 17 civilians, among them Ma Xingrui, and 16 officers, among them Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli. Chieh Chung of the Association for Strategic Foresight counts 96 Central Committee members, 26% of the body, who have left under a cloud. The communiqué called for a good start to the 15th Five-Year Plan, whose first year is 2026.

Ties with Washington have steadied: Donald Trump made a state visit to Beijing in May, and Xi made one to Washington from 23 to 25 September. Ties with Japan have worsened. In November 2025 Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi told parliament that a Chinese attack on Taiwan would be a "survival-threatening situation" for Japan, which could allow Japanese military action, and in January Beijing banned exports of dual-use items to Japan.

### Security
On 24 January the Defence Ministry announced investigations into Zhang Youxia, the senior CMC vice-chair, and Liu Zhenli, chief of the Joint Staff Department. The other vice-chair, He Weidong, had been expelled in October 2025, and the National People's Congress Standing Committee removed Zhang and Liu from the state CMC on 28 August. Chen Shih-min of National Taiwan University says only Xi and Zhang Shengmin, a commissar with no training in commanding troops, remain on the commission. Speakers at a Taipei seminar on the plenum argued that the purge lowers the risk of a planned war against Taiwan but raises the risk of a clash through miscalculation.

Beijing considers Taiwan part of its territory and has pledged to take control of the island, by force if necessary; Taiwan rejects the claim and says only its people can decide the island's future. On 29 and 30 December 2025 the PLA held Justice Mission 2025, live-fire drills that simulated blockades of Taiwan's main ports, after Washington approved an $11.1 billion arms sale. On 10 April Kuomintang (KMT) chair Cheng Li-wun met Xi in Beijing and afterwards called the 1992 Consensus and opposition to Taiwan independence the only political basis for cross-strait exchanges. In Washington Xi pressed the US to oppose "Taiwan independence", and US policy did not change. Taiwan elects 11,051 local officials on 28 November.

The main friction at sea is with the Philippines. At the contested Second Thomas Shoal, where the Philippines grounded a navy ship in 1999 to back its claim, the Philippine military says China Coast Guard personnel struck a sailor with a baton on 20 July. Bonny Lin of CSIS reports that China blocked a Philippine resupply mission there during Xi's Washington visit. As of July, ASEAN aimed to adopt a legally binding code of conduct for the South China Sea at its November summit in Manila.

### Economic
The NBS put third-quarter growth at 4.5% and January to September at 4.6%, within the target of 4.5% to 5%. Exports rose 16.4% in yuan terms over the nine months, while retail sales of consumer goods rose only 1.1% and fixed-asset investment fell 7.2%. Property development investment fell 20.6% and the sales value of new buildings 13.9%.

Producer prices rose 5.0% year on year in September, led by oil and gas extraction (up 24.5%) and fuel processing (up 21.6%), after the war in the Middle East sent crude prices soaring in the spring. Consumer prices rose 0.9%: food fell 1.7% and energy for transport rose 14.9%. In February the IMF linked the property adjustment to a debt overhang in local government finances and called the policy measures so far modest relative to the challenges. The model's IMF figures put government debt at 104.4% of GDP and the deficit at 8.0% in 2026.

The Busan truce of October 2025 cut the average US tariff on Chinese goods from 57% to 47% and paused China's planned rare-earth restrictions for a year. In September the two sides extended it from 10 November to 10 January 2027 and agreed to cut tariffs on $30 billion of each other's goods, but the White House left the remaining 10% fentanyl tariff in place. US officials say China lags on rare-earth deliveries and on $17 billion of non-soybean farm purchases.

### Social
Urban surveyed unemployment was 5.2% in September, and real disposable income per head rose 4.4% in the first three quarters, slower than GDP. The model puts youth unemployment at 15.8% in 2025, and weak retail sales point to cautious households.

## Indicators to watch
- The truce is not extended by 10 January 2027, or the Shenzhen and Miami meetings end without a joint text.
- A commerce ministry notice restoring rare-earth controls, or new export bans like the one on Japan.
- A new US arms sale to Taiwan, and any named PLA exercise that follows it.
- China Coast Guard force at Second Thomas Shoal or Scarborough Shoal that injures or kills Philippine personnel.
- CMC appointments, and the date the Politburo sets for the 21st Congress.
- Monthly NBS property investment and new home sales, and whether the PPI turns negative if oil falls.

These fit the NBS data but hide a fifth less property investment and producer prices pushed up by oil. Data coverage is complete, so the gap comes from what the indicators measure. I keep the outlook Stable because the truce and the leaders' meetings make a sharp break in the next few months less likely.

## Implications
Importers and exporters should plan around 10 January 2027 and hold buffer stocks of rare-earth magnets, since Washington already says deliveries lag. Firms shipping Chinese dual-use items to Japan face the January ban. Partners of state defence and aerospace groups should expect management turnover after the purge, and Chieh Chung expects a review of defence procurement. Lenders should treat developers and the unsustainable local government financing vehicles the IMF wants restructured as the weakest credits. Shippers and insurers should price incidents around Second Thomas Shoal. Governments should expect Beijing to keep ties with Washington calm before the Congress while it presses Japan, the Philippines and Taiwan.

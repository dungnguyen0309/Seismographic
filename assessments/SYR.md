## Bottom line up front
Syria is a High-risk country with a stable outlook to November 2027. The danger of renewed nationwide war has fallen since the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) dissolved into the army on 25 August 2026, a day after the US removed Syria from its list of state sponsors of terrorism. Risk has moved to the south, where parts of Suweida remain outside state control and Israeli troops operate inside Syria, and to living costs, which in September set off the widest protests since Assad's fall. A collapse of the north-east merger or a new massacre of a minority community would move me to Severe; a security deal with Israel plus a settlement in Suweida would move me towards Elevated.

## Key judgments
1. Ahmed al-Sharaa is still Syria's interim president on 3 November 2027: **very likely** (~90%). The SDF has dissolved into the army, the UN Security Council has taken him off its sanctions list, and Washington has removed Syria from its list of state sponsors of terrorism. The main risk is an assassination or a split in the security forces.
2. Syria holds neither a direct national election nor a referendum on a permanent constitution before November 2027: **very likely** (~90%). The People's Assembly first sat on 12 July 2026, with 70 of its 210 members appointed by Sharaa, and on 17 September the UN deputy envoy placed election preparations within the transition's remaining three and a half years.
3. Suweida is still partly outside Damascus's control in November 2027, with no agreed deployment of state security forces in Suweida city: **likely** (~70%). In September the UN deputy envoy called for the roadmap that Syria, Jordan and the US agreed in September 2025 to be reinvigorated, Suweida's three Assembly seats were left empty, and on 22 October a drone attack attributed to Druze "National Guard" militias reportedly hit a state security post.
4. Syria and Israel sign a written security or de-escalation agreement by November 2027: **unlikely** (~30%). Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani said in August that "almost everything was agreed upon on paper", but Israel's defence minister has said Israeli forces will not withdraw from Mount Hermon, and the 27 October Israeli election left no bloc with a majority.
5. An attack attributed to the Islamic State (ISIL) kills ten or more people in a single incident inside Syria before November 2027: **likely** (~60%). A UN report in July estimated up to 3,000 ISIL fighters in Syria, shifting from the desert towards urban areas, and 15,000 to 20,000 former al-Hol residents have been unaccounted for since the camp closed in February.
6. The IMF Executive Board concludes an Article IV consultation with Syria before November 2027: **unlikely** (~25%). Damascus has asked for one, but the IMF ties resumption to better statistics, and its July staff visit will not lead to a Board discussion.

## Drivers
### Political
Power sits with the presidency: Sharaa appointed a third of the Assembly, and about 10% of its members are women. Outside backing is broad, and even Russia agreed on 9 August that its Hmeimim and Tartous bases would become joint training centres.

On 15 October a military court in Aleppo issued verdicts against seven defendants over the March 2025 coastal violence against Alawites, sentencing two to death and acquitting two. It was the first time members of the current security forces had been put on trial, though critics ask whether commanders will be investigated. How Damascus treats abuses by its own forces will decide how far minorities trust the new state.

### Security
The north-east is the year's largest gain. A government offensive in January took most SDF territory, an agreement at the end of January set out a phased merger, and SDF commander Mazloum Abdi announced the dissolution in Damascus on 25 August. An analyst quoted by Al Jazeera expects a unified chain of command to take much longer, and no decision has been announced on the YPJ women's units. US forces handed their last base, Qasrak in Hasakah, to the Syrian army on 16 April.

I expect most of next year's violence in the south. ACLED recorded 84 Israeli incursions in September and 45 in the first 12 days of October, and Israeli forces resumed work on a fortified road, Sufa 53, along the buffer zone on 4 October. On 26 October Jordan struck Daraa and Suweida after a church attack in Karak. Syrian civil defence recovered seven bodies, and Damascus denied any coordination and closed the Nassib crossing. All 19 earlier Jordanian strikes that ACLED recorded since December 2024 hit Suweida; this was Jordan's first strike in Daraa since March 2024.

Tribal and communal clashes made up nearly half of all armed clashes in July and August, against under 8% in the first quarter, and have killed at least 101 people this year. Security forces intervened in only about one in four of them, and nearly a quarter were in Deir ez-Zor, where ISIL is also active.

### Economic
The IMF expects double-digit growth in 2026, after a small central government surplus in 2025. Inflation fell to low double digits in 2025 but has risen considerably this year as the regional war involving Iran raised fuel and food import costs, and a dysfunctional banking system leaves monetary policy with few tools. According to the energy minister, Syria produces about 102,000 barrels of oil a day against needs of about 325,000, so oil price shocks reach the pump fast. An explosion on the Jbessa gas pipeline in Hasakah on 15 October was described as sabotage.

Sanctions weigh less now that Washington has dismantled its broader Syria programme and removed the terrorism designation. Reconstruction money is the larger gap: the World Bank put needs at $216 billion in October 2025, nearly ten times Syria's projected 2024 GDP. My confidence on the economy is low, because the IMF itself says data gaps limit its assessment.

### Social
UNDP estimates that 90% of Syrians live below the poverty line. Fuel price rises of up to 40% on 13 September set off the widest protests since Assad's fall, blocking the Damascus to Aleppo motorway and reaching Idlib and Hama; the UN says the fuel increases pushed food prices up by about 30%. About 1.7 million refugees and 1.9 million internally displaced people had gone home by July, but 5.5 million people remain displaced inside Syria and 2.7 million children are out of school. Only a third of the 2026 UN humanitarian appeal had been funded in July.

## Indicators to watch
- The decision on the YPJ, and whether former SDF units deploy outside Hasakah and Kobani.
- Any deal that lets state security forces into Suweida city, or new fighting there that empties villages.
- Israeli coalition talks after the 27 October election, work on the Sufa 53 road, and ACLED's monthly count of Israeli incursions (84 in September).
- The next fuel price review, and whether protests spread beyond the six governorates they reached in September.
- Reopening of the Nassib crossing, and any further Jordanian strikes in Daraa.
- The IMF statement after its next staff visit, and new central bank and banking laws.

## Why my rating differs from the model
The model rates Syria Severe at 97, first of 203 countries and unchanged on last year. I rate it one tier lower, at High.

The stability pillar scores 100 on a battle-death rate of 11.07 per 100,000, a UCDP annual figure from before 2026, and the war-level violence red flag (1,000 or more battle deaths in a year) rests on the same data. ACLED counted 202 armed clashes in January 2026 and fewer than 35 a month since June, and the largest armed actor outside the state has since dissolved. The January offensive may keep the 2026 death toll high, but the monthly trend points down.

The economic pillar has no data, so the score rests on stability, governance (96) and social (94). Adding it would not clearly lower the score: inflation has picked up this year, though the model would also see double-digit growth and a small budget surplus. The WGI scores end in 2025: government effectiveness is 19.9, control of corruption 15.4, and political stability rose only from 22.7 in 2022 to 25.0. They predate the US delisting and the return of state control in the north-east, and the app's watchlist has no Syria events.

The other two red flags still hold: 41.23% of Syria's population is forcibly displaced and youth unemployment is 33.1%. With parts of Suweida outside state control and Israeli forces on the Syrian side of Mount Hermon, that keeps me at High.

## Implications
Sanctions exposure for companies has dropped, but payments and due diligence stay hard while banks are weak and statistics thin. Security risk is lower in Damascus, Aleppo and the coast than in Suweida, Daraa, Quneitra or Deir ez-Zor, and the Nassib closure shows the land route through Jordan can shut overnight. NGOs face a funding gap, and the recovery and reconstruction conference the UN is exploring with Damascus is the best chance to close it. For governments, US pressure on Israel to settle security arrangements is the step most likely to calm the south.

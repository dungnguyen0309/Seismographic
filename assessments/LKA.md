## Bottom line up front
Sri Lanka's political risk is Elevated and stable over the next 12 months, and most of it is economic and external. President Anura Kumara Dissanayake's National People's Power (NPP) holds 159 of 225 seats and passed a contested constitutional amendment on 25 September 2026 with votes to spare. The economy leaves its IMF programme in March 2027 with government debt the model puts at 95.2% of GDP, about half of its petroleum imports coming from the Middle East, and inflation at 8.3% in October, its highest in more than three years. I would move the outlook to Deteriorating if the US-Iran conflict forced fuel rationing, or if the IMF Board had not approved the seventh review by the end of January 2027.

## Key judgments
1. The NPP is **very likely** (~90%) to still hold at least 150 of the 225 seats, the two-thirds needed to amend the constitution, in November 2027. The 22nd Amendment passed by 158 votes to 63, and the government did not need opposition votes. The opposition has turned to procedure: the Committee on Parliamentary Business rejected its no-confidence motion against Speaker Jagath Wickramaratne, and on 3 November some opposition MPs tried to seize the mace.
2. Provincial council elections are **very unlikely** (~15%) to be held in any province before November 2027. The government once aimed for the first half of 2026; in August 2026 Minister Nalinda Jayatissa said legal obstacles had to be removed and delimitation finished first, and gave no date. The NPP took 43.26% of the vote in the May 2025 local elections, which gives it little electoral reason to hurry.
3. Sri Lanka is **likely** (~75%) to complete its four-year Extended Fund Facility, with the IMF Board approving both remaining reviews, before November 2027. IMF staff reached agreement on the seventh review, worth about $345 million, on 4 October, provided the budget presented on 12 November fits programme parameters and financing assurances are confirmed. The final review falls close to the arrangement's expiry in March 2027, and an oil shock or a looser budget could delay it.
4. Colombo consumer price inflation is **likely** (~60%) to be at or below 7%, the upper limit of the central bank's band, in the October 2027 reading. October 2026 was the fourth month above that limit, and the central bank expects high single digits through the first quarter of 2027 before a return towards its 5% target. The IMF, the central bank and the World Bank all list El Niño among the risks to their forecasts.
5. The government is **very unlikely** (~15%) to ration fuel by quota, for example through QR codes, before November 2027. Remittances in January to September came to $6.87 billion, against $5.81 billion a year earlier, and reserves reached $6.9 billion at end-August, so the country can pay for fuel at current prices. When queues formed in March, the government raised prices and said stocks covered 40 days. Rationing would take a physical break in Gulf supply, as in the downside scenario.

## Drivers
### Political
The government's main constitutional move this year was the 22nd Amendment, which the Supreme Court cleared without a referendum on 22 September. It raises the retirement age of Supreme Court judges from 65 to 67 and of Court of Appeal judges from 63 to 65, and limits a Chief Justice to six years, a limit that does not apply to the current holder. The government says it will speed up cases; opposition leader Sajith Premadasa says it weakens judicial independence, and the Bar Association petitioned against it.

Namal Rajapaksa, an MP for the Sri Lanka Podujana Peramuna (SLPP), is accused of taking a Rs 100 million bribe over a land transfer; he won bail in that case on 3 November but stays in remand on separate proceedings. The budget is the next test, with votes on 20 November and 14 December.

### Security
Sri Lanka has no armed conflict, and the model records zero battle deaths for 2024. The Protection of the State from Terrorism Bill (PSTA) would replace the Prevention of Terrorism Act (PTA), which the government has promised to repeal. The UN human rights office says the bill reproduces or expands many of the PTA's failings, including detention orders of up to a year issued by the defence secretary. The Supreme Court is hearing petitions, Tamils across the north and east protested in October, and on 1 November Minister Bimal Rathnayake said the government was open to discussion. Minister Ananda Wijepala, who expects an Organised Crime Prevention Bill to pass within a month, has acknowledged that several leading underworld figures are being held under the PTA.

In Jaffna, police removed a statue of Thileepan from his Nallur memorial after the September commemoration and opened a PTA investigation, with the government citing the ban on the LTTE; police have since ordered the Jaffna Municipal Council to demolish what they call unauthorised structures at the site.

### Economic
Real GDP grew 4.7% in the first half of 2026, and the World Bank expects 4.4% for the year and 4.2% in 2027. At its May review the IMF cut its 2026 growth forecast from 4% to 3% because of the Middle East war, and its end-2026 reserves projection from $9.506 billion to $8.645 billion, noting that the region supplies about 40% of remittances. After a primary surplus of 5.4% of GDP in 2025, it accepted a temporary fiscal easing this year, with consolidation to resume in 2027; the model carries a 2026 fiscal deficit of 4.2% of GDP.

The central bank tightened policy in May and on 30 September kept its policy rate at 8.75%. Food inflation reached 8.8% in October and core inflation 5.6%, and fuel prices rose again on 1 November. Oil fell more than 7% on 2 November after Iran said its contacts with the United States were continuing.

Tourist arrivals fell 19.8% in March and 22.3% in April after the war began, and January to September ended 1.8% below 2025. October then set a record for the month, with 168,625 visitors.

The IMF says the debt restructuring is largely complete. Rathnayake said in late September, "We will definitely exit this agreement", but on 8 October Deputy Finance Minister Anil Jayantha told Parliament that talks with the Fund on what follows were under way, describing a shift towards a "long-term growth-targeted engagement model".

### Social
Cyclone Ditwah struck the east coast on 28 November 2025. By mid-December 643 people had died and more than 107,000 homes were damaged or destroyed. The World Bank put direct damage at $4.1 billion, about 4% of GDP. Floods and mudslides killed at least seven people in the central highlands in August 2026. Poverty, at 16.9% on the World Bank's measure, is well above pre-crisis levels, and the model has youth unemployment at 18.58% (2025).

On 3 November the Government Medical Officers' Association announced protests at major hospitals over pay, allowances given up during the crisis and staff shortages. In Jaffna, residents of Vasavilan have protested monthly since August for land the military has held for 36 years.

## Indicators to watch
- The budget speech on 12 November and the IMF's response, then the Board date for the seventh review.
- Inflation above 9%, or a policy rate rise at the 20 November review or later.
- A return of fuel queues, or any quota scheme.
- Gross reserves below $6 billion, or monthly remittances below the same month of 2025.
- Monthly tourist arrivals falling more than 10% year on year, as in March and April 2026.
- The Supreme Court determination on the PSTA, and arrests of journalists, unionists or protesters under the PTA or its replacement.
- Parliament passing a legal framework and delimitation for provincial council elections.
- Floods and landslides in Kandy, Badulla and Nuwara Eliya during the northeast monsoon.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 50 (Elevated), 64th of 203 countries, with full data coverage and red flags for thin reserves (under three months of imports) and public debt of at least 90% of GDP. The score rose 4.3 points on last year, from 45.5 in 2025; its only year outside the Elevated tier since 2015 was 2022, at 60 (High).

The stability pillar (38, the lowest of the four) fits the politics: the political stability indicator rose from about 52 in 2022 to about 65 in 2025. Homicide data are from 2019 and the app's watchlist holds no Sri Lanka events. The model has no measure of disaster exposure, so Ditwah's 643 deaths do not register; the displacement figure (0.86% of the population in 2025) counts people forcibly displaced from the country.

The economic pillar (61) is the highest, and I agree that the economy carries most of the risk, though some of its inputs are off. The reserves flag rests on 2024 data (2.87 months of imports). Gross reserves were $6.9 billion at end-August 2026 and the IMF projects $8.645 billion by end-2026, so the flag may overstate that problem. The 2026 inflation estimate of 5.3% is an annual average and looks low against 8.3% in October, and the model cannot see the end of IMF support in March 2027. Governance (55) rests on 2025 WGI scores, which predate the 22nd Amendment and the PSTA. I read these errors as roughly cancelling.

## Implications
Holders of Sri Lankan debt should watch the budget and the seventh review; after March 2027 fiscal policy loses the programme's anchor unless a new arrangement is agreed. Rathnayake has also complained that some restructured debt pays creditors more as growth rises. Companies should expect fuel price revisions that feed into transport and hotel costs. The IMF calls the Middle East a major aviation hub for travel to Sri Lanka, so tourism is exposed to any disruption of flights there. NGOs and media in the north and east should follow the PSTA, since the UN says its offences are broad enough to reach journalists, rights defenders and peaceful protesters.

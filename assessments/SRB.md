## Bottom line up front
I rate Serbia's political risk Elevated and Stable, in line with the model (49, Elevated). The Student List, which grew out of the protests after the Novi Sad canopy collapse, won the snap election of 25 October with about 53% of the vote and 140 of 250 seats, and Aleksandar Vučić conceded. The conflict between the street and the state has moved into institutions, which lowers the risk of violent confrontation. Against that, a presidential election is due by 27 December, the new majority has never governed, the US licence that keeps the NIS refinery running expires on 11 December and energy prices are pushing inflation up. A Student List government in place by March with a cooperative president would move me towards Moderate; a hostile president, a split in the majority or a halt at Pančevo would move me up.

## Key judgments
1. The National Assembly elects a government backed by the Student List majority by 31 March 2027: **very likely** (~85%). The list holds 140 seats against the 126 needed and needs no partners. The new Assembly must sit within 30 days of the final results and elect a government within 90 days after that. The risk is internal: the list has not named a prime minister, and Radomir Saičić of the Student List said lawmakers would choose one in consultation with the student movement.
2. A candidate backed by the Student List wins the presidential election due by 27 December 2026: **likely** (~70%). The list beat Vučić's United Serbia by about 13 points on a turnout near 69%, the highest since 1992. A presidential race is personal and the movement has no named candidate, so I hold this with low confidence. When Vreme checked on 27 October, acting president and speaker Ana Brnabić had not yet called the vote; the deadline for calling it is 27 November.
3. MOL completes its purchase of the Russian-held 56.15% stake in NIS by 12 November 2027: **likely** (~60%). Serbia and MOL have signed an agreement on the future management of NIS, and OFAC has licensed preparatory steps until 28 February 2027. Completion still needs a sale and purchase agreement and a separate OFAC authorisation, and OFAC has extended MOL's licence for the talks only to 11 December.
4. The EU opens Cluster 3 (competitiveness and inclusive growth) with Serbia by 12 November 2027: **unlikely** (~25%). The Commission's report of 4 November again says Serbia meets the benchmarks. In July eight member states, including the Netherlands, Sweden and Croatia, opposed opening it, citing the rule of law and Serbia's refusal to align with sanctions on Russia. A new government helps on the first point; the Student List is expected to keep the no-sanctions policy.
5. Serbia holds another early parliamentary election before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~8%). It would take a split in the 140-seat majority or a failure to form a government, both of which sit in my downside case.

## Drivers
### Political
Vučić called the snap vote and resigned as president on 27 September to run as his party's candidate for prime minister. The international observers (ODIHR, PACE and the European Parliament) said the campaign took place in "an atmosphere of profound polarization", with pressure on voters, media bias towards United Serbia and misuse of state resources, including commitments of almost one billion euro in benefits announced before and during the campaign.

The Student List has no formal leader. Its programme centres on procurement transparency, audits of state-funded projects, confiscation of illegally acquired assets and lustration. Its figures include former central bank governor Dejan Šoškić, who has criticised Western sanctions on Russia, and engineer Zoran Đajić, who has vowed that Vučić will face treason charges. Vučić has vowed to return to power and still controls an SNS of over 700,000 members with networks in local government and the civil service. A president from his camp could slow the new majority, and purges of judges and officials could reproduce the politicisation the students promised to end.

### Security
Serbia has no armed conflict and recorded 1.31 homicides per 100,000 in 2023. The risks are political violence and Kosovo. Observers reported physical attacks on polling staff, citizen observers and journalists, and unresolved allegations of surveillance with Pegasus spyware. The Commission finds only limited progress on normalisation with Kosovo, which declared independence in 2008 and which Serbia does not recognise, blames domestic politics on both sides for stalling the 2023 Agreement, and notes no meaningful accountability for the Banjska attack of September 2023. The Student List also opposes recognising Kosovo, so I expect little movement.

### Economic
On 12 November the National Bank of Serbia (NBS) raised its key rate from 5.75% to 6% to contain second-round effects of the Middle East energy shock. Inflation was 4.1% in September, and the NBS expects it to breach the 3±1.5% band in 2027 and return by the end of that year. The flash estimate puts third-quarter growth at 4.0%.

Energy is the weak point. NIS holds about three quarters of the fuel market and runs the only refinery, at Pančevo, under a US operating licence to 11 December. About 90% of Serbia's gas comes from Russia under a contract extended by annexes. In banking, a Belgrade court opened bankruptcy proceedings against Alta banka on 3 November, and the NBS revoked the exchange licence of Alta Group, imposed fines of RSD 250 million and said it would send its findings to the organised crime prosecutor; on 9 November it said the dinar was stable and Banka Poštanska štedionica was operating normally. Of about €1.6 billion earmarked for Serbia under the EU Growth Plan, no more than €168 million had been paid by early November.

### Social
The protests began after the canopy at Novi Sad railway station collapsed in November 2024, killing 16 people. The largest demonstration, on 15 March 2025, drew between 275,000 and 325,000 people by the independent Public Assembly Archive's count. The movement unites liberals and nationalists. The Commission criticised the attendance of senior state and military officials at the funeral of the convicted war criminal Ratko Mladić in September 2026, and its report counts 427 attacks, threats or cases of pressure on journalists in 2025, against 168 the year before. Youth unemployment was 20.9% in 2025.

## Indicators to watch
- Whether Brnabić calls the presidential election by 27 November, and who the candidates are.
- The date of the constitutive session, the prime minister nominee and whether the plenums accept the cabinet.
- OFAC's decision on the NIS operating licence before 11 December, and any authorisation to complete the MOL purchase before 28 February 2027.
- The NBS decision on 10 December and monthly inflation above 4.5%.
- How courts treat cases against former officials and Alta banka figures.
- A Council decision on Cluster 3, and whether about €224 million of Growth Plan money is lost at the end of 2026.
- Incidents in the north of Kosovo and steps on the 2023 Agreement.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier. Serbia scores 49 (Elevated), 68th of 203 countries, with no red flags and full data coverage. The score fell 2.5 points from 51 in 2025, its highest since the series began in 2015, and has stayed between 46 and 51 throughout.

The stability pillar (34) uses a WGI political stability score that slipped from 64.5 in 2022 to 59.9 in 2025, before the election. Governance (56) is pushed up by weak control of corruption (35.9 in 2025), the area the new majority promises to fix, though no reform has passed yet. The economic pillar (43) uses IMF figures for 2026 of 3.1% inflation and 3.3% growth; the NBS now reports 4.1% inflation in September and expects at least 3.5% growth. The model has no indicator for dependence on one sanctioned refiner or on Russian gas. The social pillar (81) carries the highest risk score and rests on displacement (3.7% of the population in 2025) and youth unemployment, figures that move slowly.

The watchlist records the 25 October election at medium significance, but the model cannot see the change of power, the presidential vote or the NIS deadline. The end of the confrontation between government and protesters lowers the risk, while the transition and the energy exposure raise it in the short term, so I keep Elevated and Stable.

## Implications
Fuel distributors and heavy energy users should plan for a gap in Pančevo supply if the NIS licence is not renewed by 11 December, and for the same risk at each later expiry until the MOL purchase closes. Companies holding state contracts should expect audits and disclosure demands, and possible reviews of contracts signed under the SNS. For EU governments, the window to reward a reformist government is early 2027, but the decision on Russia sanctions will stay in Belgrade's hands.

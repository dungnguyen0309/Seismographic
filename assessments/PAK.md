## Bottom line up front
Pakistan's political risk is High, and I expect it to stay there through November 2027. Militant attacks in the first ten months of 2026 were 53% above the same period of 2025, Pakistani and Afghan Taliban forces have fought along the border in rounds since February, and on 11 October the federal government suspended the elected Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) government of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (KP). The army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, holds the system together, and the IMF programme keeps the economy steady. A mass-casualty attack that drew India into strikes, or a Pakistani ground push into Afghanistan, would move my outlook to Deteriorating.

## Key judgments
1. Imran Khan is **very likely** (~85%) to still be in custody in Pakistan on 1 November 2027. He has been jailed since August 2023, talks with the government on his release failed in October, and on 3 November family members and lawyers were again refused meetings at Adiala jail. The likeliest way out is a deal like those under which Benazir Bhutto and Nawaz Sharif went into exile, which opposition leader Mahmood Khan Achakzai says the government is pressing on him.
2. KP is **likely** (~65%) to have a chief minister elected by its provincial assembly in office on 1 November 2027, probably not Sohail Afridi, who has been in "preventive custody" at an undisclosed place since 11 October. Governor's rule can run for six months at most, to about April 2027, but the parallel emergency has no fixed end once parliament approves it, and the prime minister's coordinator on information for KP said governor's rule could continue "until the elections" if required. I hold this with low confidence.
3. The Pakistan Institute for Conflict and Security Studies (PICSS) is **likely** (~75%) to count at least 1,200 militant attacks in the 12 months to October 2027. It counted 1,376 between January and October 2026, and talks with Kabul, facilitated by Qatar and Turkey in 2025 and with China involved this year, have produced no breakthrough on the Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan (TTP) and other groups Pakistan accuses the Taliban of sheltering.
4. Pakistan is **very likely** (~85%) to carry out air or drone strikes inside Afghanistan again before November 2027. It did so in February, late June and September 2026, the last two times in retaliation for attacks at home, and the Moscow Format meeting on Afghanistan ended on 5 October without a joint statement.
5. The IMF Executive Board is **very likely** (~85%) to complete the fourth review of Pakistan's $7 billion Extended Fund Facility (EFF) before 31 January 2027, releasing about $1.2 billion with the climate facility. Staff-level agreement was reported on 8 October, and the Fund also called on Pakistan to promptly phase out its fuel support scheme.
6. India and Pakistan are **very unlikely** (~15%) to exchange missile, drone or air strikes before November 2027. Diplomacy was still frozen in June, a year after the four-day conflict of May 2025, and a new round would most likely need a mass-casualty attack in India that New Delhi blames on Pakistan-based groups.

## Drivers
### Political
Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif's Pakistan Muslim League-Nawaz (PML-N) governs with the Pakistan Peoples Party (PPP), whose Asif Ali Zardari is president, but the army sets the terms. The 27th constitutional amendment, signed on 13 November 2025, made Munir Chief of Defence Forces over the army, air force and navy, moved constitutional cases to a new Federal Constitutional Court and gave the field marshal and the president lifetime immunity from criminal prosecution. Munir's term now runs to November 2030. Pakistan played a lead role in brokering the 8 April US-Iran ceasefire and mediated direct talks between US and Iranian officials in Islamabad on 11 and 12 April, which ended without a deal; Munir then went to Tehran as Islamabad tried to set up a second round.

Talks over the PTI's demand for Khan's release failed, and on 4 October more than 5,000 supporters set out from KP towards Islamabad, whose main access routes were blocked with shipping containers. On 11 October President Zardari proclaimed an emergency (Article 232) and governor's rule (Article 234) in KP, accusing the provincial government of failing to deal with terrorism, and both houses of parliament approved on 14 October. PPP governor Faisal Karim Kundi took over, and police reported more than 270 PTI arrests in the first days. Khan's three sisters have been held under public order law since late September. On 3 November the Peshawar High Court sent petitions over the detention of Afridi and other PTI leaders to a larger bench, after the government failed to reply. The PTI says it would welcome an alliance with Fazlur Rehman's JUI-F, and Fazl has criticised the federal government over governor's rule.

### Security
PICSS counted 1,376 militant attacks between January and October 2026 and 3,902 deaths from attacks and security operations. July was the worst month, with at least 606 dead, 372 of them in Balochistan. A suicide bombing claimed by Islamic State killed at least 31 people at a Shia mosque in Islamabad on 6 February, and on 18 September Ittehad ul Mujahideen Pakistan (IMP), a militant alliance with roots in the TTP, killed 31 in a suicide attack on a police compound in the north-west. October was quieter, with 143 attacks against 226 in September, though 70 of the month's 88 kidnappings were in Balochistan.

The conflict with Kabul comes in rounds. A Qatari-mediated ceasefire ended heavy fighting in October 2025, but in late February 2026 Pakistan bombed Kabul and Defence Minister Khawaja Asif declared "open war". Pakistan struck again after an attack in Karachi in late June, and on 24 September hit 10 targets in Kandahar, Paktia and Khost. In October the two sides clashed in Kurram, on the Paktika border and at Torkham. Crossings have been mostly closed for more than a year, and bilateral trade fell 95% in fiscal 2026.

Relations with India stayed frozen through mid-2026. India suspended the Indus Waters Treaty in April 2025 and in June moved to restrict the water flowing to Pakistan, which has warned that any disruption could be treated as an act of war.

### Economic
The IMF puts growth in fiscal 2026 (the year to June) at 3.6%, slowed by higher energy prices and supply disruptions from the Middle East conflict. Gross reserves reached about $21.5 billion at the end of September, and remittances kept the current account broadly balanced. Consumer prices rose 9.6% in the year to October, against 6.2% a year earlier. Insecurity is costing investment: production at the Reko Diq copper and gold mine in Balochistan could slip from 2028 to 2030, and Barrick's chief executive has said security must improve before full construction starts.

### Social
In the north-west, HRCP reports curfews and military deployments in North and South Waziristan, Bannu and Miranshah, police raids on PTI workers in Peshawar, Swat and Khyber, and earlier internet and mobile shutdowns. A separate secretariat now runs 12 "designated districts", mostly former tribal areas on the Afghan border, where the constitutional separation of judiciary and executive does not apply. Concern over Khan's health, in particular a right eye condition, prompted the campaign for his release.

## Indicators to watch
- PICSS monthly counts above 200 attacks.
- Pakistani strikes on Kabul or other Afghan cities, or Torkham reopening to trade.
- Whether parliament extends governor's rule when its first two months end around 11 December 2026, and the high court's ruling on Afridi's detention.
- Family and lawyer access to Khan at Adiala, and news of his health.
- IMF board approval of the fourth review, the end of fuel support, and consumer inflation above 12%.
- A mass-casualty attack in Indian-administered Kashmir.

## Why my rating differs from the model
I rate Pakistan one tier above the model, which gives it 59 (Elevated), 28th of 203 countries, with one red flag for war-level violence. The score fell 2.3 points on last year, back into the Elevated tier it held from 2015 to 2022, after three years in High (63 in 2023 and 2024, 62 in 2025). It sits just below the High threshold, so the gap is small, but I think the latest fall points the wrong way.

The stability pillar (59) lags events. Battle deaths (0.61 per 100,000) are for 2024, before the October 2025 border fighting and the 3,902 deaths PICSS has counted in 2026, and the political stability indicator (about 31 on the app's scale, down from about 34 in 2022) stops at 2025. The governance pillar (80) uses 2025 WGI scores that predate the suspension of KP's elected government. The economic pillar (55) carries IMF figures for 2026, including 3.6% growth, a fiscal deficit of 2.6% of GDP and debt of 69.8% of GDP; I trust this part most, though its 7.1% inflation sits below October's 9.6%. The social pillar (32, the lowest of the four) rests on 2025 youth unemployment of 9.6% and a displaced share of 0.06%, which miss the curfews in the border districts. Data coverage is complete, and the app's watchlist holds no Pakistan events.

## Implications
Firms with staff or assets in KP and Balochistan should plan for attacks on police and infrastructure, kidnappings, and curfews or internet shutdowns without notice. Trade through Torkham cannot be relied on before a settlement with Kabul, and Balochistan mining projects should price in delays like Reko Diq's. Lenders can treat the IMF programme as an anchor while watching fuel subsidies and oil prices. Governments dealing with Islamabad should expect security and Afghanistan policy to be set by the army chief.

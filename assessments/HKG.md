## Bottom line up front
Hong Kong's political risk is Moderate and stable. The economy grew 5.1% in the first three quarters of 2026 and the streets are quiet, but national security cases now reach journalists and the US Hong Kong Autonomy Act (HKAA) still covers the city's leaders. The Wang Fuk Court fire report, released on 29 October, cost the housing secretary her post eight months before John Lee's term as Chief Executive ends. A collapse of the US-China trade truce, now extended to 10 January 2027, followed by new US sanctions would move my outlook to Deteriorating.

## Key judgments
1. There is a **roughly even chance** (~50%) that John Lee is sworn in for a second term as Chief Executive on 1 July 2027. Beijing's approval of Housing Secretary Winnie Ho's removal on 3 November keeps the blame for the fire at bureau level, but the inquiry faulted four government bodies and Lee would not say on 29 October whether he will run. I hold this with low confidence, because Beijing decides and signals little.
2. Jimmy Lai is **very unlikely** (~10%) to leave prison before November 2027. He decided in March not to appeal his conviction or his 20-year sentence, and on 15 May Donald Trump said Xi Jinping's response was "not positive" when he raised the case on his visit to China. Three prosecution witnesses at his trial have reportedly been released early, but Lai, 78, pleaded not guilty.
3. The US is **very unlikely** (~15%) to impose new sanctions on a Hong Kong official or bank before November 2027. On 17 July Washington let its 2020 national emergency on Hong Kong lapse and moved 39 people, Lee among them, from its main sanctions list to a menu-based list under the HKAA, a step Hong Kong linked to the China-US trade consultations. The HKAA itself stays in force and could be used again if the trade truce fails.
4. Real GDP growth for the first three quarters of 2027, in the advance estimates due around November 2027, is **likely** (~65%) to be 2.5% or higher year on year. Goods exports carried 2026, rising 30.4% in real terms in the third quarter against 2.8% for private consumption, which sets a high base; the government names Middle East tensions and tighter monetary policy at major central banks as external risks.
5. The Rating and Valuation Department's private domestic price index is **likely** (~60%) to be higher for September 2027 than its provisional September 2026 reading of 320.8. Prices rose 9.3% in the year to September and negative-equity mortgages fell from 4,356 to 3,819 between end-June and end-September, though the index has slipped from 323.4 in June and US rates are on hold at 3.75% to 4%.

## Drivers
### Political
Turnout in the Legislative Council election on 7 December 2025, 11 days after the fire, was 31.9% in the geographical constituencies. Polls in 31 contested Election Committee subsectors on 22 November will fill 723 seats. The new committee, which elects the Chief Executive, takes office on 1 February 2027, and Lee's term ends on 30 June. A review committee vets candidates on the opinion of the Committee for Safeguarding National Security.

The fire at Wang Fuk Court in Tai Po on 26 November 2025 killed 168 people. Three days later local media reported that national security police had arrested a student, Miles Kwan, on suspicion of sedition over a petition demanding an independent inquiry. The inquiry's report points to suspected non-fire-retardant scaffolding nets and flammable foam boards, and faults four bodies, among them the Housing Bureau's Independent Checking Unit; government departments had received 180 complaints and enquiries before the fire. The government has proposed a bid-rigging offence and amendments to six ordinances, and has asked the Public Service Commission to investigate individual officers.

### Security
There is no armed conflict, and the model records 0.38 homicides per 100,000 (2023). Security risk in Hong Kong comes from national security enforcement. On 11 September former Tiananmen vigil organisers Lee Cheuk-yan and Chow Hang-tung were jailed for seven years and for seven years and three months for inciting subversion. On 30 September police arrested Tang Ho-wing of the online outlet Boom News, the first publicly known sedition arrest of a journalist under the 2024 Safeguarding National Security Ordinance (the Article 23 law), according to the Hong Kong Journalists Association. Joshua Wong, who admitted conspiring with Nathan Law and others to seek foreign sanctions, is due for sentencing on 5 November. Under a regulation made on 9 June, a criminal case becomes a national security case once the Chief Executive certifies that the act involved concerns national security.

### Economic
Third-quarter GDP grew 5.2% year on year, and a revised 2026 forecast is due on 13 November. Retail, restaurants, construction and offices outside the main business districts are still weak. The government ran a deficit of HK$101.3 billion before bond proceeds from April to September and held fiscal reserves of HK$681.7 billion at end-September. The model adds inflation of 1.9% and a current-account surplus of 13.8% of GDP (both 2026).

### Social
Unemployment rose to 3.9% in July to September, and the Financial Secretary says construction, catering and retail face more pressure; the model puts youth unemployment at 9.0% (2025). The government says the average wait for subsidised rental housing fell from 6.1 to 4.8 years under Ho.

## Indicators to watch
- Turnout in the Election Committee polls on 22 November, and whether Lee seeks a second term.
- Whether the trade truce is extended beyond 10 January 2027.
- New HKAA designations, or a reversal of the July changes.
- Joshua Wong's sentence, and sedition arrests of journalists or of critics around the fire's anniversary on 26 November.
- The price index falling three months in a row, or negative-equity loans back above 4,356.
- Quarterly goods export growth below 10%.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating is one tier above the model's: a composite of 18 (Low), 191st of 203, with no red flags and full data coverage. It fell 1 point on last year and sits just under the Moderate threshold of 20, a tier it last held in 2024.

The stability pillar (12) is the lowest of the four, and a rise in political stability from about 73 in 2022 to about 79 in 2025 pushed it lower. In Hong Kong that score measures the absence of protest under the national security laws, and voice and accountability (about 48 in 2025) is the main indicator that registers the cost. Government effectiveness (about 84 in 2025) predates the fire report, and nothing in the model captures exposure to US sanctions law.

The economic pillar (27) uses growth of 3.8% for 2026, below the 5.1% recorded so far, while the social pillar (17) uses unemployment of 2.8% (2025), below the 3.9% now reported. Reserves (5.69 months of imports) date from 2023, and the app's watchlist holds no Hong Kong events. These gaps point in opposite directions, so my Moderate rating rests on the legal and external risks.

## Implications
Most legal risk in Hong Kong attaches to what people say and publish. Seeking foreign sanctions is a crime, as Wong's case shows, and sedition cases now reach journalists, so firms should review staff, media and social media exposure. Banks should keep screening against OFAC's Non-SDN Menu-Based Sanctions List, which now holds Lee and Security Secretary Chris Tang; assets blocked before 14 July 2026 stay blocked. Property buyers carry US interest-rate risk through the currency peg. The UK has a direct interest in Lai, a British citizen, whose case Prime Minister Keir Starmer raised with Xi in Beijing in January.

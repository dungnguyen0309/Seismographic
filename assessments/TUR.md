## Bottom line up front
I rate Türkiye's political risk Elevated and stable over the next 12 months, in line with the model (53, 47th of 203 countries). Two trends roughly cancel out. The courts have stripped the main opposition of its leadership and kept Ekrem İmamoğlu in jail, while the PKK has said it will end its armed struggle and disband, and parliament has passed a law to reintegrate its members. The main short-term risk is economic: inflation of 29.77% in October, an investment fund collapse in September and oil prices pushed up by the Iran war. A conviction that bars İmamoğlu from the 2028 presidential race together with a run on the lira would move me to High.

## Key judgments
1. İmamoğlu is still in detention or serving a sentence on 12 November 2027: **very likely** (~85%). He has been held for more than 19 months, and his trial with 435 co-defendants, which opened on 9 March, reached his own defence only in October. Release would most likely come only in my upside scenario.
2. Parliament votes to renew elections under Article 116, or a general election is held, before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~15%). The People's Alliance has 326 of the 360 votes needed, and Erdoğan's chief adviser has named 16 April 2028 as the most likely election date and put the chance of a vote in late 2027 as low.
3. The government announces that PKK disarmament has been verified and begins applying the August 2026 law's deferral mechanism before 12 November 2027: **likely** (~65%). The PKK has left four camps in northern Iraq and all parliamentary groups are being consulted on a monitoring committee. Confidence is moderate: verification rests with the security forces and the intelligence service (MIT), and I have no view of their assessments.
4. TurkStat's annual CPI for October 2027 is below 25%: **unlikely** (~40%). Inflation fell only three points in the past year, from 32.87% to 29.77%, and on 12 November the central bank raised its 2026 year-end forecast again, to 29.5%.
5. The central bank's policy rate is below 36% on 12 November 2027: **likely** (~70%). The bank resumed cutting in October and says domestic demand is weak. Only my downside scenario, a lira crisis that forces hikes, rules this out.

## Drivers
### Political
İmamoğlu, jailed since March 2025, has been on trial since 9 March 2026 on corruption charges carrying up to 2,430 years in prison. The case covers 436 defendants, 53 of them held pending trial. In his defence in October he said the case was meant to stop him challenging Erdoğan, and prosecutors opened a new investigation into his remarks about prosecutors. A separate case over the annulment of his university diploma lost at first instance and is on appeal. The European Commission notes that if the annulment stands, he cannot run for president.

On 21 May the 36th Civil Chamber of the Ankara Regional Court of Justice annulled the CHP congress of November 2023 that had elected Özgür Özel and reinstated Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu. Özel's appeal was not heard before the judicial recess began on 20 July. On 24 July he declared Yeni Parti with 90 MPs, leaving the CHP with 44 seats, and İmamoğlu later joined the new party. Since 1 September 2025, 22 CHP mayors have been suspended or arrested.

The fund scandal has reached the AK Party. Its deputy chair, Fatma Betül Sayan Kaya, resigned in late September over alleged share sales before the collapse and was arrested on 5 November; she denies wrongdoing. On 6 October Devlet Bahçeli asked Erdoğan to run again in 2028, which needs a parliamentary vote to renew elections.

### Security
The PKK announced in May 2025 that it would end its armed struggle and disband. Parliament passed the framework law on 10 August with 468 votes, including those of the AK Party, the MHP and the DEM Party. Its deferral of investigations, prosecutions and sentences applies only after verification that the PKK has dissolved and handed over its weapons, and it excludes intentional killing. Security sources reported in October that the PKK had withdrawn from four camps in the Metina area of northern Iraq. Speaker Numan Kurtulmuş said on 28 October that parliament would soon set up a monitoring committee.

In Syria, Ankara backed Syrian government operations against Kurdish-controlled areas from January 2026 and welcomed the SDF's decision in August to dissolve. The larger external risk is the Iran war, which began with US and Israeli strikes on 28 February. Iran is blockading the Strait of Hormuz, and Qatar and Pakistan lead the efforts to end the war.

### Economic
On 28 August the Capital Markets Board tightened fund rules, and withdrawals hit funds holding thinly traded shares. After a market fall on 15 and 16 September the board ordered 131 funds run by seven firms, with more than 450,000 investors, to be wound up. The justice minister, Akın Gürlek, said in November that over 430 people were under investigation and 112 had been arrested. Over the five weeks to 16 October gross reserves fell by $9.6 billion to $169.1 billion, and net reserves by $10.7 billion to $51.5 billion.

The central bank cut its policy rate from 37% to 36% on 22 October, its first move since January, and meets next on 10 December. On 12 November Governor Fatih Karahan raised the 2026 year-end inflation forecast to 29.5% from 28%, citing the Iran war and oil, and kept an interim target of 21% for the end of 2027. Brent reached $112 a barrel on 22 October. The Commission notes that the budget deficit stopped narrowing in 2026 because of support measures against the Middle East shock.

### Social
Monthly inflation was 2.58% in October, and housing and utility prices rose 40.24% on a year earlier. Türkiye hosts about 2.3 million refugees; more than 720,000 Syrians had returned home by August 2026. In October the state deposit insurance fund (TMSF) seized Halk TV, a channel that covers the opposition, and took it off the air.

## Indicators to watch
- The central bank's decision on 10 December and the inflation report in early 2027.
- Weekly net reserves, especially a fall below $40 billion.
- The appeal court ruling on İmamoğlu's diploma and any interim verdict in the municipality case.
- The Court of Cassation's ruling on the CHP congress.
- Formation of the parliamentary monitoring committee and any official statement that PKK disarmament has been verified.
- A parliamentary motion to renew elections and the size of the vote for it.
- Brent prices and shipping through Hormuz.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: 53 (Elevated), 47th of 203 countries, with full data coverage and two red flags, active armed conflict and inflation of 20% or more. The score is 0.4 points above last year's 53.0. In the app's history, which starts in 2015, Türkiye has been Elevated every year, between 48.2 in 2015 and 54.7 in 2023.

The pillars hide movements in opposite directions. Governance (64) is the highest, but its World Bank scores for 2025 (voice and accountability 37.28, rule of law 45.25) predate the annulment of the CHP congress and the seizure of Halk TV, so it understates current risk. The stability pillar (48) leans the other way. Its armed conflict flag rests on UCDP data for 2024 (0.04 battle deaths per 100,000 people), and political stability improved from 48.97 in 2022 to 50.17 in 2025. Since then the PKK has begun leaving its camps in northern Iraq.

The economic pillar (52) uses the IMF's 2026 average inflation of 31.2%, close to the central bank's 29.5% year-end forecast, and reserves of 5.13 months of imports for 2025, before net reserves fell $10.7 billion in the five weeks to 16 October. The model has no watchlist events for Türkiye, so neither the fund crisis nor the Iran war registers. Social risk (51) rests on unemployment of 8.52% and youth unemployment of 16.04% in 2025. Adding the political deterioration and subtracting the lower violence leaves me within the Elevated band, well short of the High threshold of 60.

## Implications
Investors should treat legal action as a market risk: the Commission says the court case against the main opposition party raised the country risk premium and triggered large capital outflows. Firms with Turkish partners linked to the opposition should allow for seizure through trustees, as happened to Tele1 in 2025 and Halk TV in 2026. Energy-intensive businesses should plan for high oil prices while Hormuz stays closed. The Commission's report of 4 November found further backsliding on democracy and the judiciary, so I do not expect European governments to move on the Customs Union update Ankara wants before 2028.

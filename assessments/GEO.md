## Bottom line up front
I rate Georgia's political risk Elevated, the same tier as the model (49, 65th of 203 countries), though nearer the top of it, and I expect it to worsen over the next 12 months. Georgian Dream faces no national vote before the 2028 parliamentary election and, in the European Commission's words, is "enabling further state capture". The main driver is its lawsuit to ban the five main opposition parties, which the Constitutional Court must decide by 24 June 2027. A ban followed by an EU suspension of visa-free travel for ordinary Georgians, or by wider Western sanctions, would move me to High; a ruling that drops or narrows the ban would bring the outlook back to Stable.

## Key judgments
1. The Constitutional Court bans the United National Movement (UNM) and at least two of the other four parties in the case by 24 June 2027: **likely** (~70%). The suit was filed by 88 ruling-party MPs, the European Commission says judicial independence remains "seriously weakened", and by 11 November the court had granted only one of about 14 defence motions, and that in part. Two things keep me below 80%: Judge Eva Gotsiridze told the plaintiffs that their test for an "anti-constitutional" party was too loose, and four of the seven judges hearing the case must agree. Confidence is moderate.
2. Irakli Kobakhidze is still prime minister and Georgian Dream still governs alone on 12 November 2027: **very likely** (~90%). The daily protests that followed the October 2024 election continue, but by August 2026 the weekly Saturday rally drew nearly a thousand people. The jailing of former prime minister Irakli Garibashvili in January shows that Bidzina Ivanishvili can discipline his own camp.
3. The EU decides to suspend visa-free travel for all holders of ordinary Georgian passports before 12 November 2027: **unlikely** (~20%). The Commission said on 4 November that Georgia's failure to address its democracy concerns is "putting at risk the visa-free regime more generally", and EU foreign ministers will discuss Georgia in January. Member states must approve such a step, as they did for the narrower suspension for diplomatic, service and official passports announced on 6 March 2026.
4. The United States lifts its sanctions on Ivanishvili before 12 November 2027: **unlikely** (~20%). Kobakhidze says relations have "considerably improved" and that he raised the strategic partnership with Donald Trump and Marco Rubio at the UN General Assembly. But in March 2026 the administration renewed the emergency under which Ivanishvili is listed, and I have found no sign of a delisting process.
5. Geostat's preliminary estimate puts real GDP growth for January to September 2027 above 4%: **likely** (~75%). Growth averaged 7.4% in the first nine months of 2026 and the IMF projects 5.7% for 2027. A shock to remittances, tourism or the Kulevi refinery in the downside scenario could pull it lower.

## Drivers
### Political
Georgian Dream has governed since 2012. It won the local elections of 4 October 2025, which much of the opposition boycotted; its candidate Kakha Kaladze took 71.58% in Tbilisi on preliminary results, and the EU criticised the government for not inviting OSCE/ODIHR observers in time. That evening, at a rally its organisers called a "peaceful revolution", protesters broke through the gates of the presidential palace. By June 2026, 64 people had been charged, and on 7 May a court gave five defendants, including the opera singer Paata Burchuladze, seven years each. Former UNM chair Levan Khabeishvili received two and a half years for sabotage and inciting a coup in May.

The ban case opened in Batumi on 21 October. It targets the UNM, Coalition for Change, Lelo-Strong Georgia, For Georgia and the Federalists. Seven judges are hearing it; Judge Manana Kobakhidze resigned on 13 October after the court rejected her request to recuse herself. On 11 November the court's chairman, Revaz Nadaraia, expelled Coalition for Change leaders Nika Gvaramia and Maia Kopaleishvili from the courtroom. The suit also blames the UNM for the 2008 war.

The ruling camp shows strain at the top. Garibashvili, once a favourite of Ivanishvili, is serving five years for money laundering and faces a second, bribery case linked to former defence minister Juansher Burchuladze; from prison he calls its closed-door trial "shadow justice". On 10 November Metropolitan Iov of Urbnisi said in a sermon that the government had "completely exhausted its potential". According to the Centre for Social Justice, the Church received more than 283 million lari from central and municipal budgets in 2020-2024. Georgian Dream MP Levan Machavariani replied that the metropolitan's political views were out of touch with reality.

### Security
The model records no battle deaths for 2024, and the risk of violence comes from the policing of protest. In the ban case the plaintiffs' representative, MP Archil Gorduladze, said about 170 police officers were injured in November and December 2024. Russia recognises Abkhazia and South Ossetia as independent states; Georgia regards them as occupied territory and has no diplomatic relations with Moscow. On 21 October Kobakhidze named First Deputy Foreign Minister George Zurabashvili his special representative for relations with Russia, a post vacant since December 2025. On 9 November Sergei Shoigu, secretary of Russia's Security Council, said the Georgian authorities had "successfully thwarted an attempted coup" in 2024.

### Economic
Growth is strong but slowing: 6.0% in September against a nine-month average of 7.4%. The National Bank of Georgia (NBG) has held its rate at 8.25% since raising it in May, and in October lifted its inflation forecasts to 5.4% for 2026 and 3.5% for 2027. The IMF expects the current account deficit to widen to 5.0% of GDP in 2026 and projects public debt at 33.6% of GDP.

Sanctions are the exposure to watch. In its 21st Russia package of 24 July the EU listed the Kulevi refinery, Georgia's only one, with a transaction ban deferred by six months; its operator, Black Sea Petroleum, said it would replace Russian crude with oil from Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan. The UK sanctioned the pro-government channels Imedi and POSTV on 24 February. Georgia's alignment with EU statements on sanctions decisions fell to 38% on 1 September, from 40% in 2025.

### Social
Youth unemployment was 30.26% in 2025. The protest movement passed 615 days in August; rights groups count nearly 60 political prisoners, and attending a rally can cost a 5,000-lari fine. The Commission wants the 2024 foreign agents law, the 2025 FARA and amendments to the grants law repealed. The Communications Commission has barred the critical channels Formula and TV Pirveli from 93 words and phrases, including "regime" and "political prisoners", and on 1 October fined Formula 10,000 lari.

## Indicators to watch
- The Constitutional Court's ruling, due by 24 June 2027, and whether any judge dissents.
- The EU Foreign Affairs Council discussion on Georgia in January 2027.
- The next report under the visa suspension mechanism (the last came in December 2025).
- The Kulevi transaction ban taking effect in late January 2027, and whether Brussels delists the refinery.
- Any change in the US designation of Ivanishvili.
- Further sermons against the government from senior clergy.
- Communications Commission action against the licences of Formula or TV Pirveli.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: a composite of 49 (Elevated), 65th of 203, up 0.2 on last year, with full data coverage. The score has stayed between 43 and 50 in every year of the app's history, which starts in 2015, and has risen from 44 in 2023. The stability pillar (40) carries a red flag for political stability, which fell from 63.83 in 2022 to 56.93 in 2025.

I place Georgia nearer 55 than 49 because the governance pillar (43) looks generous. Its WGI inputs for 2025 give voice and accountability 51.14 and rule of law 60.2, while the Commission says Georgian Dream is enabling "further state capture" and that judicial independence remains seriously weakened. The app's watchlist holds no events for Georgia, so the ban case, the visa threat and the Kulevi listing do not register. The economic pillar (44) uses a 2026 current account deficit of 3.5% of GDP, smaller than the IMF's 5.0%, and reserves of 3.02 months of imports in 2025. I found no 2026 event that would add to the displacement red flag (8.81% of the population in 2025), and the homicide figure dates from 2019. These gaps add points, but not the ten or so needed for High while growth is strong and debt low.

## Implications
Firms that trade fuel or refined products should check exposure to Kulevi before the EU ban takes effect, and advertisers and suppliers of Imedi and POSTV face UK sanctions risk. NGOs and foreign donors should assume FARA and the grants law will be enforced, with prison terms for breaches. Investors can count on strong growth and low public debt, but a wider current account deficit and the risk of an EU visa suspension would hit remittances, tourism and the lari together.

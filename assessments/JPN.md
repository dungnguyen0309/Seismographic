## Bottom line up front
Japan's political risk is Low and stable over the next 12 months. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's Liberal Democratic Party (LDP) won 316 of 465 lower house seats on 8 February 2026, enough to override the upper house, where her coalition is in the minority, and no national election is due before 2028. The risks are an LDP leadership contest in 2027, the cost of a food tax cut, dear oil and the dispute with China over her November 2025 remarks on Taiwan. A bond sell-off that forced a change of course, or a deadly clash with Chinese forces, would move me to Moderate.

## Key judgments
1. Takaichi is **likely** (~75%) to still be prime minister on 1 November 2027. The threat is the LDP leadership election in 2027: she has no faction, and Jiji's approval reading fell for a fifth month to 42.4% in October. Other October polls mostly had her near or above 50%, and few lawmakers will challenge the leader who won the largest single-party majority since the war.
2. The 1% consumption tax rate on food is **likely** (~75%) to be in force on 1 May 2027. The LDP aims to enact it before the Diet session ends on 12 December and can override the upper house; the obstacles are delay there and LDP doubts about a revenue loss of about ¥5 trillion a year.
3. The Bank of Japan's (BoJ) policy rate is **very likely** (~85%) to be 1.5% or higher on 1 November 2027, up from 1.25%. The Bank says it will keep raising, and one board member proposed 1.5% on 30 October. A pause would most likely be blamed on the tax cut, which the Bank estimates will take 1.3 points off core inflation in fiscal 2027.
4. China is **very unlikely** (~10%) to lift its January 2026 ban on dual-use exports to Japan before November 2027. Beijing has taken its campaign into wartime history with Russian support, and in mid-October it was unclear whether Xi Jinping would meet Takaichi at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on 18 and 19 November.
5. There is **almost no chance** (~3%) of a clash between Japanese and Chinese forces that kills anyone before November 2027. Chinese naval activity around the First Island Chain has grown, but neither government gains from a shooting incident.

## Drivers
### Political
Takaichi has governed since 21 October 2025 with the Japan Innovation Party, whose plan to cut lower house seats has caused tension in the LDP. In February the LDP gained 118 seats and the Centrist Reform Alliance (the Constitutional Democrats and Komeito) fell to 49. In the upper house the government needs opposition votes bill by bill, and the Democratic Party for the People opposes the tax cut.

Support has eased. Kyodo's approval reading fell from 64.4% at the start of her term to 49.1% in October, while the Yomiuri had 57%. Agriculture Minister Kazuo Yana, in effect dismissed on 14 October over alleged breaches of election law, had reportedly bragged about cutting funding for municipalities that did not support his February campaign; he denied breaking the law. Some suspect Takaichi missed an online G7 summit in early October for a dinner with LDP Vice President Taro Aso and other party executives. Aso's group helped her win the leadership and could matter again in 2027.

### Security
North Korea fired about ten short-range ballistic missiles on 29 October; Japan's defence ministry said a possible ballistic missile fell outside its exclusive economic zone. Vladimir Putin visited Iturup, one of the southern Kuril islands that Russia administers and Japan claims, in August, and on 13 October the FSB arrested a Hokkaido Shimbun journalist on Sakhalin on espionage charges.

China is the larger problem. In November 2025 Takaichi told parliament that a Chinese attack on Taiwan could be a "survival-threatening situation" for Japan, the threshold for collective self-defence. Beijing, which regards Taiwan as part of its territory, banned dual-use exports to Japan on 6 January, and a China-Russia statement in May accused Japan of remilitarising. Japan reached defence spending of 2% of GDP two years early and expects to finish revising its three security documents by the end of 2026.

### Economic
The BoJ raised its rate to 1.0% in June and 1.25% in September and held it on 30 October. It expects core inflation of 2.5% in fiscal 2026 and 2.6% in fiscal 2027, with growth of 0.7% in both, and assumes Dubai crude, near $105 a barrel after the US and Israeli war on Iran, falls to about $70 by the end of fiscal 2028. The yen was 157.80 to the dollar on 2 November. The 10-year bond yield hit a 30-year high of 2.930% on 17 August amid doubts over funding the tax cut, which lowers the food rate from 8% to 1% for two years from April 2027, and was 2.817% on 2 November. US tariffs of 10% and 12.5%, imposed in July on 60 partners including Japan, add pressure.

### Social
People aged 65 and over were a record 29.6% of the population in September, and unemployment was 2.5% in 2025 (3.9% for 15 to 24-year-olds). Prices are the main grievance: 64% disapproved of the government's handling of inflation in an August Nippon TV poll.

## Indicators to watch
- The upper house vote on the tax cut before 12 December, or a lower house override.
- Jiji's cabinet approval below 35%, from 42.4% in October.
- A Takaichi-Xi meeting at APEC on 18 and 19 November.
- A BoJ rise to 1.5%, or a pause blamed on the tax cut.
- The 10-year yield above 3.5%, the yen weaker than 165 to the dollar, or Finance Ministry intervention.
- New Chinese export controls on Japan, or any easing of the January ban.
- The date of the LDP leadership election and any challenger.

## Implications
Firms that sell to Japanese defence industry or rely on Chinese inputs should plan for the dual-use ban to outlast 2027; Teikoku Databank counted 10,118 Japanese companies in China in June, over 20% fewer than in 2024. Restaurants lose out because dine-in meals stay taxed at 10%, and 46.9% of them expected lower sales in an August survey. Investors should expect further rate rises, higher long-term yields and BoJ sales of ETFs of about ¥860 billion a year at book value from January 2027.

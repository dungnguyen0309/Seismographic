## Bottom line up front
I rate Belarus's political risk Elevated, the same tier as the model (51, 59th of 203 countries), and expect it to stay there over the next 12 months. Alexander Lukashenko has no election to face and keeps tight control at home, and prisoner releases have bought sanctions relief from Washington but none from the EU. Most of the risk comes from outside: Russian missile units based in Belarus, drones and smuggling balloons crossing into Lithuania, and dependence on a slowing Russian market. An incident from Belarusian territory that killed people in a NATO state would move me to High.

## Key judgments
1. Lukashenko is still president on 12 November 2027: **very likely** (~92%). He won a seventh term in January 2025 with 86.82% of the vote by the Central Election Commission's count, no national election is due within the horizon, and in 2024 he took on the leadership of the All-Belarusian People's Assembly, which a Wilson Center analyst reads as preparation for a possible transfer of power. Sudden incapacity is the main way this fails.
2. Viasna counts fewer than 600 political prisoners on 1 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~15%). It still counted 887 at the start of November 2026. A drop of that size would need a release on the scale of March's 250 or larger, and US envoy John Coale's hope in September of "hundreds" more had not been met by early November.
3. The EU renews its Belarus sanctions regime beyond 28 February 2027 with the potash measures intact: **very likely** (~88%). The Council extended it unchanged in February 2026, Lithuania turned down an unofficial US request in March about potash transit routes, and the chair of the Lithuanian parliament's foreign affairs committee said on 21 September that there was no reason to lift sanctions. The residual risk is US pressure on Brussels and the need for unanimity.
4. Lithuania closes its border crossings with Belarus to general traffic again before 12 November 2027: **unlikely** (~25%). Balloons and drones keep crossing, and the Lithuanian foreign ministry threatened new restrictions in April. I have found no report of a closure since the crossings reopened in November 2025, probably in part because Belarus held Lithuanian lorries in retaliation until the end of March 2026. The figure sits a little above my 20% downside scenario because Vilnius could also close the crossings briefly over balloons without a wider crisis.
5. Belarusian forces take part in combat against Ukraine before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~5%). In September Ukraine's military intelligence chief saw no sign that Minsk was preparing to enter the war and rated the threat from Belarus as low.
6. Belstat's estimate of real GDP growth for January to September 2027 is below 2%: **likely** (~60%). Growth was 1.3% in 2025 and 2.0% in the first nine months of 2026, short of the government's target of at least 2.8%, and about 65% of exports go to a Russian market where the revenue of Belarus's main state companies fell 17% in 2025.

## Drivers
### Political
The Central Election Commission gave Lukashenko 86.82% in the vote of 26 January 2025, and the last parliamentary election was in February 2024. Viasna still counted 887 political prisoners at the start of November, after more than a year of releases, and Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya says engagement with Minsk has to end the repression as well as free prisoners.

Lukashenko is using prisoners to buy his way out of isolation. Since Trump returned to office in January 2025, US-brokered deals have freed hundreds of political prisoners. In March 2026 Minsk released 250 in return for the lifting of US sanctions on two state banks, the Finance Ministry and the main potash producers, and in September it freed 25 more in return for US sanctions relief for Lakokraska and Bellesbumprom. Trump announced a potash deal on 21 September. Poland is also talking again: Andrzej Poczobut was freed in April in a five-for-five prisoner exchange brokered by Coale, and in September Radosław Sikorski met Maxim Ryzhenkov in New York, the first meeting of the two foreign ministers since 2016. On 3 November Feridun Sinirlioglu made the first visit to Belarus by an OSCE secretary-general since 2019. Moscow has largely let this happen, apart from a February warning from its foreign intelligence service about a supposed Western coup plot.

### Security
iSANS estimates about 2,200 Russian personnel in Belarus, including S-400 battalions at Mozyr and Zyabrovka. A Russian defence ministry photo showed an Oreshnik system installed at an undisclosed location in Belarus on 30 December 2025, and Ukraine's intelligence chief said in an interview published on 7 September that units of the 101st Missile Brigade were deployed there. iSANS has found launch pads at a facility near Krichev but no reliable confirmation that the Oreshnik system itself is deployed. In September Russia's foreign ministry called non-strategic nuclear weapons in Belarus an integral element of the Union State's security.

iSANS counted at least 124 drone incursions into Belarusian airspace in September, 112 of them Russian. On the night of 10 to 11 November a drone of unknown origin crossed from Belarus into Lithuania, and NATO jets twice failed to shoot it down near Biržai; about ten balloon sightings the same night led to restrictions on landings that affected three flights. Belarus warned Vilnius in advance about objects near the border, which the head of Lithuania's crisis centre welcomed.

### Economic
The economy is slowing as Russian demand weakens. Belstat put growth at 2.0% for January to September 2026. Lukashenko has said that 65% of exports go to Russia, up from 45% in 2019, and called the Russian market "oversaturated". MAZ truck sales there fell 43% in 2025, and industrial inventories reached 80.3% of average monthly output. The goods trade deficit widened to $5.96 billion in 2025.

The National Bank cut its refinancing rate to 9.25% in June, may cut further in 2027 and plans to lower its inflation target from 7% this year to no more than 6% next year. US relief helps at the margin: the first 30,000 tonnes of potash were reportedly due in New Orleans in October. EU sanctions, which list 312 people and 57 organisations, and Lithuania's ban on potash transit still block the most efficient export route, through Klaipėda.

### Social
Unemployment is low (3.38% in 2025, 9.18% for 15 to 24-year-olds) while the labour force shrinks, and the authorities have failed to attract migrant workers. Reservist call-ups are growing: iSANS counted at least 12,845 people called up for training in 2026 by the end of September.

## Indicators to watch
- The next Coale visit and the size of any release.
- Viasna's monthly political prisoner count against 887.
- The EU Council's decision on renewal before 28 February 2027.
- Lithuanian airport restrictions caused by balloons, and any government decision to close the border crossings.
- iSANS reports of Oreshnik launchers or nuclear storage work, and of Belarusian troop numbers in the south.
- Belstat's quarterly GDP figures and the stock of industrial inventories.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model's tier: 51 (Elevated), 59th of 203, with full data coverage, no red flags and no watchlist events. The score fell 1.1 points on last year. It has been Elevated in every year of the app's history, which starts in 2015 at 53, with a low of 45 in 2018 and 2019 and a peak of 54 in 2022.

The governance pillar (84) carries the score, and I agree with it: voice and accountability is 25.81 and rule of law 38.41 in 2025. The stability pillar (38) looks too low to me. The WGI political stability score rose from 54.17 in 2022 to 62.57 in 2025, battle deaths are zero (2024) and the homicide figure dates from 2019. None of these captures Russian missile infrastructure, Russian drones crossing Belarusian airspace or the incidents on the Lithuanian border.

The economic pillar (49) fits recent data: the model's 1.9% growth for 2026 is close to Belstat's 2.0% for the first nine months, and inflation is 5.1% (2026). It does not measure dependence on Russia, and reserves of 3.03 months of imports (2025) leave little cushion. The social pillar (26) reads low unemployment as strength when it partly reflects a shrinking workforce. The regime's control and the thaw with Washington offset these gaps, so Elevated and Stable hold.

## Implications
Companies should treat US and EU sanctions as diverging: the US has lifted its sanctions on Belaruskali and the Belarusian Potash Company, but EU sanctions on Belarusian potash remain. Hauliers and logistics firms in Lithuania should plan for border closures at short notice and for retaliation like the holding of lorries from late 2025 to March 2026. Airlines flying into Lithuania should expect more night-time landing restrictions this winter.

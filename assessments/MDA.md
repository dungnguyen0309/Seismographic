## Bottom line up front
I rate Moldova's political risk Elevated, the same tier as the model (44, 102nd of 203 countries), with a stable outlook. The Party of Action and Solidarity (PAS) has a parliamentary majority with no regular election due before 2029, and EU accession talks are moving fast, but the winter will be hard: inflation reached 10.8% in October after tariff rises, Transnistria's gas is assured only to the end of 2026, and Russian drones and missiles keep crossing Moldovan airspace. A gas cut-off in Transnistria that spreads to the right bank, or a second change of government within a year, would turn my outlook to Deteriorating.

## Key judgments
1. Vasile Tofan is **likely** (~70%) to still be prime minister on 12 November 2027. PAS won 55 of 101 seats in 2025, so an early election is in its hands. The risk is another sudden exit: Alexandru Munteanu resigned on 3 July after eight months, amid media reports of inflated salaries and the hiring of President Maia Sandu's relatives at state agencies. Tofan inherits the same majority and the same unpopular reforms.
2. The EU opens the last two negotiating clusters with Moldova by 31 March 2027: **likely** (~70%). Moldova has opened four of six clusters since June, the Commission says it meets the conditions for the rest, and it expects Moldova to close 25 of 33 chapters in 2027. Hungary blocked the procedural step on 20 October without explanation, but diplomats expect the block to be temporary and officials expect the clusters to open in December.
3. Transnistria loses its gas supply for at least seven days in a row before 12 November 2027: **unlikely** (~30%). Gas reaches the region through a Hungarian company with Chisinau's permission to transit, Moldovan authorities say supply is covered only to the end of 2026, and Chisinau considers the scheme unreliable. I keep this below even because Moscow wants the region kept afloat: Vladimir Putin decorated its leader on 4 November. The figure sits above my 25% downside scenario because a gap of a week or more could also come before a new scheme starts in the base case. Confidence is low: Gazprom cut supplies in January 2025 over a debt that Chisinau disputes, and nothing has been announced for 2027.
4. Annual consumer price inflation for October 2027 is **likely** (~60%) to be below 6.5%, the top of the National Bank of Moldova's (NBM) target range. October's jump came largely from month-on-month rises in gas and heating tariffs that drop out of the annual comparison next autumn, and the NBM expects inflation back in range from the third quarter of 2027. Oil prices and the further tariff rises the NBM assumes for 2027 are the risks.
5. A Russian drone or missile kills someone on territory controlled by Chisinau before 12 November 2027: **very unlikely** (~10%). Incursions are frequent (65 drone incursions in 2026 by late October), but Russia is aiming at Ukraine, and the only casualty I found this year is a border guard injured near the Ukrainian border on 5 October.

## Drivers
### Political
PAS won 50.14% of the vote and 55 seats in September 2025. The Patriotic Electoral Bloc, led by the Party of Socialists, took 24.19%, and three smaller parties passed the 5% threshold. Officials said Russia had spent over $300 million on interference, and more than 2,500 searches targeted a network linked to the fugitive oligarch Ilan Shor. On 11 November anti-corruption prosecutors said they had sent to court a case accusing Shor of illegally financing the small ALDE party with $452,700; he will be tried in absentia.

The majority has been steadier than the cabinet. Munteanu, invested on 1 November 2025, quit after street protests against fiscal and public pay reforms and while the hiring scandals were unfolding. Parliament approved Tofan, a former private equity executive, in late July. He has to carry costly reforms before local elections in 2027, which the government wants held under a new municipal map with no mayoralty of under 3,000 residents. Foreign Minister Mihai Popșoi said in an interview aired on 6 November that any unification with Romania should come from a democratic process backed by a broad majority on both banks of the Prut, and on 12 November the Communists demanded his resignation. I expect the opposition to campaign on the issue in 2027.

Gagauzia elects a new bashkan and People's Assembly on 15 November. The assembly's mandate expired in November 2025, and on 9 July the Constitutional Court removed its right to form the regional election body and to approve the heads of regional police and security units, which the assembly's chairman called a political ruling. Eight candidates are standing for bashkan. Alexandr Stoianoglo, an MP from the Alternativa faction running as an independent, is seen as the favourite; PAS is fielding no one.

### Security
Russia keeps about 1,600 troops in Transnistria. Putin awarded the region's leader, Vadim Krasnoselsky, the Order of Honour at the Kremlin on 4 November. Chisinau answered by barring him from leaving Moldova for five years, which Tofan said means he cannot use Chisinau airport.

The war in Ukraine reaches Moldova through its airspace. A Russian Geran drone fell about 100 metres from a house in Telenești district on 23 October, and Moldova expelled a Russian diplomat. A border guard was wounded in Russian drone strikes near the Palanca crossing on 5 October, and two Russian Banderol missiles crossed Ștefan Vodă district and Transnistria on 11 November. The EU has allocated €317 million to Moldova's defence since 2021, partly for air defence.

### Economic
Consumer prices rose 10.77% in the year to October; in that month alone regulated gas tariffs rose 28.8% and central heating 61.2%. The NBM raised its base rate to 11.00% on 5 November and forecasts average inflation of 7.3% in 2026 and 6.9% in 2027. A 60-day state of emergency in the energy sector began on 26 September, and petrol reached 34.35 lei a litre at the end of that month.

The energy position is stronger than a year ago. Sandu said on 8 November that Moldova "buys zero Russian gas" and announced the launch of the 400 kV Vulcanesti-Chisinau line, which will tie Moldova more closely to the European grid. Transnistria is the weak point: its economy shrank 18% in 2025. Tiraspol turned down the EU's €60 million aid offer in early 2025, saying its conditions, which included moving energy tariffs towards market levels, would hurt local industry; on 10 November Commissioner Marta Kos said the offer still stands. Moldova's IMF programmes expired in October 2025, which leaves the EU's Growth Plan as the main outside anchor for reform.

### Social
A StratCom index published on 4 November put support for EU membership at 57%, but only 8% in Gagauzia and Taraclia, and an IMAS poll in Gagauzia published on 12 November found 12% for and 66% against. Gas tariffs rose going into the heating season while the household compensation plan was still being worked out in September, and the IMF lists high emigration among Moldova's structural weaknesses.

## Indicators to watch
- The Gagauz elections on 15 November (and any runoff): turnout, whether the result is accepted in Chisinau, and whether the new assembly meets.
- A gas supply arrangement for Transnistria from 1 January 2027, and any state of emergency in Tiraspol.
- An intergovernmental conference opening the last two clusters, and whether Hungary lifts its block.
- Monthly CPI against the NBM forecast; a rise above 12% would put the 2027 path in doubt.
- PAS defections, a no-confidence motion, or another minister leaving over a scandal.

## Why my rating differs from the model
My rating matches the model: 44 (Elevated), 102nd of 203, up 0.9 points on last year, with full data coverage. The score has been Elevated in every year of the app's history, which starts in 2015, except 2021 (37, Moderate). Its pillars score stability 38, governance 47, economic 67 and social 12.

The economic pillar drives the score through two red flags for 2026: a fiscal deficit of 5.0% of GDP and a current account deficit of 22.0%. Its inflation figure of 7.4% for 2026 is close to the NBM's 7.3% forecast but hides October's 10.8% annual rate.

The stability pillar misses most of the risk I see. Battle deaths are 0.0 per 100,000 (2024) and the WGI political stability score rose from 61.54 in 2022 to 63.64 in 2025, yet no indicator records Russian troops in Transnistria, the drone and missile incursions of 2026 or the Gagauz dispute, and the app has no watchlist events for Moldova. The social pillar (unemployment of 1.5% in 2025) does not capture emigration. Against that, a single-party majority and the EU's backing are stronger anchors than the governance scores (control of corruption 42.92 in 2025) suggest. The gaps roughly cancel.

## Implications
Companies in Moldova should budget for higher energy costs this winter and for measures under the energy emergency, which runs to 25 November and may be renewed. Firms using the Palanca crossing or sites near the Ukrainian border should plan for disruption during Russian attacks, and those in Transnistria for a January supply disruption. Borrowers face an 11% policy rate that is unlikely to fall before inflation does in 2027. For EU governments, the December decision on the last two clusters will show whether Moldova's target of closing negotiations by early 2028 holds.

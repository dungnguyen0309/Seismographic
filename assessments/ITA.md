## Bottom line up front
Italy's political risk is Moderate and stable over the next 12 months. Giorgia Meloni's government became the longest-serving in the Republic's history in September and survived the loss of its justice referendum in March. The main risk is the 2027 general election, the first under a law that gives a majority bonus only to a coalition with 42% of the vote: YouTrend's poll average puts Meloni's three main parties at 41.4%. A hung parliament followed by a long crisis, or a bond sell-off during the campaign, would move me to Elevated.

## Key judgments
1. A general election is held before 12 November 2027: **likely** (~75%). The parliament elected in September 2022 reaches the end of its five-year term in autumn 2027, the new electoral law has passed, and ANSA reports that the centre-left is planning around a spring vote. The rest covers a full-term vote in late November or December.
2. Meloni is prime minister, with full powers or as caretaker, on 12 November 2027: **likely** (~60%). Brothers of Italy (FdI) leads PD by 27.9% to 20.2% in YouTrend's average of 29 October. She would lose office if the centre-left reached 42% (PD, the Five Star Movement and AVS stand at 38.3% before centrist allies) or if no coalition did and another majority formed.
3. Roberto Vannacci's Futuro Nazionale runs as part of the centre-right coalition at the next election: **unlikely** (~30%). Meloni has kept the door closed and her adviser Giovanbattista Fazzolari has rejected an alliance. Against that, a YouTrend simulation reported by Il Sole 24 Ore found the centre-right would win an absolute majority in both houses only with Futuro Nazionale, which YouTrend's average puts at 6.1%. Confidence is low: pressure to deal will grow if the coalition stays below 42%.
4. The Constitutional Court strikes down the 42% majority bonus before the election: **very unlikely** (~10%). Il Sole 24 Ore reports that the Court could rule on the law as early as January or February. A bonus tied to a fixed threshold is harder to fault than one with no threshold.
5. The EU Council closes Italy's excessive deficit procedure by 12 November 2027: **roughly even chance** (~50%). Closure depends on the 2026 deficit, which Eurostat will report in April 2027. The Economy Ministry targets 2.9%, the IMF puts it at 3%, and 2025 came in at 3.1%. The parliamentary budget office estimates that the 2027 budget adds about €30 billion to the deficit over 2027 to 2029.

## Drivers
### Political
Meloni has governed since October 2022 with Forza Italia and the League. On 4 September, at 1,413 days, her government passed Silvio Berlusconi's second as the longest in the Republic. Its worst defeat came on 22 and 23 March, when 53.7% of voters rejected the constitutional reform that would have separated the careers of judges and prosecutors; turnout in Italy was 58.9%. Meloni said she regretted the result and that the government would carry on.

Parliament has since passed a new electoral law. A coalition that reaches 42% gets a majority bonus; below that, seats are shared proportionally. On the left, Elly Schlein (PD), Giuseppe Conte (M5S) and the AVS leaders agreed at the end of October to finish a joint programme within a month and hold primaries by February. Rome also elects a mayor in 2027.

### Security
After clashes and looting in Milan, the cabinet approved a decree on 20 October creating an offence of violent participation in a demonstration, punishable by up to five years, and sending 500 more troops to Milan until the end of the year. Police powers for soldiers were dropped after objections from the President's office and the Interior Ministry. On 11 November thousands of schools received bomb threats; nothing was found, and schools in the United Kingdom and Ireland got similar messages.

### Economic
Eurostat's October figures put the deficit at 3.1% of GDP in 2025, down from 3.4%, and debt at 136.7%, up from 134.3%. On 3 June the Commission found that Italy had taken effective action and held the procedure in abeyance without closing it. Italy has asked to use the national escape clause for about €36 billion over 2026 to 2028, for defence and for energy after the war in Iran began in February.

The 2027 budget, approved by the cabinet on 28 October, is worth about €26 billion and is paid for partly by a levy of about €5 billion on the financial sector, including banks and insurers. Carlo Messina, chief executive of Intesa Sanpaolo, called some bank measures "clearly unconstitutional". The Bank of Italy told parliament on 12 November that unexpected taxes add uncertainty, and noted that the spread over German bonds has narrowed.

### Social
Unemployment was 6.4% in 2025 and youth unemployment 20.6%. The CGIL has called a general strike for 20 November, its fifth during this government's budget rounds, and wants a 1% levy on net wealth above €2 million.

## Indicators to watch
- The dissolution of parliament and the election date.
- Coalition totals against the 42% line in the YouTrend poll average.
- The Constitutional Court's ruling on the electoral law.
- Any pact between Futuro Nazionale and the centre-right.
- The Eurostat deficit notification in April 2027 and the Commission's spring package.
- The BTP-Bund spread during the campaign, and rating agency decisions.
- Further violent protests and prosecutions under the October decree.

## Implications
Bond investors should treat the 42% line as the main event: a hung parliament would revive questions about fiscal policy while the procedure is open. Banks and insurers face a larger levy, and banks a ten-year deferral of Superbonus tax credits; Messina has said shareholders may go to court. Protest organisers and NGOs should expect tougher policing under the October decree.
